C01 — STARTER INVENTORY AND SURVIVAL / CRAFTING CHAIN

Review topic H03. Refines Q1-Q5, Q21, Q26, Q38, Q66 and Q69.

Normal Origin start: - Appropriate clothing and a small amount of coin; nothing else. - No free knife, weapon, food, waterskin, fire-making gear, map, or compass. - The character starts in Origin's tavern under the existing Normal rules. Naked remote start: - Literally no clothes, money, tools, equipment, food, or navigation aids. - Placement must offer a difficult but viable survival route without staff intervention, with accessible natural drinking water within survivable reach. No fixed distance or automatic portable water container was set. - Other outfitting tiers retain Q21; this review did not define their lists.

Bootstrap cutting tools: Suitable terrain supplies a sharp stone/flint-like material. A sharp stone can cut crudely; suitable natural materials can be fashioned into a crude stone knife without first needing a manufactured tool. The crude knife supports skinning, cutting fiber, sharpening wood, food preparation and primitive crafting. A manufactured knife is a later improvement. Do not create a circular requirement to possess a knife before making any knife.

Primitive materials and weapons: - Local wood, shoots and suitable growths support a primitive spear, bow and arrows. Purchased parts are not inherently necessary. - Suitable vegetation yields plant fiber, which becomes basic cordage. This is universal survival knowledge, with no special CAP or learned recipe prerequisite; biome-specific plants may supply the same material. - A suitable rock can be thrown or wielded: WIELD ROCK; HIT RABBIT. A thrown rock may stun a rabbit, allowing the hunter to close and finish it with hands, a knife, a rock or a spear using normal combat timing. Stun is a possible result, not an automatic success or an authored chance. - A bow or spear is useful but is not mandatory for the first meal. - Primitive arrows progress from sharpened wooden shafts to stone points and then properly fletched arrows. Feathers/stone heads are not required before the crudest bow-and-arrow path can work. - A primitive bow may be improved by replacing its string. Plant fiber can be replaced by improved leather cordage. Eventually a better carved NEW bow and deer innards for a good string are needed; restringing alone does not make an unlimited-quality weapon. Exact bonuses are not set.

Fire: Gather suitable tinder and fuel. LIGHT FIRE can use available proper equipment, primitive equipment, or an abstract bare-material friction method. Suitable combustible material and sufficient effort/time must eventually produce fire: do not make survival depend on repeated random craft failures. Tools, skill, dryness and weather principally affect time and difficulty. Do not invent a six-part friction-fire assembly. Craft-time language describes labor represented by Work Timer debt; it does not supersede the existing immediate atomic Craft architecture. If a later implementation introduces a separately timed non-Craft fire action, its timing must be specified explicitly, not inferred here.

Rabbit processing and first food: 1. Obtain a rock and/or make a primitive hunting weapon; obtain a cutting edge for skinning. Travel to suitable habitat and FLUSH RABBIT. 2. Hunt the ordinary animal through the current turn-based combat rules. Recover its CORPSE after actual death. 3. SKIN RABBIT removes its hide AND guts it. Outputs are hide, gut pile and cleaned whole CARCASS. A small rabbit requires no separate BUTCHER step. The gut pile may supply a small amount of cordage material. 4. Gather a few sticks and make a simple spit. This trivial survival work requires no special skill or elaborate components. Place the cleaned rabbit on the spit and roast over an open fire. 5. The cooked rabbit is one physical object with remaining edible quantity. EAT RABBIT progressively consumes it; multiple people can share it. Exhaustion leaves appropriate remains, not artificial serving objects.

OBSOLETE: requiring BUTCHER RABBIT between skinning and cooking; calling the dead unskinned animal a carcass; requiring a bow before the first hunt. Detailed larger-game butchering remains later work. Proposed burn timers, knife-free destructive butchering, and exact stun odds were not accepted mechanics in this conversation.

Hide and leather: TAN HIDE abstracts the entire tanning process. An animal provides enough brain/material to tan its own hide; do not simulate scraping, soaking, brain preparation, stretching, smoking or drying as separate requirements. The result is a tanned hide available for use under normal Craft execution. Tanned hides yield standardized leather squares, principally by hide size. A rabbit-sized hide can supply a small square; exact yield tables are balance data. Squares can become improved bowstring/cordage or a belt; several squares can make crude leather leggings, shirt and moccasins. Leather recipes consume squares, not bespoke dimensions for every hide.

The first rabbit is not forced into a one-hide waterskin recipe. The new chain explicitly supplies food and usable leather; waterskins remain a later option using appropriate materials. The earlier waterskin objective must not make the first meal depend on an unsupported rabbit-hide recipe.

Water, shelter and navigation: DRINK WATER works at physically accessible natural water without a vessel. A vessel is needed to carry water. Locally appropriate gourds, bark vessels and similar primitive containers are alternatives to leather waterskins. Retain Q69: shelter is useful physical space, not a separate exposure or shelter-deprivation damage meter; sleep is not a survival requirement. Retain the Q5/Q12 tent/map/compass alpha milestone: acquire instructions and physical navigation items, travel at least two miles for the introductory assignment, build/pitch a tent at a suitable chosen place, mark it, leave sight of it, navigate back, and find the same persistent tent. Origin sells maps/compasses; remote crafting of a compass requires minerals (Q26). This review did not supply exact compass minerals, tent bill of materials, food portions, ignition costs or opening-Craft tables. Do not claim these content tables were finalized or grant the missing items free.

C02 — CRAFT TIMER AND FLUSH WILDLIFE

Review topic H05 (wildlife); original audit H04. Refines Q85's deferral for FLUSH animals without inventing a general ecological depletion system.

Work Timer and "craft timer" are the SAME single per-character labor debt: - Craft results happen immediately after all requirements validate. - Consume inputs, create/transform results and add debt atomically. Validation failure changes nothing. There are no exposed halfway stages. - A Craft may start only when current remaining debt is strictly BELOW 48 real-world hours. This is an entry threshold, not a debt cap. - Add its cost to the later of now and the existing work-until timestamp: work_until = max(now, work_until) + craft_duration. - Debt falls with real elapsed time, online or offline; it does not use the accelerated 6x world clock. Sixteen hours at logout becomes six after ten real hours away. - A Craft started below 48 hours may take debt above 48. Existing ledger example: 47 hours + 48 hours of work = 95 hours, result already finished. - This is NOT a per-recipe cooldown or a wait for the product to finish. A session of repeated crafting is intentional, not an exploit to prevent.
FLUSH <animal> is a wilderness/hunting Craft: - It creates a new encounter animal within a specified nearby distance. It does not need a previously persistent individual or a simulated local population. Exact spawn distance remains configuration. - Terrain/biome must support the species. No deer at sea or arbitrary species in unsuitable terrain. Suitable habitat plus available craft capacity is sufficient; no extra depletion/population-density limiter. - Initial illustrative costs: rabbit about 2 real hours of Work Timer, deer about 12. Other species receive appropriate later content values. - From zero debt, 24 successive two-hour rabbit Crafts are intentional. The 24th starts at 46 and ends at 48; another must wait until below 48. - A flushed living animal has a hidden temporary lifetime of roughly 1-3 real hours. It behaves as an ordinary NPC animal after creation. Cleanup of a still-temporary animal is quiet, when it is not actively being observed/interacted with; loss of LOS itself is not despawning. - FLUSH is finished once the animal exists. It does not reacquire an already-flushed animal: use LOOK, SEARCH and TRACK for that individual. - No global permanent wildlife population or anti-farming cooldown is required by this design.

Rabbit behavior: A flushed rabbit hops a few feet and attempts to hide. If concealment does not defeat perception it remains visible and can be attacked again legally. When shot at it may bolt 1-25 yards in a random direction. If it hides successfully, track/reacquire it. These are behavior choices within normal movement/combat rules, not free out-of-turn movement or a stationary target.

Wild-animal bleeding and persistence: - Animals with wound level 3 or greater bleed. In current rules vocabulary, level 3 is DEEP (C5), not the old Q60 "Minor" label. - A moving bleeding animal always produces blood-trail data. Greater bleeding produces a heavier/easier trail and faster progression to death. - Untreated level-3-or-greater bleeding eventually kills wild animals that cannot self-staunch. Rabbits, deer and waterfowl cannot self-staunch. - Some species may have explicit AI/template ability to staunch bleeding by licking or rolling/rubbing. Do not give all animals this ability. - Remove the temporary despawn timer AS SOON AS bleeding is severe enough to cause eventual death without intervention, not merely upon death. The animal must remain to let the hunt resolve naturally. - Actual death produces an animal CORPSE governed by ordinary persistence/ decay; SKIN produces hide + gut pile + CARCASS. - Do not silently restore the removed FLUSH expiry on treatment; that restoration was not specified. Corpse decay intervals also were not set. - This wild-animal rule does not replace W6's exact Scale-1 MORT rule. The assistant's tentative extension of level-3 fatal bleeding to people was never accepted as a new universal PC rule; do not import it. - Trail/state history must not be recreated as a fresh animal merely because a procedural cache is evicted. Preserve meaningful interactions under the existing persistence-by-exception architecture.

C03 — LOS, RECOGNITION, WATCH AND TRAILS

Review H04; original audit H05. Completes the discussed Q73-Q85 behavior.

LOS is physical, not a fixed MUD-room radius. Terrain, vegetation, elevation, lighting, weather and obstructions determine visibility. Distance and conditions determine resolvable detail: movement -> animal/person/figure -> equipment/detail -> normal short-description or observer's private label. Favorable long Origin sightlines are a design goal, not guaranteed recognition of a person at ten miles. Existing illustrative perception bands are not hard maximum visibility distances.

Recognition never reveals the canonical character-file name. Show the normal short-desc, or that observer's existing label when recognition is adequate. Labels attach to remembered identity, survive ordinary clothing/ appearance changes, and remain private. They are not magical tracking handles through darkness, distance or concealment. Preserve Q44/Q78's concealment restrictions; elaborate disguise behavior is still deferred.

WATCH <target>: - Uses the established approximately five-second observation cadence. - Reports an eight-point heading and actual movement state, e.g. walking, jogging, running or sprinting, subject to what the observer can perceive. - Ends when LOS is lost, with a useful final observed direction/disappearance if known. It does not continue through the obstruction, predict unseen movement, or silently become TRACK.

Trail representation: Ordinary tracks and blood use lightweight trail segments recording actual path/location, age and intensity, rather than thousands of blood-drop or footprint inventory objects. Terrain/surface, size/weight, speed and conditions govern ordinary signs. Mud, snow, sand, soil and disturbed vegetation may preserve tracks; rock or hard dry surfaces may not. Time and weather degrade signs. Carried weight AND movement speed increase trail strength. Blood is deposited even where useful footprints do not form, and heavier bleeding makes the trail easier to follow. Evidence existing does not mean every observer automatically detects it.

TRACK with no target: Find detectable nearby trails and list the nearest first, then other significant detectable trails. Supply usable local references such as RABBIT TRAIL #1, DEER TRAIL #2 or HUMAN TRAIL #1. This is the discovery/ reacquisition command; no separate SEARCH TRAIL command is required.

TRACK <trail>, e.g. TRACK RABBIT TRAIL #1: Physically retrace recorded signs through world coordinates at TRUDGE. Continue only while successive segments are resolvable. Stop at the last known sign if the trail becomes unreadable; do not bridge a rock gap by knowing the target's hidden location. Tell the player the trail is lost. The player can reposition and use TRACK to reacquire detectable evidence. Reaching the animal/corpse or trail end, stopping/changing movement, or an actual obstruction/interruption ends automatic following as applicable. Blood can bridge surfaces that do not preserve ordinary tracks.

Tracks may reveal species/type when evidence and ability support it; human footprints do not reveal a name. Connecting a trail to a particular person requires independent knowledge/evidence, such as seeing it made or following it to the person. Keep unknown human trails anonymous. TRACK is primarily SENSE; the earlier illustrative secondary-CAP formula is not finalized. Existing ordinal targeting remains; descriptive trail numbers are local usable references, not leaked database IDs.

QUESTION 3 — REASONS TO RETURN

The town gives a character somewhere to begin. The wider world gives the character somewhere to belong.

QUESTION 4 — SMALLEST SATISFYING CORE LOOP

Claiming land is not part of the smallest core loop.

The smallest complete gameplay loop is:

1. Obtain or improvise a weapon by picking up and throwing a rock or by crafting a rudimentary bow and arrows. 2. Travel into the wilderness. 3. Locate and hunt a rabbit. 4. Claiming land, building a more permanent shelter, making a waterskin, selling the hide, and returning to town expand the experience but are not required to prove the smallest core loop.

QUESTION 15 — FIREARMS

QUESTION 25 — STARTING KNOWLEDGE, TIME, AGELESSNESS, AND DEATH

A newly arrived character has zero knowledge of this world's society: no politics, drama, relationships, intrigue, history, geography, settlements, or current events. MAGIC and Callings are also a surprise.

The physical world is familiar. Ordinary physics and recognizable natural surroundings—earth, trees, sky, clouds, sun, moon, stars, and similar features—behave much as the character expects from home.

Player characters may originate from essentially any place and any historical era. One may come from a 21st-century society while another may come from something resembling the 10th century.

The world's calendar is based on the arrival of its oldest inhabitant more than 700 years ago. The game begins in approximately Year 731.

Inhabitants do not age after arriving in this world and cannot die of old age. They know they are permanently bound to this world.

Physical death does not necessarily end a character. At death the character is magically removed. The player may retire the character or return that character to the world while retaining all memories.

Death and respawn rules established here: - Murder by another player NEVER qualifies for a merciful respawn. Continuing after murder automatically means Naked 10000. - A player/account receives exactly ONE lifetime reprieve for an accidental or environmental death, not one reprieve per character. - Qualifying deaths include events such as falling from a cliff, drowning, or being killed by wildlife such as a bear. - When the player's one-time reprieve is used, the player may respawn the affected character using any starting configuration they wish. - If the reprieve has already been used, later deaths of any of that player's characters lead to Naked 10000 if the character is continued. - If the reprieve has not been used, it remains available until a qualifying accidental/environmental death occurs. - Memories survive respawning. A murdered character can therefore theoretically return for revenge, but Naked 10000 makes returning to the murderer potentially extremely difficult.

These decisions supersede the earlier undecided synthetic-body/resurrection possibilities under Question 14. Additional exact death mechanics may still be refined when the combat/death questions are reached later.

Rare geometric/terrain edge cases, such as multiple structures or displacement toward impassable terrain, may be handled when the implementation is designed.

QUESTION 57 — MOVEMENT SPEEDS

Base movement speeds in game-world terms are:

- TRUDGE: 1 mph. - WALK: 3 mph. - JOG: 5 mph. - RUN: 8 mph. - SPRINT: 11 mph.

The normal world clock runs at 6× real time. Travel receives an additional 4× acceleration, making ideal uninterrupted travel cover 24 times the listed game-world miles per real-world hour.

Theoretical uninterrupted distances per real-world hour are therefore:

- TRUDGE: 24 miles. - WALK: 72 miles. - JOG: 120 miles. - RUN: 192 miles. - SPRINT: 264 miles.

These are theoretical movement rates, not sustainable endurance distances. JOG, RUN, and especially SPRINT are constrained by stamina and cannot ordinarily be sustained for an entire real-world hour.

QUESTION 58 — STAMINA MODEL

Stamina is represented internally as a continuous numeric value with a nominal maximum of 20.0. It may use fractional values, allowing regeneration and expenditure such as 2.675 rather than forcing integer arithmetic.

The player-facing prompt does not expose the precise internal stamina number. Instead it displays six stamina bands using plus signs. At full health and full stamina the prompt is:

<***** / ++++++>

The asterisks represent health/wound condition; stars disappear as wounds worsen. The six plus signs represent stamina condition; plus signs disappear as stamina falls. With an internal 0–20 range, each visible stamina band represents approximately 3.333 stamina:

- greater than 16.667 through 20.000: ++++++ - greater than 13.333 through 16.667: +++++ - greater than 10.000 through 13.333: ++++ - greater than 6.667 through 10.000: +++ - greater than 3.333 through 6.667: ++ - greater than 0 through 3.333: + - 0: no plus signs

Twenty is therefore primarily an internal mathematical maximum; the player-facing stamina display has six coarse condition bands.

Stamina recovery is processed on a 15-real-second heartbeat/tick. Because the world clock runs at 6× real time, each recovery tick represents 90 seconds of game time.

The intended recovery rate scales with ATHLX. The initial reference points discussed were approximately 1 stamina per recovery tick at ATHLX 0 and approximately 3 stamina per recovery tick at ATHLX 20. Because stamina is floating-point internally, intermediate ATHLX values may use smooth fractional recovery rates rather than integer breakpoints. Question 59 locks the exact recovery formula.

Passive stamina regeneration continues even while the character is moving. Movement and other strenuous activity have their own stamina expenditures. Thus:

net stamina change = passive regeneration - activity expenditure

SPRINT should consume stamina much faster than passive regeneration. RUN and JOG should also normally produce net stamina loss at rates to be determined. WALK should eventually produce a net positive stamina change, allowing a tired character to recover while continuing to travel, but more slowly than resting.

Example behavior: a character may sprint for 20 real seconds, lose substantial stamina, and still receive the single passive recovery tick that occurs during those 20 seconds. The character may then slow to WALK and gradually recover from one visible stamina bar back to full stamina without stopping entirely.

The exact stamina costs for TRUDGE, WALK, JOG, RUN, SPRINT, terrain, wounds, encumbrance, and other exertion remain to be designed in subsequent questions.

QUESTION 59 — STAMINA RECOVERY DETAILS

The exact passive stamina recovery formula is:

stamina recovered per 15-real-second tick = 1 + (ATHLX / 10)

Examples:

- ATHLX 0: 1.0 stamina per tick. - ATHLX 5: 1.5 stamina per tick. - ATHLX 10: 2.0 stamina per tick. - ATHLX 15: 2.5 stamina per tick. - ATHLX 20: 3.0 stamina per tick.

Fractional stamina is retained internally. Passive regeneration applies whether the character is stationary or moving; activity expenditure is calculated separately.

QUESTION 60 — MOVEMENT MODIFIERS

Terrain, weather, elevation, wounds, and encumbrance modify actual travel speed rather than changing the passive stamina-recovery formula. These modifiers are multiplicative rather than additive, so several severe conditions can make travel extremely slow without producing a negative speed.

Terrain: - The procedural terrain generator should eventually provide a deterministic Terrain Movement Factor from 1.00 down to 0.50. - The factor should be derived from the physical character of the ground at the character's coordinate, such as slope, ruggedness, vegetation, and ground cover, rather than from arbitrary room-like difficulty classes. - The exact calculation is deliberately deferred until the terrain generator is being developed/refined. - Elevation must not be counted twice: terrain represents how difficult the ground itself is to traverse, while the separate elevation modifier represents the effects of high altitude.
Weather: - Weather may reduce travel speed by as much as 50% under extreme conditions.
Elevation: - Elevation may reduce travel speed by as much as 50% under extreme high-altitude conditions.

Environmental modifiers stack multiplicatively. A 3 mph WALK subjected to maximum terrain, weather, and elevation penalties becomes:

3 × 0.50 × 0.50 × 0.50 = 0.375 mph.

The character is still using WALK and receives/pays the normal WALK stamina behavior; environmental conditions reduce physical progress rather than changing the movement mode's stamina rate.

Wounds — WORLD-TRAVEL HISTORY, NOT COMBAT SPEED: The following old names/derates are retained for the travel record only. C04/C05 and CC C5/T3 govern combat: wounds never reduce tactical Speed, and current wound names are FLSH/LITE/DEEP/SEVR/GRAV/MORT. Do not treat the old names as a second combat wound ladder. - Light: 0% speed derate. - Moderate: 5% speed derate. - Minor: 15% speed derate. - Major: 30% speed derate. - Grave: 50% speed derate. - Mortal: unconscious; no movement.

These wound derates are the final cumulative values for each wound level, not additional percentages to be summed.

Encumbrance — RECONCILED by Q61 and C04: The old thresholds against descriptive body weight are OBSOLETE. Let C be maximum capacity derived from the shared mechanical reference weight and ATHLX. Through 50% of C there is no derate; from 50% to 100% the derate rises linearly to 50%; above C the character cannot move until load is reduced. All recursive carried mass counts. No numeric reference weight was selected.

The overall movement model may therefore be expressed conceptually as:

actual speed = base movement speed × terrain factor × weather factor × elevation factor × wound factor × encumbrance factor

Exact weather and elevation scales, and the terrain-generator formula, remain to be designed later.

QUESTION 61 — MAXIMUM CARRYING CAPACITY

ATHLX substantially increases maximum carrying capacity.

Maximum carrying capacity = standard mechanical reference weight × (1 + 0.05 × ATHLX).

RECONCILED (C04): "body weight" as descriptive/player-selected weight is OBSOLETE for this calculation. All ordinary humans share the same mechanical reference constant; its numerical value remains unset.

Examples: - ATHLX 0: 100% of standard mechanical reference weight. - ATHLX 5: 125%. - ATHLX 10: 150%. - ATHLX 15: 175%. - ATHLX 20: 200%.

Encumbrance speed penalties scale against the character's actual maximum carrying capacity, not against descriptive weight or the unmodified reference constant. A character suffers no encumbrance speed derate through 50% of maximum carrying capacity. From 50% to 100% of maximum capacity, the speed derate rises continuously and linearly from 0% to 50%. At full carrying capacity, speed is reduced by 50%. Above maximum carrying capacity, the character cannot move.

QUESTION 63 — TERRAIN PASSABILITY BY TRAVEL MODE

QUESTION 67 — RELATIVE POSITIONING AND MOVEMENT INTENTS

CCMud should support convenient relative-movement controllers operating on the same continuous coordinates used by ordinary travel. These are tactical movement intents, not teleportation or guaranteed outcomes. Exact syntax and combat integration are provisional and may be substantially revised during combat design.

CLOSE ON <target> <distance> Approach the target directly and stop upon reaching the requested distance. CLOSE never moves the character away merely to create the specified separation. If already within the requested distance, the character does not move.

Example: CLOSE ON JENNY 10

KITE <target> <distance> <preferred direction> Attempt to maintain the specified separation from the target. KITE may require no movement if the desired distance is already maintained and the target is stationary. If the target closes, the character retreats, favoring the specified direction, and automatically increases movement speed as necessary and physically possible to preserve the requested gap. If the target is too far away, the character may close toward the requested distance.

Example: KITE JENNY 10 SE

KITE is not immunity from melee. Terrain, obstacles, stamina, wounds, encumbrance, relative speeds, and the target's actions may make the desired separation impossible to maintain.

ORBIT <target> <distance> <CW/CCW> Continuously move around the target at approximately the requested radius in the player-selected clockwise or counterclockwise direction. Unlike KITE, ORBIT deliberately keeps the character moving even when the target is stationary. The controller adjusts inward/outward and changes speed as necessary and physically possible to maintain the requested radius. ORBIT is defensive positioning rather than substantial travel away from the encounter area.

Example: ORBIT JENNY 10 CW

RETREAT <target> <preferred direction> Continuously attempt to increase separation from the target while favoring the specified direction. There is no desired stopping distance. The controller uses the minimum practical movement speed needed to increase/preserve increasing separation and escalates automatically when pursuit requires it, up to what the character can physically sustain.

Example: RETREAT JENNY SE

All relative-movement commands remain subject to actual world geometry and physics. They optimize only for the explicitly named target; for example, successfully kiting Jenny may inadvertently move the character into Bob's melee reach. This is intentional. In multi-character combat, positioning, terrain, obstacles, headings, speeds, and chosen movement intents may make the battlefield tactically significant.

These ideas remain provisional for experimentation. C05's fully turn-based combat is now authoritative. No continuous controller may generate free out-of-turn movement, evade Speed budgets, or bypass ENGAGED/WITHDRAW. Continuous combat automation implied by this older proposal is OBSOLETE.

QUESTION 69 — PHYSICAL SURVIVAL NEEDS (COMPLETED)

Food/calories and water are genuine physiological requirements, deliberately paced so they matter without becoming maintenance chores.

Hunger is communicated descriptively rather than primarily as a visible numeric meter. Intended progression includes: - peckish - hungry - famished - starving - dying of hunger

Thirst follows an equivalent escalating progression ending in dying of thirst.

While dying of hunger or thirst: - normal wound healing stops; - stamina recovery becomes substantially slower; - one flesh wound becomes due for every full game-world day spent in the dying state while the character is subject to the active survival process; - approximately 32 continuous game-world days in that state would ultimately be fatal through accumulated flesh wounds.

Fully logged-off characters never receive starvation/dehydration wounds. Hunger/thirst state may advance offline, but wounds are not applied offline. On login the game checks whether a wound is due; if so it applies the appropriate current wound and warns the player approximately how many additional days at the present rate are likely to be fatal. A long absence must not dump a large backlog of offline wounds onto the character.

Food and water depletion is intentionally slow. After eating and drinking to full, it should be at least about one real-world week before ordinary hunger becomes noteworthy. Exact calories, hydration, activity modifiers, thresholds, and stamina-recovery reduction remain for later balancing.

Sleep is NOT a survival requirement and has no fatigue/deprivation meter. Sleep does increase natural wound-healing rate and may permit administrator-delivered dreams or visions. This agrees with the current Q54 record. CC O1 supplies the recovery rules and pauses recovery during combat; exact world-clock integration remains as noted in C05.

Shelter is NOT a physiological requirement and lack of shelter causes no damage merely because the character is unsheltered. Shelter is valuable as a physical place: safe/useful storage, hanging or keeping a deer carcass, cooking stew, warming coffee, sleeping, working, getting out of weather, and establishing a useful wilderness home. Weather may affect other systems such as movement, but there is no hidden shelter/exposure-damage meter.

QUESTION 85 — GENERATED LOOK PROSE AND BRIEF MODE

Standard LOOK descriptions use a substantial natural-language prose generator rather than presenting a disguised list of world facts.

The architecture is: physical simulation/world data -> perception filtering -> prose composition.

The world simulation supplies factual information about terrain, slope, vegetation, hydrology, roads, structures, distant geography, weather, illumination, entities, and other physical features. Perception filters those facts according to SENSE, distance, lighting, weather, concealment, and related conditions. The prose generator then composes the perceived facts into a coherent, substantial description of the place.

The prose should understand relationships among facts. A forest following a riverbank, a road descending toward water, mountains dominating a horizon, or prairie opening into long sightlines should be described as related scenery rather than as independent database fields converted into sentences.

Variation in prose must not create factual variation. Repeated LOOKs should not make the player wonder whether the physical scene changed merely because wording changed.

A player preference, BRIEF, provides a concise factual presentation for players who do not want substantial/flowery prose. BRIEF changes presentation only; it does not alter perception or give/remove information the character is entitled to perceive.

WORLD-GENERATION/CACHING ARCHITECTURE DISCUSSED DURING Q85:

LOOK does not generate the world itself. The deterministic world generator defines physical facts; both the graphical map viewer and the MUD's LOOK system are different consumers of that same underlying world.

The intended architecture is hybrid and chunk-based: - seed + deterministic generation algorithms define the reproducible base world; - geographic chunks are generated/evaluated on demand and cached rather than regenerating the same area for every LOOK or every passing player; - active chunks may remain hot in RAM while players are nearby; - unused cached procedural data may eventually be evicted from RAM and may also expire from longer-lived caches because it can be deterministically reconstructed; - persistent world changes never expire merely because a procedural cache does.

Thus fifty players passing the same generated tree should consume the same cached world information rather than independently regenerating that tree fifty times. If a player cuts the tree down, that modification is persistent authoritative state. If the disposable base cache later expires, the server can reconstruct the deterministic base chunk and then apply persistent modifications and current conditions.

Persistent changes include player construction, roads, names where appropriate, stored/cached possessions, harvested or removed features where persistence is required, and other player/world history that cannot be recreated solely from the original seed. C02 now settles FLUSH animals: habitat-eligible temporary creation regulated by per-character Work Timer, without simulated population depletion, with removal of expiry when bleeding will be fatal and ordinary corpse persistence on death. Other resource depletion/regrowth and long-term simulation retain their later-design status.

Exact chunk size, cache lifetimes, storage strategy, and geographic resolution are implementation decisions to benchmark after the physical world generator is substantially complete; they are not locked here.

Reliable duplication of persistent written material is important enough to restrict mechanically even if low-level manual copying might seem technically possible. The exact Academics requirement and copying mechanics remain deferred.

Copies preserve the original author/creator metadata and add the copier and date, such as "copied by Thomas" on the recorded date. Question 180 extends this to the full provenance chain.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 180 — FULL COPY PROVENANCE AND A VISIBLE COPIER NOTE

Copies of copies preserve the complete relevant provenance chain in metadata: the original author/creator, each copier, copying dates, and other relevant copying information. Do not reduce the record to only the original author and most recent copier. Original artwork attribution remains preserved under Question 178.

The copier may also insert a visible metadata tag/note at the beginning of the copied book or paper. This note is part of the readable document, separate from the hidden staff metadata and provenance record.

At the end of Question 180, whether this visible note would be free-form or standardized remained unanswered. Question 181 below now resolves it.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 181 — COPIER’S VISIBLE NOTE

A copy made by anyone other than the original author must contain a standardized, unavoidable visible notation at the beginning indicating that the document is a copy, not an original. That mandatory notation does not display the copier’s identity. The copier may additionally add an optional free-form introductory note. If the original author produces another copy of their own work, it may be treated as another original and requires no visible copy notation. There is no limit imposed here on how many originals an author may produce.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 182 — WHAT COUNTS AS THE “ORIGINAL AUTHOR”?

The no-copy-notation privilege applies only to a single-author work reproduced by that same original author. If a book or document has multiple authors, any reproduction of it is considered a copy, even if one of the original co-authors performs the copying. It receives the mandatory visible copy notation. The metadata still preserves the complete authorship and copying provenance.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 183 — COPY OF A COPY

A copy is made from the actual source document in the copier's possession, not from some abstract pristine master. Copy #2 therefore reproduces Copy #1 exactly, including its mandatory copy notation and any free-form notes previously added by earlier copiers. The current copier may then add their own optional free-form comment. Nothing inherited from the source is silently stripped out.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 184 — WHERE DOES EACH NEW COPIER NOTE GO?

New copier comments are prepended. The newest copier’s optional free-form note appears first, followed by the previously inherited copier notes. Thus, readers see the most recent annotation immediately, while the complete visible history remains intact underneath it.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 185 — CAN A COPIER DECLINE TO ADD A PERSONAL NOTE?

The copier’s free-form comment is entirely optional. A copier may add nothing. The mandatory copy indication and all inherited content remain unchanged.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 186 — CAN THE COPIER EDIT THE BODY WHILE COPYING?

COPY BOOK and COPY PARCHMENT produce an exact copy of the source. The copier cannot alter, correct, delete, rearrange, or add anything within the copied body through the COPY command. The only permitted addition is the optional copier note already established.

If someone wants to create an altered version, they must do so separately—effectively copying/pasting the text into a newly authored document and editing it normally. That new document is not treated as a faithful COPY and does not inherit the COPY command's provenance status.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 187 — COPYING <ART> CONTENT

Authorized COPY BOOK and COPY PARCHMENT reproduce <art>...</art> content exactly, just like ordinary text. No separate artistic skill or capability is required. The copier's high-level copying ability represents sufficient competence to reproduce the entire document faithfully.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 188 — CAN AN EXACT COPY FAIL?

Once a character has the required copying capability and possesses the necessary book/parchment, ink, and pen, the COPY action does not fail. There is no random copying roll.

A qualified character can also translate while copying. For example, if the source is English and the character is capable of the necessary French language use, they may copy it as a French-language document. The underlying player-visible stored prose may still literally be the English text—the game treats it as French through the document's language metadata. This is a translation, rather than an exact same-language transcription.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 189 — TRANSLATION AND COPY STATUS

A translated reproduction is visibly distinguished from an ordinary copy. Its mandatory notation will indicate that it was “copied and translated from” the source language/document rather than merely saying it is a copy. The provenance metadata continues to preserve the relevant source and copying/translation history.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 190 — TRANSLATOR IDENTIFICATION

Translated copies follow the same identity rules as ordinary copies. The mandatory visible notation indicates that the document was copied and translated from another language/source, but does not identify the translator. Full translator identity, date, source, language transition, and other relevant provenance remain in metadata. The translator may add an optional free-form note under the same rules already established.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 191 — TRANSLATING THROUGH INTERMEDIATE LANGUAGES

Translation never cleans up or collapses the document's visible history. A translation made from a previous translation preserves all inherited historical information, including mandatory copy/translation notices and every copier's optional note. The new translation information is prepended, following the newest-first rule. Full provenance also remains in metadata.

So a document could acquire a long and interesting lineage over generations of scribes and translators.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 192 — TRANSLATION REQUIREMENTS

Translation requires the character to read the source language and write the destination language. Speaking does not require a separate translation check because the established language progression ensures speaking ability comes before writing ability in that language.

So English → French requires Read English + Write French. This respects the previously established separate per-language Academics unlocks rather than introducing a new translation skill.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 193 — TRANSLATING YOUR OWN WORK

A sole original author may translate their own work into another language and the resulting document is still considered an original. It receives no mandatory copy or translation notation. As with same-language originals, the author may produce as many original versions as desired.

The metadata should still retain the useful facts about the document and its language, but visibly it is the author's own original work—not a copy.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 194 — AUTHOR REVISING THEIR OWN WORK

There is no editing or revising an existing physical document. Written ink cannot be erased, overwritten through an edit command, or retroactively corrected. If an author makes a mistake or wants a changed version, they must begin again using fresh parchment/book materials and create a new document. The existing document remains exactly as written.

That also means “editing” mentioned back in Question 186 should be understood as creating a new document manually from copied/pasted text, not modifying the existing physical object.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 195 — ABANDONING A MISTAKE MID-DOCUMENT

Books consist of individual parchment pages. A page may be physically torn out from the beginning, middle, or end. The book's displayed page numbers automatically renumber to remain continuous; removing page 7 from a 21-page book leaves a 20-page book numbered 1–20.

Existing parchments can also be assembled into books with ADD PAGE while the character holds both the book and parchment. ADD PAGE with no position appends the parchment to the end. ADD PAGE 3 inserts it as the new page 3 when that position is valid, shifting subsequent pages forward. The parchment becomes a page of that book rather than having its text recreated.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 196 — REMOVING A PAGE INTACT

A removed book page remains an intact parchment object, preserving everything written or drawn on it. It can be read independently, carried, transferred, or inserted into another book. Both removing a page and adding a parchment/page to a book require a higher Academics capability. This restriction is deliberately intended to reduce casual vandalism and abuse.

Protection for institutional/library books is explicitly deferred for later design rather than decided now.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 197 — PROVENANCE WHEN PAGES MOVE BETWEEN BOOKS

DEFERRED in this interview.

Page-level provenance when parchments move between books is deliberately left unresolved for later. We won't let it drag us into designing the document data model right now.

Later Questions 243–245 establish preservation of component-parchment provenance and nested history through publication and whole-book copying. Preserve those later decisions without inventing additional page-movement metadata rules here; remaining provenance visibility/details are deferred under Question 246.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 198 — BOOK CAPACITY

Every book has a hard maximum capacity of 100 parchment pages. There are no different book capacities to manage. This is intentionally generous for unusual cases such as long-running journals, collected notes, or player histories.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 199 — BLANK PAGES

Books may contain blank parchment pages, so players can assemble journals, ledgers, notebooks, etc. in advance and fill them over time. WRITE BOOK 42 opens the writing editor for page 42 of the held/targeted book. The normal permanent-ink rules apply once writing is committed to that page.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 200 — WRITING ON A PARTIALLY USED PAGE

Existing writing can be appended to, but never altered or erased. Each parchment has a hard limit of 75 characters per line and 25 lines total, and parchment is one-sided only—nothing may be written on the back.

For books, authorship is established by TITLE, not by who physically writes each page. The character who first titles the book becomes its original author, period. Other characters may subsequently write on pages without becoming co-authors. Only that original author may change the book's title.

This also substantially narrows our earlier multiple-author discussion: ordinary collaborative writing inside one book does not create multiple authors merely because several people contributed text.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 201 — CHANGING A TITLE

A book's original author may change its title while the work remains unpublished. An unpublished work cannot be copied. Only the original author may PUBLISH it. Publication permanently fixes the work for purposes such as copying; title changes are no longer allowed afterward.

If the original author dies before publishing the manuscript, nobody else can publish it. The unfinished/unpublished manuscript remains exactly what it was—a potentially unique and genuinely rare physical artifact.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 202 — WRITING AFTER PUBLICATION

PUBLISH permanently freezes the physical book. Once published, its title, text, artwork, pages, page order, and composition cannot be modified. Nothing may be appended, no pages may be added, and no pages may be removed. It becomes an immutable finished work suitable for copying.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 203 — WHAT ABOUT PUBLISHED COPIES?

Every legitimate COPY BOOK of a published work automatically inherits published/immutable status. Once the copy is created, its text, artwork, title, pages, page order, inherited historical notes, and copy/translation notation cannot be altered; pages cannot be added or removed. The copier's optional note is added during the copying process, before the resulting copy is finalized.

This preserves the integrity of every work circulating through the formal copying system.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 204 — PHYSICALLY DESTROYING PUBLISHED BOOKS

A published book—original or legitimate copy—is permanent and indestructible. Fire, water, weather, decay, burial, ordinary damage systems, and similar effects cannot damage or destroy it. It cannot be removed through an OOC JUNK-style command or equivalent disposal mechanism. Burial does not cause decomposition. Administrators below level 100 cannot destroy it either. Only the exceptional authority of a level-100 administrator remains capable of removing a published book.

A book is forever.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 205 — PUBLISHING EMPTY OR TRIVIAL BOOKS

There is no minimum-content requirement established for PUBLISH. Every publication generates an automatic entry on the staff news board, allowing staff to review publishing activity. If staff determine that a player is abusing PUBLISH, the player loses publishing privileges for 3 months. Each subsequent publishing-abuse offense doubles the previous ban duration: 3 months → 6 months → 12 months → 24 months, and so forth.

The books already published remain permanent; the punishment restricts the player's ability to create additional published works.

Continuation clarification: the illustrative doubling sequence above was superseded by Thomas's explicit schedule in Question 206: 3 months, 5 months, 1 year, 2 years, 4 years, 8 years, 16 years. Use that later schedule.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 206 — WHAT HAPPENS TO THE ESCALATION OVER TIME?

Publishing-abuse offenses never decay or reset. The escalating ban schedule is 3 months, 5 months, 1 year, 2 years, 4 years, 8 years, 16 years, continuing on that severe escalation principle if somehow necessary. Long periods of good behavior do not erase prior publishing-abuse offenses.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 207 — PUBLISHING WHILE BANNED

A PUBLISH-abuse ban applies to the player/account, not merely the offending character. While banned, none of that player's characters may publish a book, including manuscripts created before the ban. Existing published books remain unaffected.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 208 — PUBLISHING THROUGH ANOTHER PLAYER

No special automated proxy-detection system is needed. If another player publishes abusive material, that publisher assumes the normal PUBLISH-abuse risk and enters the established escalating ban sequence if warranted. If staff discover that the publisher knowingly acted as a proxy specifically to circumvent another player's publishing ban, staff may address that deliberate rule evasion separately. Innocent collaboration isn't automatically treated as ban evasion.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 209 — PUBLISHED PARCHMENTS

Individual parchments may also be PUBLISHED. Published parchments receive the same essential protections as published books: they become immutable and indestructible and may participate in the formal copying system. Publishing a parchment also generates staff-news oversight, and frivolous/abusive parchment publishing subjects the player to the same escalating account-level PUBLISH discipline established for books.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 210 — AUTHORSHIP OF A STANDALONE PARCHMENT

A standalone parchment may be TITLED, just like a book, and follows the same authorship/provenance metadata model. The character who titles it establishes its original authorship under the same rules already established for books. Publication, copying, translation, provenance, and related metadata principles therefore apply consistently to both books and standalone parchments where applicable.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 211 — UNTITLED PARCHMENTS

A parchment does not need to be titled before it can be written on. Players may freely use untitled parchment for notes, letters, directions, drafts, and similar informal writing. TITLE remains the action that establishes formal authorship metadata when the player chooses to title the parchment.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 212 — WHO MAY TITLE AN ALREADY-WRITTEN PARCHMENT?

Any character holding an untitled parchment may TITLE it and thereby become its recognized original author. The game does not protect the first writer's claim to authorship. Metadata still records who actually wrote the existing material, so staff/history can establish what happened, but the system does not undo the consequences of handing an untitled work to someone else.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 213 — UNTITLED BOOKS

The same rule applies to untitled books. A book may contain any amount of writing while remaining untitled. Whoever possesses it and successfully uses TITLE first becomes its recognized original author, regardless of who actually wrote the existing pages. Metadata still preserves the actual writing history.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 214 — REMOVING A TITLE BEFORE PUBLICATION

Once a book or parchment has been titled, it can never return to an untitled state. Before publication, the original author may change the title, but every replacement title must contain at least 3 letters. Once published, the title is permanently frozen along with the rest of the work.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 215 — TITLE LENGTH

Book and parchment titles have a maximum length of 75 characters, while still requiring at least 3 letters. This permits deliberately long titles without allowing unbounded title strings.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 216 — DUPLICATE TITLES

Titles do not need to be unique. Multiple books or parchments may have exactly the same title. The game won't automatically append author names or otherwise disambiguate them. Players who care about distinction can include identifying information themselves—for example, *My Travels West, by Thomas Paige*.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 217 — TITLE LANGUAGE

A title has its own language, determined by the language the author was using when they issued TITLE. It is not a universally readable OOC label. The language system governs whether another character can understand that title.

That also means a book's title language doesn't necessarily have to match every page inside it.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 218 — UNREADABLE TITLES IN INVENTORY

Written foreign-language titles behave the same way as hearing a foreign language under the established language system. A character who lacks the necessary language comprehension does not get the literal title leaked through the interface; they receive the same sort of incomprehensible-language representation the game uses for speech they cannot understand.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 219 — READING VERSUS SPEAKING FOR TITLES

Understanding a written title requires READ capability in that language. Merely being able to speak the language is insufficient.

However, as with the previously established spoken-language recognition rules, a character may be able to recognize the language itself without understanding it. Thus someone might determine, *“This appears to be written in French,”* while remaining unable to read what the French title actually says.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 220 — TITLE RECOGNITION CHECK

Written-language identification uses the same established language-recognition rules as identifying an unfamiliar spoken language. We will not create a separate recognition subsystem just for writing. Reading capability determines comprehension; existing language familiarity/recognition determines whether an unreadable language can nevertheless be identified.

Continuation clarification: this later decision supersedes Question 176's earlier statement that there is no written-language recognition mechanic. Reuse the established language-recognition rules; do not introduce a separate writing subsystem.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 221 — MIXED LANGUAGES WITHIN ONE BOOK

A book or parchment may contain multiple languages. Different pages may use different languages, and separate passages appended to the same parchment may also be written in different languages. Language therefore cannot be treated merely as a single property of the entire book; the system must preserve enough information about each written section to apply the proper reading and language-recognition rules.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 222 — SWITCHING LANGUAGES MID-WRITING

The writing language is fixed for each editor session based on the character's currently selected language when the editor is opened. The writer cannot switch languages from inside the editor. To change languages on the same parchment/page, they must finish/close the current editor session, change their active language, then reopen that page and append the new passage. Each appended section retains its appropriate language metadata.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 223 — PARTIAL EDITOR SESSIONS

The writing editor is a temporary drafting buffer. Nothing becomes physical writing, consumes ink, or changes the parchment until the player commits/saves the editor session. Cancelling or abandoning the editor discards the entire uncommitted session with no effect on the document or supplies.

Once committed, however, the resulting ink is permanent under the rules we've established.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 224 — RUNNING OUT OF INK

The ink check happens before the writing editor opens. A character must possess enough ink to write a full parchment page—up to the established 25 lines × 75 characters—even if they intend to write only a short passage. If they lack that reserve, WRITE is refused with an appropriate message such as “You need more ink.”

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 225 — HOW MUCH INK IS ACTUALLY CONSUMED?

Ink is consumed in discrete samples/uses, not character-by-character or line-by-line. When a writing session is successfully committed, one sampling of ink is consumed regardless of how much text was added during that session.

So the resource cost encourages filling a writing session efficiently, but we don't need to simulate ink down to individual letters.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 226 — APPENDING AND INK

Every successfully committed writing session consumes one ink sampling. Reopening an existing parchment or book page later to append additional writing consumes another sampling when that new writing is committed, regardless of how little text is added.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 227 — INK COLORS

Ink may have different colors, and the color used for each committed writing section is preserved. Because writing can be appended later, a single parchment may contain passages written in different ink colors.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 228 — CUSTOM INK COLORS

Ink colors come from a fixed, game-defined palette. Players cannot create arbitrary color descriptions. This keeps crafting, display, copying, and metadata consistent.

We can decide the actual palette later when we're designing crafting/materials.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 229 — COPYING INK COLORS

COPY reproduces the written content, not the source's ink colors. The copier chooses/uses their own available ink, and the resulting copy records that ink color. Source ink color is therefore not part of the requirement for textual fidelity.

This slightly refines our earlier use of “exact copy”: exact means the words/content—including inherited copy notes and <art> content—not the physical ink characteristics.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 230 — MULTIPLE INK COLORS DURING ONE COPY

To copy an entire book in one operation, the selected ink bottle must contain at least as many ink samplings as there are pages being copied. The completed book uses that single ink color throughout, regardless of the source's colors, consuming the required samplings.

A scribe who wants to preserve different colors must copy pages individually using the desired inks. COPY BOOK 23, for example, copies page 23 of the book onto the parchment the character is holding, using the pen and ink on the desk. The resulting parchment can then participate in the page-assembly system we've already established.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 231 — BLANK PAGES DURING WHOLE-BOOK COPYING

Whole-book copying requires and consumes one ink sampling for every physical page in the source book, including completely blank pages. A 50-page book therefore costs 50 ink samplings, period.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 232 — BLANK PARCHMENT REQUIREMENT FOR WHOLE-BOOK COPYING

Whole-book COPY BOOK requires the copier to have a book containing exactly the same number of blank pages as the source book. A 50-page original requires a 50-page blank destination book. Loose parchments are not automatically assembled or bound by the copying command.

Combined with our ink rule, copying that 50-page book requires the 50-page blank book plus at least 50 samplings of a single ink, along with the required pen and copying capability.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 233 — DESTINATION BOOK MUST BE COMPLETELY VIRGIN?

A destination book used for whole-book copying must be untitled and never previously written in, in addition to containing exactly the required number of blank pages. A previously titled blank book is not eligible, because TITLE has already established its authorship/history.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 234 — DESTINATION BOOK AFTER COPYING

A successful whole-book COPY BOOK automatically reproduces the source book's title on the destination book. The copier does not become the author merely by creating the copy. The copied work retains its established authorship and provenance, while the copy metadata records the copying event and the mandatory visible copy notation applies.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 235 — COPYING A SINGLE PAGE

COPY BOOK 23 produces a parchment that automatically carries the source book's title and source page number. The player does not need to title it manually, and the copied page remains associated with where it came from rather than becoming an anonymous loose parchment.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 236 — REASSEMBLING INDIVIDUALLY COPIED PAGES

Only the one-step whole-book COPY BOOK operation can create an official, published/immutable copy of an existing published book. Individually copying pages and later assembling them with ADD PAGE does not recreate an official copy, even if every page is present, correctly ordered, and textually identical.

So preserving the original's varied ink colors comes with a tradeoff: the reconstructed volume is a player-assembled artifact, not a formally certified copy.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 237 — PUBLISHING THE RECONSTRUCTED BOOK AS A NEW WORK

Yes. A player who individually copies pages and assembles them into a new book may TITLE and PUBLISH that assembled volume as a new work under the normal rules. The game does not enforce intellectual-property ownership or prevent someone from claiming authorship this way. The underlying metadata still preserves the actual history of the pages, so the provenance can reveal what happened.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 238 — COPYING AN INDIVIDUALLY COPIED PAGE

A loose page produced by COPY BOOK 23 may itself be copied using COPY PARCHMENT. Each subsequent copy follows the established rules: content is reproduced faithfully, mandatory copy notation accumulates appropriately, optional copier notes are preserved and may be added, and the complete provenance chain remains in metadata.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 239 — COPYING UNPUBLISHED LOOSE PAGES

COPY PARCHMENT requires the specific parchment being copied to have published status. Having content derived from some other published source does not bypass that requirement. Unpublished parchments cannot be formally copied.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 240 — PUBLISHING INDIVIDUAL PAGES FROM AN UNPUBLISHED BOOK

A parchment removed intact from an unpublished book may become an independent work and be TITLED and PUBLISHED under the normal parchment rules. Its former membership in an unpublished book does not prevent publication. Once published, that parchment gains the same permanence, immutability, copyability, and anti-abuse oversight as any other published parchment.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 241 — ADDING A PUBLISHED PARCHMENT TO AN UNPUBLISHED BOOK

A published parchment may be inserted into an unpublished book with ADD PAGE. The parchment retains its own published/immutable status; putting it into an unpublished volume does not make it editable. The surrounding book remains unpublished and may otherwise continue to be assembled or written under the normal rules.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 242 — REMOVING THAT PUBLISHED PAGE AGAIN

A published parchment inserted into an unpublished book may later be removed intact. Adding or removing it changes the unpublished book's composition, not the published parchment itself. The parchment retains its title, content, provenance, publication status, and indestructibility throughout.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 243 — PUBLISHING A BOOK CONTAINING ALREADY-PUBLISHED PARCHMENTS

An unpublished book may contain already-published parchments and may itself subsequently be PUBLISHED. Publication of the containing book is allowed because each parchment retains its own provenance metadata—where it came from, its history, and, where known, who actually wrote it. The resulting book becomes a permanent published work without erasing the independent history of its component parchments.

This also reinforces that provenance belongs deeply enough in the document/page structure to survive assembly into other works.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 244 — REMOVING INDEPENDENTLY PUBLISHED PAGES AFTER THE BOOK IS PUBLISHED

Once the containing book is PUBLISHED, its composition is permanently frozen. Even an independently published parchment that was previously removable becomes permanently bound into that published book and cannot thereafter be removed. Its own provenance remains intact, but its physical membership in the book is now permanent.

IndexTop ↑

QUESTION 245 — COPIES OF BOOKS CONTAINING INDEPENDENTLY PUBLISHED PAGES

Whole-book copying preserves the full nested provenance of component parchments. If a page originated as an independently published work, that history remains traceable through subsequent books and generations of copies. Copying a containing book never flattens or erases the histories of the parchments within it.

IndexTop ↑

THE BEST PLACE TO BEGIN

When we begin, I recommend we start with Question 1 and proceed as an interview, but not blindly through all 208. The first twelve questions define the playable target. Once those are settled, we can identify the shortest development path and postpone whole categories that do not belong in the initial release.

My provisional recommendation is that the first real milestone prove this loop:

Create a character → enter the starting region → travel continuously → perceive generated terrain → find and gather a resource → leave persistent evidence → craft or build one useful object → log out → return and find the world correctly preserved.

If that works, CCMud’s distinctive foundation works. Combat, economies, governments, advanced construction, and large-scale civilization can then grow upon something real rather than delaying the first playable game.
