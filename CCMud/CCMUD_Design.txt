September 28, 2026 - Water Movement v1 accepted and implemented rules


The server's existing TravelController now owns LAND, WADING, SWIMMING and FLOATING.
This supersedes ordinary <=3-foot water walking; certification is geometry, not
permission. HOG geography, the 25-foot depth cap, deterministic currents, ground Z,
0.1-second integration and configurable 15-second observations remain unchanged.

| Mode | Precise legality | Geographic movement | Stamina |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LAND | Certified dry terrain | Existing selected pace, terrain and slope | Existing rules |
| WADING | Represented water, depth <4 ft | Existing pace; no current displacement | Existing ordinary movement rules |
| SWIMMING | Represented water, depth >1 ft | 1 mph effort plus authoritative current vector | Existing JOG recovery/drain |
| FLOATING | Any represented water depth | Current only above 1 ft; zero displacement at <=1 ft | TRUDGE recovery component; no movement drain |

The deliberate 1-to-4-foot overlap preserves the chosen mode. Exactly 1 foot
cannot be swum; exactly 4 feet cannot be waded. No feature label, minimum width,
minimum duration, crossing-success heuristic or artificial loop prevention changes
these rules. A narrow deep strip and repeated encounters with the same river are valid.
Selected land pace persists through water; swimming uses its specified effort speed.
Existing runtime travel-time conversion is retained; the separate future clock redesign
is not implemented here.

Commands are server-side: `WADE <direction>`, `WADE`, `SWIM <direction>`, `FLOAT`,
`STAND`, `STOP`, `SET`, `SET AUTOWADE`, `SET AUTOSWIM`, `STATUS`, and `ENTER <water feature>`.
WADE without a direction and STAND enter WADING without initiating movement when
local depth permits. SWIM in shallow water explains an actual WADE command.
FLOAT works even in an inch of represented water, but fails on dry land.
STOP while swimming enters FLOATING; STOP while floating does not cancel current.
Swimming exhaustion also enters FLOATING, visibly. Restart requires one integration
interval of net JOG stamina. Floating recovery is the existing TRUDGE gross recovery
component (90% of standing at current rules), without TRUDGE's movement drain.

AUTOWADE and AUTOSWIM are persisted per-character booleans, both initially OFF.
Plain SET lists them; each SET name toggles it. AUTOSWIM never implies AUTOWADE.
With AUTOWADE OFF, ordinary travel stops before the bank; manual WADE authorizes
entry. AUTOWADE ON continues LAND -> WADING. At 4 feet, AUTOSWIM ON with sufficient
stamina continues WADING -> SWIMMING; otherwise stop before invalid water.
At <=1 foot SWIMMING becomes WADING: continue with AUTOWADE ON, otherwise stop and
explain WADE. With both enabled, direction survives LAND -> WADE -> SWIM -> WADE -> LAND.
Manual wading with AUTOWADE OFF stops on exiting to land and needs a new direction.
Automatic settings never select FLOATING.

**Disconnect acts like STOP**, as explicitly approved: swimming becomes persisted
FLOATING. No offline drift is simulated. Elapsed offline time applies normal resting
recovery, capped at maximum; rapid reconnect cannot instantly restore stamina.
Reconnect resumes floating/current behavior. Persisted swimming recovered after an
unclean disconnect also becomes FLOATING. Offline characters receive no recovery ticks.

ENTER resolves a nearby represented channel by kind or stable feature identity,
reports ambiguity rather than guessing, and uses ordinary checked movement to the
first representable wadeable point just inside its bank. It stops there in WADING;
it neither teleports to the center nor crosses the whole feature. Unsupported water
nouns do not create geometry. Admin JUMP uses endpoint certification and explicit
wading legality (<4 feet), preserving the existing placement-only scope.

Ordered Waterway Geometry events intercept banks and 1/4-foot contours inside each
movement interval, including exact four-foot touches and narrow bands. Full shapes
are gathered from a bounded certified chunk corridor and deduplicated by feature,
preserving repeated crossings and seam continuity. No new intersection mathematics
or water sampling grid is introduced. Independent cliffs, structures, unsupported
water/terrain and incomplete chunks remain blocked. Safety interception can halt drift;
STOP itself is not an anchor. A new valid movement/FLOAT command retries after a safety stop.
Dangerous non-water consequences remain gated.

Normal prose floors depths 1-6 feet; below 1 is shallow water, >=7 is deep water.
Current is described by natural compass and deterministic qualitative bands (below
0.25/1/2/3/4 mph: barely moving/slowly/steadily/briskly/swiftly; otherwise strongly).
Exact values remain internal/RAW. STATUS identifies mode, effort direction, depth,
current and resulting geographic movement. LOOK adds reliable local deeper-water
orientation for a single unambiguous nearby channel; ambiguous bank directions are
omitted. Depth/current prose is change-driven on the existing observation cadence,
not a full LOOK every integration step; mode changes echo immediately.
The web client renders server text and contains no water rules. Server command tests
exercise behavior independently of browser rendering; no new Telnet transport is added.

No drowning, knockdown, current-induced wading displacement/hazard, skill/failure roll,
panic, unconsciousness, temperature, wet clothing, armor/encumbrance swimming penalty,
buoyancy, rapids/waterfall consequence, rescue, boat, tide, wave, undertow, surf,
automatic compensation, intelligent crossing or hydraulic simulation is introduced.
Lakes/coasts/oceans still need compatible certified geometry; absolute water-surface
and riverbed Z are not invented. No game deployment accompanies this implementation.

WORLD TIME, TRAVEL CONVENIENCE AND DAYLIGHT — THOMAS'S 2026-09-27 DECISIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SETTLED DESIGN NOT YET IMPLEMENTED. These decisions supersede conflicting older
world-time, travel-acceleration and daylight values in this preserved record.
They do not claim that a new world calendar or dynamic controller is running.
The inspected application at e54acc982c1b68450b85e9c428fd0e82daa64d9c uses a
combined 24 factor for travel; it has no complete general world-calendar service.

World time is fixed at 1.75 times real time, independently of travel convenience.
One real hour is 1 hour 45 minutes game time. One game day takes approximately
13h42m51s real time. Simple integer multipliers tend to align a player's repeated
real-world login schedule with the same game clock time. At 1.75x, same-real-time
daily login advances game time by 18 hours modulo 24 (six hours earlier on the
clock); this repeats every four real days. It reduces alignment, not all periodicity.
Implementation needs an explicit epoch/continuity decision and a time-consumer audit.

Normal travel convenience is 2.0x, with future dynamic range 0.25x through 2.0x.
At normal convenience, 1.75 x 2 = 3.5x effective geographic rate, compared with
the current conceptual approximately 24x. Under comparable pace/terrain/coverage,
steady virgin-terrain distance demand is 14.58% of current demand (85.42% less),
giving HOG approximately 6.86x more preparation time per traveled distance.
This is a rate calculation, not an 85.42% reduction in all server CPU work.

Only geographic travel convenience responds to HOG exploration pressure. World
time, SAY, combat, inventory and ordinary non-travel gameplay must not slow with
HOG pressure. Selected pace remains the player's choice. Existing stamina uses
real elapsed time and selected pace; do not silently change it with convenience.
Distinguish base pace mph, miles per game hour and miles per real hour in telemetry.

The future controller should use unmet urgent demand, oldest urgent-job age,
measured service latency, worker availability, contiguous prepared runway,
recent preparation-frontier stops and retry/failure state. Player count or generic
CPU percentage alone is not the control signal. A known geography refusal must
not be mistaken for global worker saturation. Use hysteresis and slower recovery
after healthy runway is sustained. Numerical controller tuning in the investigation
is a proposal requiring telemetry/tests, not settled thresholds or deployed behavior.

Gameplay-oriented seasonal daylight targets in GAME hours:
- Summer solstice: 75% daylight, 18 hours.
- Fall equinox: 70%, 16h48m.
- Winter solstice: 65%, 15h36m.
- Spring equinox: 70%, 16h48m.
Transition daylight duration smoothly between these anchors, without abrupt
seasonal jumps. Future environment/perception work may include dawn, dusk,
lunar phases, moonlight, starlight/ambient night illumination, and weather/cloud
suppression of night illumination. Night remains meaningful while ordinarily
clear conditions generally allow minimal navigation. This is a gameplay design,
not a claim of an implemented astronomical model or universally visible geography.

The authoritative engineering conclusions and safety limits from the completed
Step 10 investigation live in CCMud/HOG.md (published at /CCMud/hog.html).

TERRAIN MOVEMENT AND OFFLINE REST — THOMAS'S 2026-09-26 DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------
This continuation preserves the continuous-travel rules below. For on-foot
travel it supersedes Q60's unresolved terrain formula and Q63's old rule that
every factor below 0.50 is impassable. Horse/wagon rules are not redesigned.

Selected pace never changes automatically. Actual ground distance uses selected
pace times one effective terrain factor times one meaningful directional slope
factor, alongside the already established character/condition inputs. Do not
multiply overlapping surface classifications (a maintained road through brush
uses the road factor). Terrain and slope affect distance, not stamina rates.

Initial CONFIGURABLE terrain factors:
- Road / maintained trail: 1.00.
- Open grassland / ordinary firm ground: 1.00.
- Light woodland / brush: 0.85.
- Heavy woodland: 0.65.
- Rocky / rough terrain: 0.70.
- Marsh / shallow water: 0.50.
- Very difficult but traversable terrain: 0.35.

Initial CONFIGURABLE uphill factors, with grade measured as rise/run percent:
- 0 through 5%: 1.00.
- Greater than 5 through 10%: 0.90.
- Greater than 10 through 20%: 0.75.
- Greater than 20 through 30%: 0.55.
- Greater than 30%: 0.35, subject to hazard classification.
Ordinary downhill uses 1.00, never progressively larger speed bonuses. Steep
downhill must be represented as difficult or hazardous geometry as appropriate;
this phase does not invent the physical thresholds for those classifications.
Traversability is explicit and is not inferred from a factor cutoff. Shallow-water
speed does not authorize entry into dangerous water. Existing hazard gates apply.
Minor variation stays unobtrusive; safe transitions do not stop or change pace.

The provisional 25-foot sample interval is not boundary position precision.
Significant cliff lips, shores, river/creek banks, roads/trails, vegetation edges
and other movement transitions retain sufficiently precise independent geometry.
Every traversed segment is checked so a boundary between samples cannot be skipped.
Fuzzy gradual natural transitions may remain approximate. Exact physical geometry
must come from the local-geometry milestone, not invented precision in regional
interpolation. Persistent constructed roads remain game-owned overlays.

Stamina recovers offline at the normal standing/resting rate, capped at maximum.
Store an accounting timestamp and enough state to apply actual offline elapsed
time on reconnect; do not tick disconnected characters. Consuming recovery and
advancing the timestamp must be atomic so rapid reconnects cannot replay time or
instantly refill stamina. Reconnect never moves the character or resumes travel.
Future conditions may modify/prevent recovery; do not invent those systems now.
The implementation snapshots the normal resting rate at the last accounting point,
uses that rate for the offline interval, then refreshes it from current game rules.
Old rows without a timestamp establish a baseline without guessing elapsed time.

CONTINUOUS TRAVEL AND STAMINA — THOMAS'S 2026-09-26 DECISIONS
----------------------------------------------------------
This section supersedes conflicting earlier movement/stamina wording, including
the unresolved reference weight, 15-second recovery heartbeat and deferred pace
drain rates in C04 and Q57-Q61. Existing compatible movement rules remain in force.

Mechanical reference weight R is 150 pounds. Capacity is
150 * (1 + 0.05 * ATHLX) pounds. The existing encumbrance curve is unchanged:
no speed reduction through half capacity, linear reduction to half speed at
capacity, and no voluntary movement above capacity. Terrain and encumbrance
affect speed, not pace-dependent stamina rates. Other established multiplicative
speed factors remain; their unresolved physical calculations are not invented.

New characters select WALK. Bare direction commands start or redirect travel
at the previously selected pace. Pace persists across logout/reconnect. A pace
command changes pace immediately while moving, preserving direction; while
stopped it only selects the pace and never begins movement. Travel never changes
pace automatically. STOP halts non-combat travel; the encounter STOP rules remain
separate. Disconnect stops travel at the current position; reconnect never resumes.

Maximum stamina remains 20. Standing recovery is continuously accrued at
(1 + ATHLX/10) / 15 stamina per REAL second. Replace the old 15-second recovery
heartbeat with elapsed-time accounting. Stamina rates depend on selected pace and
real time, regardless of reduced progress through difficult terrain or encumbrance.

Recovery as a fraction of the standing rate:
SPRINT 0%; RUN 10%; JOG 30%; WALK 70%; TRUDGE 90%.
WALK drains exactly its recovery: net stamina change zero.
TRUDGE drains half of WALK's drain (35% of standing recovery), yielding net
recovery of 55% of the standing rate, eventually reaching full stamina.

Full-stamina endurance, INCLUDING all recovery, is:
SPRINT: 5 + ATHLX real seconds.
RUN: 2 * (5 + ATHLX) real seconds.
JOG: 4 * (5 + ATHLX) real seconds.
Thus for each of these paces, gross drain per second equals that pace's recovery
per second plus 20 divided by its endurance. The quarter-mile and 10-second
SPRINT proposals were replaced and are not retained rules.

Starting or resuming travel requires at least 20% of full stamina (4 of 20).
Once traveling, movement may continue below 20% until zero. Direction and pace
changes while active are allowed below 20%; they are not restarts. At zero,
all voluntary movement stops, including WALK and TRUDGE. Selected pace remains
unchanged. Recovery never resumes movement automatically; a new direction command
is required after reaching the start threshold.

Supply structured travel/environment observations every configurable 15 real
seconds of uninterrupted travel. This timer is independent of path safety checks
and stamina accounting. Check every traversed segment against precise significant
geometry, not just sampled endpoints. Stop immediately before unconfirmed hazards
and report the geographic threat. Minor ordinary terrain variation does not stop
travel. Observations need not repeat identical facts; no final prose engine yet.

Direction! confirms only the currently warned transition. Revalidate origin,
direction, generation and precise boundary. HOG describes the geographic event;
game handlers determine falling, wounds, swimming, drowning, current/stamina
consequences. Without a handler the crossing stays blocked. Confirmation never
authorizes a later hazard and does not teleport the character across the boundary.

The repository did not previously implement continuous travel: its legacy command
path advances 100 feet per command. The new controller implements these established
rules through the existing coordinate/service/connection architecture. Walking
geometry, game-rule input adapters and consequence consumers remain activation
gates; selecting arbitrary environmental or wound formulas is not authorized.

HOG WORLD ARCHITECTURE — NINE INDEPENDENT ZONES (2026-09-25)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The world is organized as a 3×3 arrangement of nine enormous zones:

    NW | N | NE
    ---+---+---
     W | C | E
    ---+---+---
    SW | S | SE

Each zone uses the same deterministic Heart of Gold (H.O.G.) generator with
its own seed and configuration. The Center zone contains coordinate origin (0,0) and is
the only zone currently being developed and visually reviewed. The other
eight zones may be generated later with distinct seeds/configurations.

Each zone is a square 10,000 × 10,000 miles. The 3×3 world spans approximately
−15,000 to +15,000 miles on each axis, with Center occupying −5,000 to +5,000
miles on both axes and coordinate (0,0) at the square's center. The viewer draws
the Center-zone boundary, opens new previews at 1,000 miles per screen inch
centered on coordinate (0,0), and offers Fit Zone + Continent to show the full boundary.
It reports any coastline beyond the zone for deliberate future review. These
world-level dimensions do not silently reshape the approved coastline.
CCMUD stores world coordinates in inches. For example, (22,000, 42,000)
inches is about 0.35 miles east and 0.66 miles north of (0,0); land is selected
from the chosen zone's geography.

C01 — STARTER INVENTORY AND SURVIVAL / CRAFTING CHAIN
---------------------------------------------------
Review topic H03. Refines Q1-Q5, Q21, Q26, Q38, Q66 and Q69.

Normal Origin start:
- Appropriate clothing and a small amount of coin; nothing else.
- No free knife, weapon, food, waterskin, fire-making gear, map, or compass.
- The character starts in Origin's tavern under the existing Normal rules.
Naked remote start:
- Literally no clothes, money, tools, equipment, food, or navigation aids.
- Placement must offer a difficult but viable survival route without staff
  intervention, with accessible natural drinking water within survivable
  reach. No fixed distance or automatic portable water container was set.
- Other outfitting tiers retain Q21; this review did not define their lists.

Bootstrap cutting tools:
Suitable terrain supplies a sharp stone/flint-like material. A sharp stone
can cut crudely; suitable natural materials can be fashioned into a crude
stone knife without first needing a manufactured tool. The crude knife
supports skinning, cutting fiber, sharpening wood, food preparation and
primitive crafting. A manufactured knife is a later improvement. Do not
create a circular requirement to possess a knife before making any knife.

Primitive materials and weapons:
- Local wood, shoots and suitable growths support a primitive spear, bow
  and arrows. Purchased parts are not inherently necessary.
- Suitable vegetation yields plant fiber, which becomes basic cordage.
  This is universal survival knowledge, with no special CAP or learned
  recipe prerequisite; biome-specific plants may supply the same material.
- A suitable rock can be thrown or wielded: WIELD ROCK; HIT RABBIT.
  A thrown rock may stun a rabbit, allowing the hunter to close and finish
  it with hands, a knife, a rock or a spear using normal combat timing.
  Stun is a possible result, not an automatic success or an authored chance.
- A bow or spear is useful but is not mandatory for the first meal.
- Primitive arrows progress from sharpened wooden shafts to stone points
  and then properly fletched arrows. Feathers/stone heads are not required
  before the crudest bow-and-arrow path can work.
- A primitive bow may be improved by replacing its string. Plant fiber
  can be replaced by improved leather cordage. Eventually a better carved
  NEW bow and deer innards for a good string are needed; restringing alone
  does not make an unlimited-quality weapon. Exact bonuses are not set.

Fire:
Gather suitable tinder and fuel. LIGHT FIRE can use available proper
equipment, primitive equipment, or an abstract bare-material friction
method. Suitable combustible material and sufficient effort/time must
eventually produce fire: do not make survival depend on repeated random
craft failures. Tools, skill, dryness and weather principally affect time
and difficulty. Do not invent a six-part friction-fire assembly.
Craft-time language describes labor represented by Work Timer debt; it
does not supersede the existing immediate atomic Craft architecture.
If a later implementation introduces a separately timed non-Craft fire
action, its timing must be specified explicitly, not inferred here.

Rabbit processing and first food:
1. Obtain a rock and/or make a primitive hunting weapon; obtain a cutting
   edge for skinning. Travel to suitable habitat and FLUSH RABBIT.
2. Hunt the ordinary animal through the current turn-based combat rules.
   Recover its CORPSE after actual death.
3. SKIN RABBIT removes its hide AND guts it. Outputs are hide, gut pile and
   cleaned whole CARCASS. A small rabbit requires no separate BUTCHER step.
   The gut pile may supply a small amount of cordage material.
4. Gather a few sticks and make a simple spit. This trivial survival work
   requires no special skill or elaborate components. Place the cleaned
   rabbit on the spit and roast over an open fire.
5. The cooked rabbit is one physical object with remaining edible quantity.
   EAT RABBIT progressively consumes it; multiple people can share it.
   Exhaustion leaves appropriate remains, not artificial serving objects.

OBSOLETE: requiring BUTCHER RABBIT between skinning and cooking; calling
the dead unskinned animal a carcass; requiring a bow before the first hunt.
Detailed larger-game butchering remains later work. Proposed burn timers,
knife-free destructive butchering, and exact stun odds were not accepted
mechanics in this conversation.

Hide and leather:
TAN HIDE abstracts the entire tanning process. An animal provides enough
brain/material to tan its own hide; do not simulate scraping, soaking,
brain preparation, stretching, smoking or drying as separate requirements.
The result is a tanned hide available for use under normal Craft execution.
Tanned hides yield standardized leather squares, principally by hide size.
A rabbit-sized hide can supply a small square; exact yield tables are
balance data. Squares can become improved bowstring/cordage or a belt;
several squares can make crude leather leggings, shirt and moccasins.
Leather recipes consume squares, not bespoke dimensions for every hide.

The first rabbit is not forced into a one-hide waterskin recipe. The new
chain explicitly supplies food and usable leather; waterskins remain a
later option using appropriate materials. The earlier waterskin objective
must not make the first meal depend on an unsupported rabbit-hide recipe.

C02 — CRAFT TIMER AND FLUSH WILDLIFE
-----------------------------------
Review topic H05 (wildlife); original audit H04. Refines Q85's deferral for
FLUSH animals without inventing a general ecological depletion system.

Work Timer and "craft timer" are the SAME single per-character labor debt:
- Craft results happen immediately after all requirements validate.
- Consume inputs, create/transform results and add debt atomically.
  Validation failure changes nothing. There are no exposed halfway stages.
- A Craft may start only when current remaining debt is strictly BELOW
  48 real-world hours. This is an entry threshold, not a debt cap.
- Add its cost to the later of now and the existing work-until timestamp:
  work_until = max(now, work_until) + craft_duration.
- Debt falls with real elapsed time, online or offline; it does not use
  the accelerated 6x world clock. Sixteen hours at logout becomes six after
  ten real hours away.
- A Craft started below 48 hours may take debt above 48. Existing ledger
  example: 47 hours + 48 hours of work = 95 hours, result already finished.
- This is NOT a per-recipe cooldown or a wait for the product to finish.
  A session of repeated crafting is intentional, not an exploit to prevent.

FLUSH <animal> is a wilderness/hunting Craft:
- It creates a new encounter animal within a specified nearby distance.
  It does not need a previously persistent individual or a simulated local
  population. Exact spawn distance remains configuration.
- Terrain/biome must support the species. No deer at sea or arbitrary
  species in unsuitable terrain. Suitable habitat plus available craft
  capacity is sufficient; no extra depletion/population-density limiter.
- Initial illustrative costs: rabbit about 2 real hours of Work Timer,
  deer about 12. Other species receive appropriate later content values.
- From zero debt, 24 successive two-hour rabbit Crafts are intentional.
  The 24th starts at 46 and ends at 48; another must wait until below 48.
- A flushed living animal has a hidden temporary lifetime of roughly
  1-3 real hours. It behaves as an ordinary NPC animal after creation.
  Cleanup of a still-temporary animal is quiet, when it is not actively
  being observed/interacted with; loss of LOS itself is not despawning.
- FLUSH is finished once the animal exists. It does not reacquire an
  already-flushed animal: use LOOK, SEARCH and TRACK for that individual.
- No global permanent wildlife population or anti-farming cooldown is
  required by this design.

Rabbit behavior:
A flushed rabbit hops a few feet and attempts to hide. If concealment does
not defeat perception it remains visible and can be attacked again legally.
When shot at it may bolt 1-25 yards in a random direction. If it hides
successfully, track/reacquire it. These are behavior choices within normal
movement/combat rules, not free out-of-turn movement or a stationary target.

Wild-animal bleeding and persistence:
- Animals with wound level 3 or greater bleed. In current rules vocabulary,
  level 3 is DEEP (C5), not the old Q60 "Minor" label.
- A moving bleeding animal always produces blood-trail data. Greater
  bleeding produces a heavier/easier trail and faster progression to death.
- Untreated level-3-or-greater bleeding eventually kills wild animals that
  cannot self-staunch. Rabbits, deer and waterfowl cannot self-staunch.
- Some species may have explicit AI/template ability to staunch bleeding
  by licking or rolling/rubbing. Do not give all animals this ability.
- Remove the temporary despawn timer AS SOON AS bleeding is severe enough
  to cause eventual death without intervention, not merely upon death.
  The animal must remain to let the hunt resolve naturally.
- Actual death produces an animal CORPSE governed by ordinary persistence/
  decay; SKIN produces hide + gut pile + CARCASS.
- Do not silently restore the removed FLUSH expiry on treatment; that
  restoration was not specified. Corpse decay intervals also were not set.
- This wild-animal rule does not replace W6's exact Scale-1 MORT rule.
  The assistant's tentative extension of level-3 fatal bleeding to people
  was never accepted as a new universal PC rule; do not import it.
- Trail/state history must not be recreated as a fresh animal merely
  because a procedural cache is evicted. Preserve meaningful interactions
  under the existing persistence-by-exception architecture.

C03 — LOS, RECOGNITION, WATCH AND TRAILS
--------------------------------------
Review H04; original audit H05. Completes the discussed Q73-Q85 behavior.

LOS is physical, not a fixed MUD-room radius. Terrain, vegetation, elevation,
lighting, weather and obstructions determine visibility. Distance and
conditions determine resolvable detail: movement -> animal/person/figure ->
equipment/detail -> normal short-description or observer's private label.
Favorable long Origin sightlines are a design goal, not guaranteed
recognition of a person at ten miles. Existing illustrative perception
bands are not hard maximum visibility distances.

Recognition never reveals the canonical character-file name. Show the
normal short-desc, or that observer's existing label when recognition is
adequate. Labels attach to remembered identity, survive ordinary clothing/
appearance changes, and remain private. They are not magical tracking
handles through darkness, distance or concealment. Preserve Q44/Q78's
concealment restrictions; elaborate disguise behavior is still deferred.

WATCH <target>:
- Uses the established approximately five-second observation cadence.
- Reports an eight-point heading and actual movement state, e.g. walking,
  jogging, running or sprinting, subject to what the observer can perceive.
- Ends when LOS is lost, with a useful final observed direction/disappearance
  if known. It does not continue through the obstruction, predict unseen
  movement, or silently become TRACK.

Trail representation:
Ordinary tracks and blood use lightweight trail segments recording actual
path/location, age and intensity, rather than thousands of blood-drop or
footprint inventory objects. Terrain/surface, size/weight, speed and
conditions govern ordinary signs. Mud, snow, sand, soil and disturbed
vegetation may preserve tracks; rock or hard dry surfaces may not.
Time and weather degrade signs. Carried weight AND movement speed increase
trail strength. Blood is deposited even where useful footprints do not
form, and heavier bleeding makes the trail easier to follow.
Evidence existing does not mean every observer automatically detects it.

TRACK with no target:
Find detectable nearby trails and list the nearest first, then other
significant detectable trails. Supply usable local references such as
RABBIT TRAIL #1, DEER TRAIL #2 or HUMAN TRAIL #1. This is the discovery/
reacquisition command; no separate SEARCH TRAIL command is required.

TRACK <trail>, e.g. TRACK RABBIT TRAIL #1:
Physically retrace recorded signs through world coordinates at TRUDGE.
Continue only while successive segments are resolvable. Stop at the last
known sign if the trail becomes unreadable; do not bridge a rock gap by
knowing the target's hidden location. Tell the player the trail is lost.
The player can reposition and use TRACK to reacquire detectable evidence.
Reaching the animal/corpse or trail end, stopping/changing movement, or an
actual obstruction/interruption ends automatic following as applicable.
Blood can bridge surfaces that do not preserve ordinary tracks.

Tracks may reveal species/type when evidence and ability support it;
human footprints do not reveal a name. Connecting a trail to a particular
person requires independent knowledge/evidence, such as seeing it made or
following it to the person. Keep unknown human trails anonymous.
TRACK is primarily SENSE; the earlier illustrative secondary-CAP formula
is not finalized. Existing ordinal targeting remains; descriptive trail
numbers are local usable references, not leaked database IDs.

Command-context clarification: STOP during an encounter invokes C06's
anonymous encounter proposal. A movement stop is not consent to surrender
and must not silently remove encounter membership. Outside an encounter,
the established travel/tracking stop meaning remains. Do not use old
movement-stop prose to bypass encounter timing.

C04 — MOVEMENT, REFERENCE WEIGHT AND ENCUMBRANCE
-----------------------------------------------
Review H02. Preserves Q45 and Q57-Q63 with the accepted reconciliation.

Descriptive height, weight and build remain cosmetic. All ordinary humans
use ONE standard mechanical reference weight R for carrying calculations,
independent of descriptive prose. Thomas selected R = 150 pounds on 2026-09-26.
The assistant's older 180-pound example is NOT an adopted constant.

Maximum carrying capacity C = R * (1 + 0.05 * ATHLX).
ATHLX 0/5/10/15/20 gives 100/125/150/175/200 percent of R.
Use the established effective capability/condition rules where applicable;
do not introduce a new Strength stat or a second wound deduction.
Every carried container and every recursively contained item contributes
weight. Nesting never removes mass. General bulk/volume remains unmodeled.

With load L and load fraction f = L/C:
- f <= 0.5: encumbrance speed factor 1.
- 0.5 < f <= 1: factor 1.5 - f (linear).
- f = 1: factor 0.5.
- f > 1: cannot move until load is reduced.
This is a movement limit, not the rejected proposal that one simply cannot
pick up anything beyond capacity. No new higher lift/drag formula or
named run/jog lockout thresholds were settled.

World travel retains TRUDGE/WALK/JOG/RUN/SPRINT = 1/3/5/8/11 mph, Q57's
6x world clock and additional 4x travel acceleration, and the existing
multiplicative environmental/load modifiers. Uninterrupted ideal WALK is
72 miles per real hour; CCMud.html's older 4-mph/96-mile example is obsolete.
Standing recovery remains equivalent to 1 + ATHLX/10 per 15 real seconds,
now accrued continuously. The 2026-09-26 section above establishes pace recovery,
drain, endurance and start/stop thresholds and supersedes the earlier deferral.

Combat/world scope:
Use CC T3/C5 for tactical combat Speed: species Base Speed + FULL ATHLETICS,
rounded up to 5 feet; typical human Base Speed 30 feet. Wound penalties
reduce affected CAP rolls, not combat Speed. Q57's travel multipliers and
Q60's wound travel derates are not extra tactical movement allowances or
combat Speed penalties. Q60's old labels remain historical travel entries,
not alternative combat wound ranks.
CC T3 has no tabletop encumbrance math; the accepted CCMUD carrying system
above is an explicit world-system adaptation. Its exact interaction with
tactical movement rounding/Speed still needs implementation reconciliation;
do not quietly invent a new combat stacking/rounding rule. The accepted
above-capacity inability to move remains a CCMUD carrying constraint.
Continuous world coordinates remain authoritative; T3's grid/diagonal rules
are reproduced below as the combat source, not silently replaced by a
new continuous-distance metric.

C05 — PRESERVED CROWN & CALL COMBAT FOUNDATION (SEE OCTOBER 2 OVERRIDE)
--------------------------------------------
Review H01. Combat is fully TURN-BASED.

OBSOLETE — DO NOT IMPLEMENT:
CCMud.html section 13's simultaneous declarations followed by server
resolution, declaration windows/revision/reveal procedure, and waiting for
a round boundary before a legally arriving attacker participates.
An automatic repeat-attack MUD loop was expressly rejected. A server still
validates/resolves each legal action, but does not batch declarations into
a competing combat model.

Turns and initiative (T1-T7):
- A round represents up to seven seconds of activity and ends after every
  character and relevant NPC completes a turn. Human decision time may be
  several real minutes; it is not extra fictional travel/activity time.
- Initial initiative: 1d20, 11-20 PCs first, 1-10 NPCs first. Each side
  chooses internal order. Do not invent a new initiative stat.
- MOVE up to Speed; PREP is a brief activity of at most about one second;
  ACT is the main undertaking. They may interweave, with legal movement
  before/during/after ACT. Related actions, such as two weapon attacks,
  can share ACT when reasonably possible.
- HOLD delays a turn. Re-enter at a chosen position; that initiative
  position persists until someone HOLDs again. NPCs use the same rules.
- Forgo ACT for a second MOVE (T4); this does not bypass ENGAGED/WITHDRAW.
- T3 source: five-foot squares; adjacency includes the eight neighboring
  squares including diagonals. Speed/5 is the square allowance; at most
  half those squares, rounded down, can be diagonal; diagonals cost one
  square. Ordinary melee reach is adjacent unless the weapon says otherwise.
  Implementation of this on the continuous world must preserve its intent;
  no alternative diagonal/measurement rule was selected by the review.
- PROTECT is declared during PREP on one's turn, either for a named person
  generally or against a named enemy. Reserve leftover Speed. It lasts
  until the hit is taken or the next turn begins, whichever is first.
  Interpose against the first qualifying melee attack that can be reached;
  open protection is mandatory when possible, named-enemy protection
  triggers only on that enemy. Reserved movement must reach a square
  adjacent to both protected person and attacker, through a legal path,
  where the protector can legally be that attack's target. Resolve normal
  defense. At most once per turn; multiple protectors use declaration order.
  Remain where interposition ended, successfully or not. Being adjacent to
  a willing enemy there means ENGAGED; no free departure afterward.

Aggression and engagement (W1, W1a, W1b):
- Aggression ranges -10 to +10; 0 is neutral. It adds to melee offense
  and subtracts from melee defense, including unarmed/dodging defense.
- Nonzero Aggression requires adjacency within five feet to an enemy in
  the fight. Otherwise it is 0. Set freely at turn start if already adjacent,
  or in PREP upon becoming adjacent before striking.
- Ranged offense and defense never use Aggression. Ranged attacks require
  at least ten feet; inside ten feet use melee or legally withdraw.
- ENGAGED means adjacent to an enemy in the fight willing to oppose you.
  Encounter membership alone is not ENGAGED.
- WITHDRAW consumes the entire ACT to break that adjacency. No strike that
  turn; MOVE out only on success. Roll separately against every adjacent
  enemy opposing withdrawal: d20 + WEAPONS or ATHLETICS, opposed. An enemy
  may allow departure without rolling. Win EVERY contested roll to take a
  legal path out; losing any leaves you in place. No free/opportunity hit.
- Choosing Aggression -10 before withdrawing is optional, not the cost.
- Q67's provisional KITE/ORBIT/RETREAT controllers cannot bypass the turn,
  Speed budget, engagement or withdrawal rules.

Attacks, defenses and wounds (R1-R4, C5, W2-W10):
Opposed resolution uses die + appropriate CAP + applicable modifiers on
each side; higher total wins, difference is Margin of Victory (MoV).
Defense is an appropriate weapon/shield/ATHLETICS dodge or other plausible
defense, not static armor class. C07's unconscious Defense 0 is an explicit
CCMUD exception. No extra numerical multiple-opponent/flanking penalty:
each attack is a separate opposed resolution, and multiple attacks are
the disadvantage. Bare hands/feet are innate weapons using normal WEAPN
resolution with a low ceiling; no separate unarmed subsystem or invented
numeric ceiling was finalized. CAPTURE is a distinct desired mechanic,
not a new generic GRAPPLE subsystem with invented throws/chokes/escapes.

Raw wound: MoV 1-4 FLSH; 5-8 LITE; 9-12 DEEP; 13-16 SEVR;
17-20 GRAV; 21+ MORT. September 30 adoption replaces weapon ceilings with
current W4 Severity Modifiers (+0 through +3); Hunting Knife has SM +0.
Apply weapon SM, then W5 armor. Armor changes severity, not the chance to hit: none +1, leather 0,
chainmail -1, plate -2. No tier exists above MORT.
Two equal wounds combine into one next tier through GRAV -> MORT;
MORT boxes do not combine into a higher rank. W8 value in FLSH units:
1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. Healing subtracts stated value and rewrites the remainder
as the fewest wounds (e.g. SEVR minus LITE = DEEP + LITE).
C5 penalty uses worst wound LEVEL, not the binary wound value:
FLSH 1, LITE 2, DEEP 3, SEVR 4, GRAV 5, MORT 6. Reduce WEAPONS,
RANGED, ATHLETICS, THIEVERY and SENSE; not MAGIC, ACADEMICS or FAITH.
Speed uses full ATHLETICS. For a creature able to remain standing with
multiple MORTs, each MORT after the first adds one to wound level.
Scale is how many MORTs defeat a creature; it is not bonus CAP or added
wound steps. Scale 1 people drop at one MORT, with W6's subsequent-wound
death rule. Larger creatures follow W7/W9, not automatic human collapse.

September 30 bounded rabbit SKIN adoption:
- SKIN CORPSE requires an accessible rabbit corpse (own hand or ground) and a
  cutting tool in a hand; Hunting Knife qualifies and remains in the same hand.
- Remove embedded items first. Atomically consume the corpse and create three
  persistent objects at the character's feet: rabbit carcass, raw rabbit pelt,
  rabbit guts. No inventory insertion or automatic hand juggling.
- Ordinary perception, reach, ground/ROOM placement and object handling apply.
  Retries/concurrency cannot process one corpse twice; restart preserves outputs.
- This bounded implementation adds no Craft/work timer, tanning, butchering,
  spit-making, fire, cooking, EAT, decay or broader resource system. The cleaned
  carcass is reserved for later whole roasting. CC_Objects section 17 governs.

September 30 bounded rabbit wound/death adoption:
- Rabbit MOB severity modifier +2 applies AFTER weapon SM and armor. Clamp to
  the existing wound ladder; no wound exceeds MORT. This supersedes the proposed
  DEEP death threshold. MOB fragility changes severity, not hit probability or HP.
- Persist survivor wounds, combine by W8, and apply C5 to subsequent ATHLX dodge.
- For this bounded rabbit increment only, MORT is immediate death, including
  combined wounds. Ordinary Scale-1 character W6 remains unchanged. No bleeding
  simulation is introduced. Other species require their own reviewed profiles.
- Atomically remove the active MOB, create one generic corpse object populated

- Ordinary object perception, approach, hands, portability and GET/DROP apply.
  REMOVE recovers from a visible ground corpse or one's own held corpse. The

  cooking, food, AI or encounter expansion.

Ranged equipment (W11):
Thrown attacks use RANGED, the thrown item's attack modifier and SM, and -1 per
ten feet to target. Launched attacks use projectile attack modifier/SM and
launcher effective range, range penalty, allowed ammunition and hand rule.
A bow used as a club uses its melee line, not its arrow's statistics.
Loading sling, shortbow, longbow, light crossbow and blowgun is PREP;

W11's launcher table lists Sling fire = 1 hand, bows/light crossbow/blowgun

firing is illegal except a spanned heavy crossbow. That source discrepancy
for the sling is not resolved by this conversation; do not silently choose

Relevant source examples (do not treat primitive-crafted upgrades as these

Conditions, healing and dice:

C06 — PRESERVED ENCOUNTER BEHAVIOR (SEE OCTOBER 2 OVERRIDE)
---------------------------------------------------------
Review H01. These are CCMUD additions to the combat foundation.

Turn timeout / link-dead:
A player has only the normal response time. Expiry forfeits that turn:
no MOVE, PREP or ACT, and initiative advances. No invented automatic attack
or defensive ACT substitutes for it. The exact duration was not selected.
A connection failure never pauses the fight or grants a reconnect window.
On link-death the server may immediately lower Aggression to -10; this is
the accepted timing exception to ordinary turn-time setting. W1's adjacency
and melee-only restrictions still apply; it grants no ranged-defense bonus.
Otherwise normal combat continues. If the character dies, they die.
This policy is intentional for now and may be made more forgiving later.
Preserve the separate Q47-Q51 aggression/logout timer; that timer is NOT
the -10..+10 combat Aggression setting. Do not invent a reset-action list
or claim all logout/encounter-clock edge cases were specified.

Late joining and zones:
Encounters are spatial and open; targets cannot refuse an otherwise legal
attack because they did not volunteer to join. A legally arriving character
may enter immediately when a valid melee or ranged attack is in range.
The entry attack establishes their initiative position and subsequent turn
timing; no waiting until the next round. "Legally" includes actual position,
range, LOS and the established approach/time-dilation constraints. It does
not authorize impossible travel during other players' decision time.
Nearby participants can be watching, fleeing, maneuvering or waiting; being
in an encounter never requires attacking.
The established pseudo-time-dilation/combat-zone approach system also
applies while leaving. There is no newly invented instant LEAVE COMBAT
command, distance cutoff, or unrestricted travel switch after WITHDRAW.
Causal travel constraints cannot simply vanish at encounter end to allow
distant reinforcements to arrive early. The detailed envelope algorithm
remains later work as in Q6/CCMud.html, not a new required alpha simulator.

Overlapping combat AREAS merge into one encounter with one turn/time
structure. They NEVER automatically split afterward, even if people
separate. The encounter ends by all-party agreement under STOP/DENY.
Thomas explicitly called this a sticky issue subject to later reconsideration;
retain it as current authority, not as an invitation to implement splitting.
Do not silently add geographic auto-removal as another termination route.

STOP/DENY, the final accepted procedure:
- Any participant may propose STOP at any time, not only on their turn.
- The whole encounter is prompted to STOP or DENY. The proposal and all
  responses are anonymous; reveal neither proposer nor dissenter.
- Everyone participates, including the proposer, who may also DENY.
- One explicit DENY immediately fails the proposal; combat continues.
- Unanimous STOP ends it. The normal response period also bounds waiting:
  nonresponse defaults to STOP; without DENY the encounter ends at expiry.
- At most ONE proposal per encounter per combat round. A denied proposal
  cannot be repeated that round. A round may take several real minutes.
- NPCs vote too, using personality/AI settings. Rabbits always favor STOP;
  a killer NPC or configured grizzly may DENY and fight to the death.
  NPC votes are equally anonymous.
- Roleplaying negotiation is encouraged but is not required to use STOP.
- STOP is a proposal, not a persistent personal consent flag or a surrender.
  It does not bind, disarm, capture, release, or make anyone invulnerable.
- An encounter ended by consent may be followed by a new legal engagement.
  No response default of DENY, named notifications, automatic revocation
  of a persistent STOP flag, or auto-splitting survives the final correction.

C07 — SURRENDER, SUBDUE, PRISONERS, MORTAL AND UNCONSCIOUS
--------------------------------------------------------
Review H01/H06. Keep death, incapacitation and surrender distinct.

SURRENDER <target>, e.g. SURRENDER TIMOTHY:
- Target must be adjacent. Voluntary surrender is immediate and cannot be
  refused. It costs/interferes with none of the recipient's MOVE/PREP/ACT.
- Drop everything currently held/in hand, not all worn/carried inventory.
- Surrenderer is controlled, taken prone and bound automatically without
  an opposed CAPTURE roll. Cordage is abstractly supplied; no inventory
  requirement. Do not impose an equipment prerequisite.
- Prisoner is helpless and a NONCOMBATANT encounter participant, not gone
  from the encounter. They may see and speak: no automatic gag/blindfold.
- They can still be attacked/killed; there is no protective immunity.
- Binding is indefinite until ANOTHER character releases them. No self-
  escape roll, cutting loose, wriggling out or expiry. Generic O4 ENTANGLED
  escape does not apply to this special state.
- No release while the encounter remains active. After it ends, ANYONE
  may release them; it need not be the original surrender recipient.
- SURRENDER does not itself end the encounter or trigger a STOP proposal.

After the encounter:
Anyone may assist a captured party to their feet. Captors may force bound
prisoners to follow physically, including multiple prisoners at once (six
PCs following one captor was the accepted example). No one-prisoner limit
was adopted. Standing/walking under forced-follow does not release bindings.
Prisoners remain bound until released; subsequent treatment is ordinary
world interaction, not a new special combat subsystem. Do not invent
commands, consent checks, escape timers or new custody transfer restrictions.

MORT is not immediate death (W6; ordinary Scale 1):
Immediately fall unconscious and prone, drop held items, stop voluntary
combat actions and begin bleeding out. Worn/carried inventory is not all
dropped until actual PC death. The body remains physically present while
alive; "no PC corpse" does not remove an unconscious living person.
Secretly roll the dice currently in play (O3); their total is the number
of seconds until bleed-out. Keep the number hidden. Do not replace it with
a fixed number of rounds, a generic animal bleed curve, or the player turn
response timer. CC allows GM fiction-based adjudication instead of a secret
roll when appropriate; automatic server criteria for that are not invented.
Combat represents fictional seconds; human decision time is not extra
fictional time. Exact within-round scheduling of that second-based deadline
must preserve W6 and T1; no new rounding or minimum survival grace is set.

Anyone at the body may spend the ENTIRE ACT with TWO FREE HANDS to stem
the bleed; no CAP check or special skill is required. In time, this stops
bleed-out but leaves the victim unconscious and MORT, not healed. Any later
wound while MORT kills them. Untreated expiry kills them.
"Binding the wound" here is medical stabilization, distinct from prisoner
bindings. Neither SURRENDER nor SUBDUE automatically stabilizes MORT;
medical stabilization does not automatically capture or revive.

General UNCONSCIOUS:
It is a game-wide condition, not exclusive to Mortal wounds: exhaustion,
poison, environmental or other effects may cause it. The character is prone
and cannot voluntarily act while unconscious. They may be attacked with
Defense automatically 0. They are not automatically captured.
There is no universal duration. The cause supplies an appropriate recovery
window with randomness in the actual wake-up time; do not expose the exact
wake time to the player. This does not override MORT's continuing state.
Do not add a sleep-deprivation meter; Q54/Q69's no-required-sleep rule stands.

REVIVE <target>:
Wake an adjacent unconscious character with no supplies, skill or CAP roll
provided the target does NOT have a MORT wound. It bypasses natural waking
delay, allowing drinking/aid/ordinary action subject to remaining injuries
and conditions. It is not wound healing or blanket removal of the cause.
A stabilized but still-MORT patient is still ineligible. Do not mistake
absence of active bleeding for absence of MORT.

SUBDUE <target>:
Automatically succeeds on an adjacent unconscious character, with no roll
or equipment, and creates exactly the same indefinite bound-prisoner state
as SURRENDER. It does not wake, heal or stop bleeding; waking later does not
release bindings. SURRENDER is voluntary conscious capture; SUBDUE captures
someone already unconscious. The earlier broad question about carrying,
searching and taking all possessions without opposed rolls was not itself
a separately accepted universal rule; apply actual existing object rules.
Adversarial CAPTURE remains desired, but no new comprehensive opposed
capture/escape subsystem was supplied by this review.

C08 — PC DEATH AFTERMATH: CONFIRMED, NOT REDESIGNED

Actual PC death:

  Naked 10000 consequence, or petition for administrative review. A death

- "-10,000 penalty" in the conversation refers to the established Naked
  10000 return, not a newly invented deduction of XP, money or CAPs.
- Banked funds are not carried death loot. After 90 IN-GAME days they pass

  The bank schedule is separate from registered-property succession.

Preserve Q25's once-per-account accidental/environmental reprieve and

C09 — REGISTERED PROPERTY INHERITANCE: CONFIRMED
------------------------------------------------
Review H08. Q154-Q160 already settle the core mechanism.

Registered property has numbered doors controlled by the owner. The
property/door identity-permission system replaces physical door keys.
Owners can grant/revoke named access per door and apply door-specific bans.
Ownership transfer does not depend on handing over physical keys.
The conversation describes abandoning keys broadly; the detailed existing

An owner may designate multiple heirs in an ordered list. At death the first

heirs remain eligible. There is no formal refusal: doing nothing lets time
widen eligibility. A valid claim transfers registered ownership and all

After the final heir has had 30 in-game days with no claim, begin a further
30-day eviction notice. At expiry remove old access, relocate stored offline
login positions immediately outside the main entrance, and forfeit contents
left inside. Offer the property for sale at its front door; the first buyer
who pays the required price becomes owner. No remote Land Office trip.

Retain Q157-Q159: initial price 2 * structure base value; daily in-game
decline compounded at 1% of current price; floor 10% of INITIAL ASKING PRICE,

This confirms rather than redesigns inheritance. Empty-heir-list timing,

C10 — PRESERVED FOUNDATION NEEDED TO CONTINUE WITHOUT THE OLD CHAT

These are established CCMud.html decisions, not new review inventions.

World and persistence:
Use signed 64-bit integer X/Y/Z coordinates at one inch per unit; +X east,
+Y north, Z elevation. Spaces and physical boundaries control perception
and traversal; buildings occupy real geometry and entering is not teleporting
to an unrelated room. Seed/coordinates define reproducible base geography.
Instantiate meaningful changes/history rather than every untouched tree.
Caches are disposable; persistent changes are not. Wake what can interact;
query distant perceptible landmarks without waking all distant simulation.
Calculate long-term changes from elapsed timestamps when needed.

Perception and communication:
Generate prose from world facts after perception filtering; BRIEF changes
presentation only. Seeing a light is distinct from seeing by its illumination.
Movement updates and five-second observation notices are separate concepts.
Speech, whispers, shouts and visible emotes obey distance/boundaries and

Craft/learning:
Tools/materials must be held/carried or physically within the defined work

Display prerequisites, duration and completion time before commitment.
Learning and Work Timer can run independently. The optional recipe/plan
layer beneath broader Crafts remains unfinalized.

Advancement:
Eight CAPs; current names WEAPN/RANGE/ATHLX/THIEV/SENSE/MAGIC/ACADX/FAITH.
Newbie allocation is Q34-Q36: 15 points across exactly five CAPs, 1-5 each.
Individual maximum 20. CAP Boost Tokens use real time plus meaningful play;
idle connection and raw command spam are not advancement currency.
Tokens can be banked; an increase costs its new level. At total CAP 100
(provisional normal ceiling), remaining tokens become zero and automatic
earning permanently ends for that character. Warn before that purchase.
Exceptional logged admin awards (typically 1-3 tokens) may continue and
allow total CAP above 100, but never an individual CAP above 20. Exact
earning cadence/content tables remain tuning. Crafts do not directly raise
CAPs. Retain the numbered account and anti-cycling rules.

PRESERVED NUMBERED DECISIONS
===========================

QUESTION 1 — EXPERIENCE THE FIRST PLAYABLE RELEASE MUST PROVE
-------------------------------------------------------------

The first playable release must demonstrate coordinate travel, exploration, gathering, construction, roleplay, combat/hunting, light crafting, and persistence through a compact frontier-survival experience.

A player must be able to:

- Create a character and begin in the starting town.
- Travel continuously through the coordinate world.
- Explore and perceive generated terrain.
- Gather basic natural materials.
- Perform light crafting.
- Obtain or improvise a hunting weapon.
- Throw a rock as a rudimentary weapon.
- Carve a rudimentary bow and arrows.
- Locate and hunt a rabbit.
- Recover and skin the rabbit.
- Skin/gut, spit-roast, and progressively eat the rabbit (C01); no separate rabbit BUTCHER step.
- Gather fuel and light a fire.
- Construct and set up a simple shelter or tent.
- Tan the rabbit hide into usable leather/squares; retain waterskins as a later option using appropriate materials (C01).
- TAN HIDE abstracts the complete tanning process using the animal's sufficient own materials; no separate tanning chemistry or preparation simulation (C01).
- Return to town.
- Sell appropriate hunted goods.
- Purchase and drink a beer.
- Log out and later find relevant character and world state preserved.

This vertical slice is intended to exercise the defining CCMud architecture: coordinate movement, generated terrain, physical objects, perception, wildlife, ranged attacks, carcass processing, deterministic crafting, fire, cooking, consumption, NPC commerce, and persistence.


QUESTION 2 — FIRST-HOUR EXPERIENCE
-----------------------------------

Characters choosing the standard Normal start begin in Origin's tavern with Normal outfitting and a minimal amount of coin. They may be offered a choice of very simple introductory jobs instead of being forced through one scripted tutorial. Characters choosing a remote start under Question 21 do not begin in town.

Possible first jobs include:

- Delivering a message or parcel to an NPC across the street.
- Hunting and returning with a rabbit.
- Gathering a small amount of basic material.
- Performing another short, easily understood task.

The delivery job teaches movement, targeting, NPC interaction, inventory, and payment with minimal danger. The rabbit job introduces wilderness travel, perception, hunting, carcass processing, and potentially cooking.

During the first hour, a new player should be able to complete at least one job, earn a few coins, understand the basic interface, and possibly purchase a first beer.


QUESTION 3 — REASONS TO RETURN
-------------------------------

Early jobs gradually lead characters away from dependence upon the starting town and toward finding their own place in the world.

Return hooks may include:

- Learning Fishing or another useful Craft.
- An NPC offering a follow-up task.
- Follow-up work that sends the character progressively farther beyond town.
- Hunting animals and selling meat, hides, or other useful materials.
- Learning that land outside town may eventually be claimed.
- Prospecting and striking a resource claim.
- Establishing a campsite and later building a cabin.
- Developing a ranch, farm, workshop, wilderness homestead, or place within town.
- Discovering more of the world and deciding where the character belongs.

NPC follow-up appointments use relaxed in-world timing. An NPC might say:

“Come back in a day or three, when you’ve got time. I’ll be here waiting.”

The player may return four real hours later, the following real-world day, or later still. These invitations are not precise real-world appointments:

- There is no penalty for returning late.
- Ordinary absence does not fail the job.
- The NPC remains available until the player chooses to continue.
- The language remains compatible with CCMud’s 6× world-time clock.

The town gives a character somewhere to begin. The wider world gives the character somewhere to belong.


QUESTION 4 — SMALLEST SATISFYING CORE LOOP
-------------------------------------------

Claiming land is not part of the smallest core loop.

The smallest complete gameplay loop is:

1. Obtain or improvise a weapon by picking up and throwing a rock or by crafting a rudimentary bow and arrows.
2. Travel into the wilderness.
3. Locate and hunt a rabbit.
4. Recover the rabbit's corpse.
5. SKIN it, which also guts it, producing hide, gut pile and cleaned carcass.
6. Gather fuel, light a fire, and make a simple spit from sticks.
7. Roast the cleaned rabbit on the spit (C01).
8. Eat it.

Claiming land, building a more permanent shelter, making a waterskin, selling the hide, and returning to town expand the experience but are not required to prove the smallest core loop.

The following package is the absolute minimum. Nothing in this list should be removed from the private-alpha vertical slice:

- Coordinate movement and generated terrain.
- LOOK and basic perception.
- Inventory, holding, dropping, and object targeting.
- Gathering rocks, sticks, wood, and other basic materials.
- Throwing rocks.
- Crafting a rudimentary bow and arrows.
- Rabbits that can be found, move, be attacked, and die.
- Animal corpses with SKIN/gut transformation into hide, gut pile and cleaned carcass; small rabbits need no separate BUTCHER step (C01).
- Fire building and lighting.
- Cooking raw rabbit into roasted rabbit.

QUESTION 8 — INITIAL PLAYER CAPACITY
-------------------------------------

The private-alpha system should be designed and tested for approximately 10–20 simultaneous players.

A huge population is not expected. A small, committed community may nevertheless contain daily players. Architecture and data modeling should avoid assumptions that permanently limit expansion. Later support for hundreds of simultaneous players should be possible through optimization and scaling rather than fundamental redesign.


QUESTION 9 — MEANING OF “UP AND RUNNING”
-----------------------------------------

The private alpha progresses through three stages:

1. Development validation
   The minimum package works on the development server, survives restart and reconnect testing, and supports the complete rabbit-survival and tent/map/compass loops without administrator intervention.

2. Thomas-only production play
   The tested build is deployed to production, but no outside testers are invited. Thomas plays normally for an open-ended period to evaluate persistence, usability, pacing, reliability, and whether the game is genuinely enjoyable.

3. Invitation-only private alpha
   Once Thomas is satisfied with private production play, he may create accounts for selected testers.

There is no predetermined duration for Thomas-only production testing. Inviting testers requires Thomas’s explicit decision.


QUESTION 10 — UNRESTRICTED TRAVEL AND GRADUATED DANGER
------------------------------------------------------

Travel is unrestricted from the beginning. Testers may travel anywhere the generated world permits.

Early testers may establish permanent homesteads and retain them through private alpha, final release, and beyond. Test play is intended to become part of the world’s real history rather than disposable pre-release data.

Danger increases gradually with distance from the starting settlement named Origin:

- Town to one mile: Extremely safe. Numerous guards patrol and respond to threats. Safety comes from an actual in-world presence rather than an invisible protected-zone rule.
- One to five miles: Generally safe countryside without constant guard coverage.
- Five to twenty-five miles: Wilderness danger increases gradually with distance.
- Beyond twenty-five miles: Fully wild and potentially dangerous territory.

Hostile wildlife, creature strength, encounter frequency, and environmental hazards may increase as civilization recedes. There is no invisible wall. A determined new character may travel directly into serious danger.


QUESTION 11 — RESTRICTED-REGION EXPLANATION
-------------------------------------------

Skipped as inapplicable.

The original Question 11 asked what in-world explanation should prevent travel beyond an early restricted region. Question 10 established that travel is unrestricted, so no such explanation or restriction is needed.


QUESTION 12 — DEFINING NON-ROOM-BASED DEMONSTRATION
----------------------------------------------------

The introductory tent, map, and compass assignment should demonstrate that CCMud is fundamentally different from a traditional room-based MUD.

The new character will:

1. Obtain a physical map, compass, and basic tent-building instructions.
2. Leave town through continuous coordinate travel.
3. Travel at least two miles from town.
4. Select any physically suitable location rather than being sent to a predetermined campsite.
5. Build and pitch a tent.
6. Mark the tent’s real location on the physical map.
7. Travel far enough away to lose sight of it.
8. Use the map and compass to navigate back.
9. Find the same persistent tent waiting at its exact location.

Two miles is the recommended distance for a new player because it moves the character beyond the heavily patrolled one-mile zone while keeping the character within the generally safe five-mile region.

The two-mile requirement belongs to the introductory assignment, not to a universal camping rule. Characters may ordinarily camp wherever physical and ownership rules permit.

A tent does not require a formal land claim. Before property systems exist, remoteness and concealment provide its protection.


QUESTION 13 — TECHNOLOGICAL ERA
--------------------------------

CCMud uses an early-to-mid-19th-century frontier technology level, but it is not an American Old West setting.

The world supports:

- Pistols, rifles, and shotguns.
- Bows and traditional melee weapons.
- Knights and mounted warriors alongside gunfighters.
- Horses and wagons.
- Ranches, farms, homesteads, and prospecting.
- Hand tools, blacksmithing, woodworking, and small workshops.
- Sailing and other modest transportation where appropriate.

The initial world does not contain:

- Railroads.
- Huge industrial machinery.
- Major factories.
- Enormous established cities.
- A densely populated or fully developed civilization.

The frontier-homesteading experience is central. The world is sparsely populated and largely a blank slate. Players determine where civilization spreads, what communities arise, and what those communities become. Administrators may add flavor, characters, events, mysteries, and roleplay without predetermining the world’s full development.

“Knights on horseback brandishing revolvers” captures the desired setting character. The world is original and does not need to follow Earth’s exact firearm patents or historical dates.

QUESTION 15 — FIREARMS
----------------------

Firearm technology is deliberately mixed rather than tied to exact Earth chronology.

- Percussion-cap muzzle-loading firearms exist alongside cartridge firearms.
- Colt Peacemaker-style cartridge revolvers exist.
- Lever-action rifles exist.
- Shotguns may be single-shot or double-barrel.
- Firearms operate as single-shot attacks in combat: one projectile is fired per attack action, comparable conceptually to firing one arrow from a bow. There are no automatic weapons in this technology model.
- Firearms and bows may cause broadly comparable wounds depending on the hit, but weapon type, range, accuracy, and power matter substantially.
- Rifles have a major advantage in effective range, accuracy, and potential lethality. An appropriate rifle may plausibly kill a deer with a single well-placed shot at roughly 100 yards, while hitting the same target at that distance with a pistol should be extremely unlikely.
- Firearms are extremely expensive compared with bows and other simple weapons.
- An established adventurer carrying both a pistol and rifle may be fairly common, but acquiring them represents a significant investment and progression beyond inexpensive bows and arrows.
- Exact combat statistics, range bands, damage, reload times, and accuracy modifiers remain to be decided with the later combat rules.


QUESTION 16 — INITIAL TRANSPORTATION
------------------------------------

Initial implemented transportation consists of:

- Walking on foot.
- Riding horses.

Wagons and boats fit the setting and remain planned, but they will be implemented later rather than being required for the initial playable system.


QUESTION 17 — MAGIC IN THE SETTING
----------------------------------

Magic is extremely common and is used in everyday life. Magical workings are called Callings, not spells, matching the terminology of Crown & Call. The mere use of a Calling is not extraordinary in this world, although newly arrived player characters initially find magic surprising.


QUESTION 18 — CALLINGS AS MAGIC
-------------------------------

Callings are magic and use the MAGIC CAP. Each Calling requires a specified minimum MAGIC level. There are no spell slots or Vancian daily allotments; a qualified character may use a Calling on a combat turn, subject to later action and effect rules. Exact balance, costs, durations, and other limitations remain to be designed. Callings themselves belong to MAGIC; FAITH remains a distinct CAP/system unless later design explicitly connects them.


QUESTION 19 — PLAYABLE PEOPLES AT LAUNCH
-----------------------------------------

Player characters are strictly human at launch. Other playable peoples may be added in the future, but none are committed yet.


QUESTION 20 — ARE ALL PLAYER CHARACTERS HUMAN INITIALLY?
---------------------------------------------------------

Yes. Answered by Question 19. Initial character creation permits human player characters only.


QUESTION 21 — STARTING LOCATION AND DIFFICULTY
-----------------------------------------------

CCMud offers both a conventional town start and deliberately difficult remote starts.

Standard start:
- Choosing Normal without a distance begins the character in the local tavern at Origin.
- The character receives appropriate clothing and minimal coin ONLY (C01); no free tools, food, weapons, navigation or water/fire gear.

Remote start:
- The player chooses an outfitting level: Naked, Minimal, Normal, Extra, or Total.
- The player also chooses any rough starting distance from 10 through 10,000 miles from Origin, e.g. Normal 2000 or Naked 10000.
- The requested distance is approximate. The game chooses the actual direction and location and may place the character within plus or minus 50 percent of the requested distance. A request for 2,000 miles therefore means roughly 1,000–3,000 miles from Origin.
- The player chooses the rough distance, not the exact coordinate or direction.
- Naked means literally no possessions; the remote start must nevertheless be survivable, including reachable natural drinking water (C01). Normal Origin outfitting is now clothing plus small coin only. Minimal remains minimum outfitting; Extra and Total remain progressively greater outfitting. Their exact lists remain to be designed.

Thus Normal means the conventional tavern start, while Normal 2000 means Normal equipment at a randomized remote location roughly 2,000 miles from Origin.


QUESTION 22 — INITIAL SETTLEMENTS AND FORTS
--------------------------------------------

The world begins with one primary starting settlement, Origin. A series of military forts may eventually exist within roughly 100 miles of Origin, but they will not all be created at once. Begin with Origin and one military fort, then establish additional forts gradually as appropriate during development and play.


QUESTION 23 — CREATION OF ORIGIN
--------------------------------

Thomas will personally hand-pick Origin's location after examining the generated geography. The terrain itself remains procedurally generated, but Origin is manually designed and laid out rather than procedurally generated. Its initial population is approximately 200 NPCs.


QUESTION 24 — WHY PLAYER CHARACTERS ARRIVE
-------------------------------------------

Player characters are suddenly and magically transported into this world from their original homes. The transportation is generally involuntary, and a newly arrived character may be just as surprised to be here as local inhabitants are to see the newcomer appear.

There is no single prescribed reason. A character may have been banished, betrayed, deliberately magicked away by someone who wanted rid of them, transported accidentally, or brought here for some other known or unknown reason. The possibilities are intentionally broad so players may define their own histories.

The Westworld/synthetic-body premise previously discussed under Question 14 is not canonical. Magical transportation is canonical.

Once deposited here, the character must adapt and struggle to survive or die.


QUESTION 25 — STARTING KNOWLEDGE, TIME, AGELESSNESS, AND DEATH
--------------------------------------------------------------

A newly arrived character has zero knowledge of this world's society: no politics, drama, relationships, intrigue, history, geography, settlements, or current events. MAGIC and Callings are also a surprise.

The physical world is familiar. Ordinary physics and recognizable natural surroundings—earth, trees, sky, clouds, sun, moon, stars, and similar features—behave much as the character expects from home.

Player characters may originate from essentially any place and any historical era. One may come from a 21st-century society while another may come from something resembling the 10th century.

The world's calendar is based on the arrival of its oldest inhabitant more than 700 years ago. The game begins in approximately Year 731.

Inhabitants do not age after arriving in this world and cannot die of old age. They know they are permanently bound to this world.

Physical death does not necessarily end a character. At death the character is magically removed. The player may retire the character or return that character to the world while retaining all memories.

Death and respawn rules established here:
- Murder by another player NEVER qualifies for a merciful respawn. Continuing after murder automatically means Naked 10000.
- A player/account receives exactly ONE lifetime reprieve for an accidental or environmental death, not one reprieve per character.
- Qualifying deaths include events such as falling from a cliff, drowning, or being killed by wildlife such as a bear.
- When the player's one-time reprieve is used, the player may respawn the affected character using any starting configuration they wish.
- If the reprieve has already been used, later deaths of any of that player's characters lead to Naked 10000 if the character is continued.
- If the reprieve has not been used, it remains available until a qualifying accidental/environmental death occurs.
- Memories survive respawning. A murdered character can therefore theoretically return for revenge, but Naked 10000 makes returning to the murderer potentially extremely difficult.

These decisions supersede the earlier undecided synthetic-body/resurrection possibilities under Question 14. Additional exact death mechanics may still be refined when the combat/death questions are reached later.


QUESTION 26 — STARTING MAPS AND COMPASSES
------------------------------------------

Characters do not automatically begin with a map or compass.

In Origin, maps and compasses are readily available for purchase. A wilderness character—including someone forced into Naked 10000—can eventually craft both a map and a compass. Crafting a compass requires finding specific minerals, so reliable directional navigation must be earned through exploration and resource discovery rather than being granted automatically.

Exact mineral requirements and crafting procedures remain to be designed.


QUESTION 27 — ESSENTIAL SERVICES IN ORIGIN
------------------------------------------

Origin begins as a functional frontier settlement of roughly 200 NPCs. Its initial services and structures include at least:

- Tavern/inn.
- General store.
- Stable/farrier.
- Stockyards.
- Lumberyard.
- Blacksmith.
- Stonemason.
- Houses.
- Apartments.
- Doctor's office.
- Metalsmith.
- Armorsmith.
- Weaponsmith.
- Gunsmith.
- Baker.

This list is deliberately not exhaustive or final. Additional businesses and services may be added naturally when Origin is actually designed. The important decision is that Origin should function as a believable small settlement rather than merely as a collection of player-service NPCs.


QUESTION 28 — WHO OPERATES INITIAL CIVILIZATION?
-------------------------------------------------

NPCs operate Origin and all of its storefronts from the beginning. Each storefront is available for eventual purchase by a player.

When a player purchases a storefront, the existing NPC merchant remains and continues operating normal sales and day-to-day business functions. Player ownership therefore does not remove the NPC who previously ran the business.

If the player-owner dies, retires, leaves, or otherwise ceases operating the business, the NPC takes over its operation again. Exact later rules for legal ownership, inheritance, inventory, profits, resale, and related consequences remain to be designed.

The important principle is that Origin and its economy continue functioning even when particular players disappear.


QUESTION 29 — CHARACTERS AND ACCOUNTS PER PERSON
-------------------------------------------------

Ordinary players are limited to one character per account and one account per real person.

Staff members may receive exceptions allowing additional accounts or characters when required for staff duties, administration, testing, events, NPC portrayal, or similar legitimate purposes. Staff exceptions do not create ordinary gameplay privileges.

Multiple accounts connecting from the same IP address are not automatically prohibited because multiple real people may share a household or connection. However, shared-IP accounts may be required to provide reasonable proof that they represent separate real people. Exact verification and privacy-conscious enforcement procedures remain to be designed under administration policy.

The one-time accidental/environmental death reprieve established in Question 25 therefore belongs to the player's single ordinary account and cannot be refreshed by creating alternate characters or accounts.


QUESTION 30 — SAME-ACCOUNT CHARACTER INTERACTION
-------------------------------------------------

Skipped as inapplicable for ordinary players.

Question 29 establishes one character per ordinary account and one account per real person. Ordinary players therefore cannot use same-account alternate characters to share property, maps, messages, inheritance, or other resources. Staff-only exceptions exist for legitimate staff functions and must not provide ordinary gameplay advantages.


QUESTION 31 — SIMULTANEOUS CHARACTERS AND STAFF PERMISSIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

Ordinary players cannot have multiple simultaneous characters because they are limited to one account and one character.

Staff may be allowed to control multiple characters simultaneously, but this is not automatic for all staff. All staff capabilities are managed through granular permission-based staff management controlled by senior staff. Permissions are assigned individually: for example, Sam may be granted permission to multi while Jerry is not. Staff titles alone do not confer every staff power.

Staff exceptions exist for administration, testing, events, NPC portrayal, and similar legitimate duties and must not provide an ordinary gameplay advantage.


QUESTION 32 — ALT ABUSE
-----------------------

Skipped as inapplicable to ordinary players.

Ordinary players have one account and one character, so they cannot use alts to bypass geography, scouting, specialization, communication, progression, or other restrictions. Any staff exceptions are permission-controlled and are not ordinary gameplay privileges.


QUESTION 33 — CHARACTER CREATION CHOICES
----------------------------------------

Character creation includes:

- Name.
- Short description, for example: "a gray-haired man".
- Long description (ldesc), for example: "A gray-haired man with a bushy beard stands here."
- Full description: a few sentences describing the character in a neutral tone without actions.
- Age.
- CAP allocation.

Height, weight, and build are not separately tracked fields; Question 45 later established that these have zero mechanical effect and may simply be described in the full description if the player considers them important.

Starting outfitting and location choices under Question 21 are part of entering the world but are conceptually separate from the descriptive character fields above.


QUESTION 34 — INITIAL CAP ALLOCATION AND RETURNING CHARACTERS
-------------------------------------------------------------

A brand-new character receives 15 CAP points distributed among exactly five CAPs. Each of those five selected CAPs must receive at least 1 point and no more than 5 points. The other CAPs begin at 0.

Death may lead to different return paths:

- Naked 10000: the returning character retains ALL CAP levels held at the time of death. The penalty is geographic and material rather than loss of advancement.
- Reduced-CAP return: each individual CAP is transferred at 50% of its value at the time of death, always rounded down. This is not a pooled total that may be redistributed. MAGIC 12 becomes 6; MAGIC 13 also becomes 6; WEAPN 18 becomes 9.
- Newbie restart: the player may instead restart under the normal newbie rules and receive newbie benefits, but CAPs reset to the normal 15-point, exactly-five-CAP allocation.

Question 25 previously established that murder by another player never qualifies for the one-time accidental/environmental reprieve and that continuing after murder uses Naked 10000. The exact eligibility among the other return routes for non-murder deaths should be reconciled when the full death system is designed; do not silently treat murder as eligible for a more favorable route.


QUESTION 35 — UNSELECTED CAPS
-----------------------------

Yes. Any CAP not selected during newbie allocation begins at 0. Exactly five CAPs receive the initial 15 points.


QUESTION 36 — STARTING CAP MINIMUMS AND MAXIMUMS
------------------------------------------------

A newbie must distribute exactly 15 CAP points among exactly five CAPs. Each selected CAP has a minimum starting value of 1 and a maximum starting value of 5. All unselected CAPs begin at 0.


QUESTION 37 — SUGGESTED CAP PACKAGES
------------------------------------

Character creation offers optional suggested CAP packages/templates. These are not classes. A package is simply a legal prefilled 15-point distribution across exactly five CAPs.

New players should be strongly encouraged to choose a package, but manual allocation remains available.


QUESTION 38 — AUTOMATIC OPENING CRAFTS
--------------------------------------

New characters do not choose starting Crafts directly.

Certain Crafts will be designated as opening Crafts for particular CAPs. A character who has the qualifying CAP, typically at 1 or greater, automatically receives that CAP's designated opener Craft. For example, ATHLX may eventually grant an opener such as Gather Firewood.

The exact opener Crafts and any thresholds beyond the basic CAP qualification will be determined later. A CAP at 0 does not grant its opener. C01's universal basic cordage and accepted primitive bootstrap must remain possible; do not turn an optional opener example into a new prerequisite that blocks Naked survival.

This fits the broader Craft model already discussed elsewhere: CAPs are numeric capabilities, while Crafts represent learned practical knowledge and transformations.


QUESTION 39 — BACKGROUND AND MECHANICS
--------------------------------------

Character background is descriptive and roleplay-only. Claiming to have been a surgeon, blacksmith, soldier, or any other profession in the character's prior life grants no CAP points, Crafts, equipment, abilities, or other mechanical bonuses. Actual game mechanics determine what the character can do.


QUESTION 40 — INEFFECTIVE CHARACTER BUILDS
-------------------------------------------

A player may make a poor but legal CAP allocation. The system does not automatically correct or protect the player from an ineffective manual build.

New players should be strongly encouraged to use one of the suggested CAP packages, but a player who chooses manual allocation accepts the consequences of that choice.


QUESTION 41 — RETIREMENT, REMAKING, AND DELIBERATE CYCLING
----------------------------------------------------------

Characters may be retired.

- Every previous character remains permanently visible in the player's pfile/history and is marked with its final status, such as DIED or RETIRED.
- Retirement generates an admin-news notice.
- Multiple retirements within a short period may attract administrator review.
- Obvious deliberate suicides used to cycle characters may receive the same scrutiny.
- Senior staff may impose a temporary advancement freeze when they determine character cycling is being abused.
- The duration/status of such a freeze is shown on the player's pfile.

This is staff judgment rather than an automatic declaration that every suicide or retirement is abuse.


QUESTION 42 — CHARACTER NAME UNIQUENESS
---------------------------------------

Official character names are globally unique forever. Once an official name has been used, it may never be reused, even after that character dies or retires.

This permanent canonical name is primarily important for administration and historical identification. Players may use similar-looking names or claim other names in roleplay, but the underlying official name remains unique.


QUESTION 43 — LEARNING NAMES, PRIVATE LABELS, AND TARGETING
-----------------------------------------------------------

A character's actual character-file/canonical name is never automatically revealed through ordinary gameplay. A character whose official name is Adolph may tell everyone that his name is Gray; the game does not automatically expose the truth.

Characters may normally be targeted through suitable descriptive keywords, such as "gray" for a gray-haired man.

If reasonably practical to implement, a player may also attach an arbitrary private label to another character, such as Gray, cheater, bigfatpig, or pushover.

- The label is the observing player's private note and is not guaranteed to be true.
- Only the label maker sees/uses that label.
- The private label becomes a valid targeting keyword for the label maker only. If one player labels Adolph "Gray", that player can use commands such as LOOK GRAY; other players gain no such keyword or knowledge from the label.

Exact validation and edge cases for arbitrary labels may be addressed during implementation.


QUESTION 44 — HOODS AND DISGUISE
---------------------------------

An appropriate hood may physically conceal a character's face so that the visible identity becomes generic, such as "a hooded figure."

More elaborate disguise systems—hair dye, false beards, makeup, impersonation, disguise contests, and similar mechanics—are deferred.

C03 now confirms that adequate recognition displays the observer's label despite ordinary clothing changes. Concealed identity does not gain magical recognition from a label; detailed exceptional disguise handling remains deferred.


QUESTION 45 — BODY SIZE AND MECHANICS
-------------------------------------

Height, weight, and build have zero mechanical effect and are not tracked as separate character fields. If a player considers such details important, they may include them in the full description.

Descriptive claims do not establish mechanical superlatives such as being the world's tallest, biggest, or strongest character. Capability comes from the game's actual mechanics and CAPs. C04 resolves Q61: ordinary humans share a standard mechanical reference weight, independent of descriptive weight/build.


QUESTION 46 — DELIBERATE LOGOUT AND CAMPING
--------------------------------------------

Characters may safely log out from appropriate locations such as a tavern, inn, apartment, or similar safe place.

In the wilderness, a character may use CAMP in an appropriate location. CAMP is a delayed action requiring the character to remain still for a set period; the exact duration will be determined later. When CAMP completes, the character curls up/goes to sleep and safely logs out.

Logging out safely does not guarantee that the surrounding world will still be safe when the player returns.


QUESTION 47 — FULL LOGOUT AND AGGRESSION TIMER
-----------------------------------------------

Once a character is completely logged off, the character leaves the active world and cannot be seen, targeted, robbed, moved, attacked, or killed through ordinary gameplay. The game stores the character's return position/state.

A five-minute aggression/combat timer prevents logout entirely. A character who has been in combat/aggression during the preceding five minutes cannot complete a normal logout or CAMP logout. Exact actions that reset the aggression timer will be defined with combat.


QUESTION 48 — WHERE CAMPING IS ALLOWED
---------------------------------------

A deliberate logout requires either an appropriate safe-logout location or a successful CAMP.

CAMP is contextually restricted. A character cannot CAMP in physically inappropriate locations such as the middle of a lake or in the street outside the tavern. The character must first be in an appropriate place and satisfy the CAMP delay. The aggression timer overrides otherwise valid logout conditions.


QUESTION 49 — UNEXPECTED DISCONNECT
------------------------------------

An unexpected disconnect does not immediately remove the character from the active world.

- With no active aggression, the disconnected character remains physically present for one real-world minute and then safely leaves the active world.
- If an aggression timer is active, the character remains physically present until the five-minute aggression timer expires, plus one additional real-world minute.
- New qualifying aggression during the grace period may reset/extend the aggression timing as appropriate.

This prevents disconnecting from functioning as an instant escape while giving ordinary connection failures a short, finite exposure period.

ACTIVE COMBAT CLARIFICATION (C06): no pause or extra reconnect window; only the normal turn-response period. Timeout forfeits MOVE/PREP/ACT. Link-death may immediately lower Aggression to -10 subject to W1's scope. Normal injuries/death apply. The exact logout/ongoing-encounter boundary must not be inferred as an extra combat escape mechanic.


QUESTION 50 — INTERACTION DURING DISCONNECT GRACE
-------------------------------------------------

During the disconnect/grace period, the character remains physically present and may be attacked, robbed, moved, rescued, or otherwise interacted with normally.

Once the character becomes fully logged off, the character leaves the active world and ordinary players can no longer interact with the character.


QUESTION 51 — DISCONNECTION WHILE TRAVELING
--------------------------------------------

There is no ordinary travel while disconnected. An unexpected disconnect immediately stops the character's active movement at the current coordinate, after which the normal disconnect/grace rules apply.

A character deliberately using CAMP must stop traveling before camping.


QUESTION 52 — OFFLINE PASSENGERS ON CONVEYANCES
------------------------------------------------

No separate consent is required for movement caused by a conveyance. If a character safely logs out while aboard a wagon, boat, ship, or similar conveyance, the stored character position remains attached to that conveyance.

If the conveyance moves while the player is offline, the character's return position moves with it automatically: if you are on the wagon, you go where the wagon goes. The fully logged-off character remains non-targetable and cannot be killed through ordinary gameplay while absent.


QUESTION 53 — CONSTRUCTION NEAR A LOGGED-OFF CHARACTER
-------------------------------------------------------

Logged-off characters have a 500-foot construction buffer around their stored position.

If someone builds anything or pitches a tent within 500 feet of a logged-off character, the logged-off character's stored position is automatically pushed directly away from the new construction in a straight line until the stored position is 500 feet from it.

The character remains fully offline and uninvolved; only the stored return coordinate changes. This resolves the spatial conflict when construction occurs rather than waiting until the absent player returns.

Rare geometric/terrain edge cases, such as multiple structures or displacement toward impassable terrain, may be handled when the implementation is designed.


QUESTION 54 — SLEEPING BENEFITS
-------------------------------

Sleep is not a survival requirement and there is no fatigue or sleep-deprivation meter. A character does not suffer merely for remaining awake.

Sleep has one routine mechanical benefit: it increases natural wound-healing rate compared with being awake. It does not automatically grant extra stamina recovery, advancement bonuses, or other general buffs.

Sleep may also produce a dream or vision. Administrators may use dreams and visions for narrative, atmosphere, mysteries, Callings, clues, or administrator-run content.


QUESTION 55 — OFFLINE ENVIRONMENTAL DANGER
-------------------------------------------

A fully logged-off character is ordinarily immune to environmental damage and death.

CCMud does not autonomously simulate destructive natural disasters as lethal random systems. Major natural disasters are administrator-run roleplay events.

An administrator deliberately running such an event may explicitly determine that offline characters are affected—for example, ruling that everyone inside a particular house died in a fire. Such outcomes are exceptional staff adjudications rather than routine offline simulation.


QUESTION 56 — LOGOUT AND NON-COMBAT DANGER
-------------------------------------------

There is no broad "danger nearby" flag that prevents camping merely because a potential threat exists.

CAMP is prevented by physical impossibility, inappropriate circumstances, and actual aggression. A drowning character cannot CAMP because the character is not in a valid camping situation. A character who can see a bear a mile away going about its business may still CAMP normally.

Once actual combat/aggression occurs, the five-minute aggression timer governs logout.


QUESTION 57 — MOVEMENT SPEEDS
-----------------------------

Base movement speeds in game-world terms are:

- TRUDGE: 1 mph.
- WALK: 3 mph.
- JOG: 5 mph.
- RUN: 8 mph.
- SPRINT: 11 mph.

The normal world clock runs at 6× real time. Travel receives an additional 4× acceleration, making ideal uninterrupted travel cover 24 times the listed game-world miles per real-world hour.

Theoretical uninterrupted distances per real-world hour are therefore:

- TRUDGE: 24 miles.
- WALK: 72 miles.
- JOG: 120 miles.
- RUN: 192 miles.
- SPRINT: 264 miles.

These are theoretical movement rates, not sustainable endurance distances. JOG, RUN, and especially SPRINT are constrained by stamina and cannot ordinarily be sustained for an entire real-world hour.


QUESTION 58 — STAMINA MODEL
---------------------------

Stamina is represented internally as a continuous numeric value with a nominal maximum of 20.0. It may use fractional values, allowing regeneration and expenditure such as 2.675 rather than forcing integer arithmetic.

The player-facing prompt does not expose the precise internal stamina number. Instead it displays six stamina bands using plus signs. At full health and full stamina the prompt is:

<***** / ++++++>

The asterisks represent health/wound condition; stars disappear as wounds worsen. The six plus signs represent stamina condition; plus signs disappear as stamina falls. With an internal 0–20 range, each visible stamina band represents approximately 3.333 stamina:

- greater than 16.667 through 20.000: ++++++
- greater than 13.333 through 16.667: +++++
- greater than 10.000 through 13.333: ++++
- greater than 6.667 through 10.000: +++
- greater than 3.333 through 6.667: ++
- greater than 0 through 3.333: +
- 0: no plus signs

Twenty is therefore primarily an internal mathematical maximum; the player-facing stamina display has six coarse condition bands.

Stamina recovery is processed on a 15-real-second heartbeat/tick. Because the world clock runs at 6× real time, each recovery tick represents 90 seconds of game time.

The intended recovery rate scales with ATHLX. The initial reference points discussed were approximately 1 stamina per recovery tick at ATHLX 0 and approximately 3 stamina per recovery tick at ATHLX 20. Because stamina is floating-point internally, intermediate ATHLX values may use smooth fractional recovery rates rather than integer breakpoints. Question 59 locks the exact recovery formula.

Passive stamina regeneration continues even while the character is moving. Movement and other strenuous activity have their own stamina expenditures. Thus:

net stamina change = passive regeneration - activity expenditure

SPRINT should consume stamina much faster than passive regeneration. RUN and JOG should also normally produce net stamina loss at rates to be determined. WALK should eventually produce a net positive stamina change, allowing a tired character to recover while continuing to travel, but more slowly than resting.

Example behavior: a character may sprint for 20 real seconds, lose substantial stamina, and still receive the single passive recovery tick that occurs during those 20 seconds. The character may then slow to WALK and gradually recover from one visible stamina bar back to full stamina without stopping entirely.

The exact stamina costs for TRUDGE, WALK, JOG, RUN, SPRINT, terrain, wounds, encumbrance, and other exertion remain to be designed in subsequent questions.


QUESTION 59 — STAMINA RECOVERY DETAILS
----------------------------------------

The exact passive stamina recovery formula is:

stamina recovered per 15-real-second tick = 1 + (ATHLX / 10)

Examples:

- ATHLX 0: 1.0 stamina per tick.
- ATHLX 5: 1.5 stamina per tick.
- ATHLX 10: 2.0 stamina per tick.
- ATHLX 15: 2.5 stamina per tick.
- ATHLX 20: 3.0 stamina per tick.

Fractional stamina is retained internally. Passive regeneration applies whether the character is stationary or moving; activity expenditure is calculated separately.


QUESTION 60 — MOVEMENT MODIFIERS
--------------------------------

Terrain, weather, elevation, wounds, and encumbrance modify actual travel speed rather than changing the passive stamina-recovery formula. These modifiers are multiplicative rather than additive, so several severe conditions can make travel extremely slow without producing a negative speed.

Terrain:
- The procedural terrain generator should eventually provide a deterministic Terrain Movement Factor from 1.00 down to 0.50.
- The factor should be derived from the physical character of the ground at the character's coordinate, such as slope, ruggedness, vegetation, and ground cover, rather than from arbitrary room-like difficulty classes.
- The exact calculation is deliberately deferred until the terrain generator is being developed/refined.
- Elevation must not be counted twice: terrain represents how difficult the ground itself is to traverse, while the separate elevation modifier represents the effects of high altitude.

Weather:
- Weather may reduce travel speed by as much as 50% under extreme conditions.

Elevation:
- Elevation may reduce travel speed by as much as 50% under extreme high-altitude conditions.

Environmental modifiers stack multiplicatively. A 3 mph WALK subjected to maximum terrain, weather, and elevation penalties becomes:

3 × 0.50 × 0.50 × 0.50 = 0.375 mph.

The character is still using WALK and receives/pays the normal WALK stamina behavior; environmental conditions reduce physical progress rather than changing the movement mode's stamina rate.

Wounds — WORLD-TRAVEL HISTORY, NOT COMBAT SPEED:
The following old names/derates are retained for the travel record only. C04/C05 and CC C5/T3 govern combat: wounds never reduce tactical Speed, and current wound names are FLSH/LITE/DEEP/SEVR/GRAV/MORT. Do not treat the old names as a second combat wound ladder.
- Light: 0% speed derate.
- Moderate: 5% speed derate.
- Minor: 15% speed derate.
- Major: 30% speed derate.
- Grave: 50% speed derate.
- Mortal: unconscious; no movement.

These wound derates are the final cumulative values for each wound level, not additional percentages to be summed.

Encumbrance — RECONCILED by Q61 and C04:
The old thresholds against descriptive body weight are OBSOLETE. Let C be maximum capacity derived from the shared mechanical reference weight and ATHLX. Through 50% of C there is no derate; from 50% to 100% the derate rises linearly to 50%; above C the character cannot move until load is reduced. All recursive carried mass counts. No numeric reference weight was selected.

The overall movement model may therefore be expressed conceptually as:

actual speed = base movement speed × terrain factor × weather factor × elevation factor × wound factor × encumbrance factor

Exact weather and elevation scales, and the terrain-generator formula, remain to be designed later.

QUESTION 61 — MAXIMUM CARRYING CAPACITY
---------------------------------------

ATHLX substantially increases maximum carrying capacity.

Maximum carrying capacity = standard mechanical reference weight × (1 + 0.05 × ATHLX).

RECONCILED (C04): "body weight" as descriptive/player-selected weight is OBSOLETE for this calculation. All ordinary humans share the same mechanical reference constant; its numerical value remains unset.

Examples:
- ATHLX 0: 100% of standard mechanical reference weight.
- ATHLX 5: 125%.
- ATHLX 10: 150%.
- ATHLX 15: 175%.
- ATHLX 20: 200%.

Encumbrance speed penalties scale against the character's actual maximum carrying capacity, not against descriptive weight or the unmodified reference constant. A character suffers no encumbrance speed derate through 50% of maximum carrying capacity. From 50% to 100% of maximum capacity, the speed derate rises continuously and linearly from 0% to 50%. At full carrying capacity, speed is reduced by 50%. Above maximum carrying capacity, the character cannot move.


QUESTION 62 — BULK
------------------

Bulk is not separately modeled at this time. Carrying capacity is primarily weight-based. The design deliberately accepts some abstraction rather than adding a general volume/bulk simulation. Particularly impossible objects may still be handled by their specific physical rules if necessary.


QUESTION 63 — TERRAIN PASSABILITY BY TRAVEL MODE
-------------------------------------------------

The deterministic Terrain Movement Factor (TMF) established in Question 60 also governs whether terrain can be traversed by different travel methods. Initial thresholds are intentionally tunable during terrain testing:

- TMF 0.80–1.00: passable on foot, horseback, and wagon.
- TMF 0.70–<0.80: passable on foot and horseback; wagon passable but difficult.
- TMF 0.60–<0.70: passable on foot; horseback passable but difficult; wagon impassable.
- TMF 0.50–<0.60: passable on foot under extreme difficulty; horseback and wagon impassable.
- Below 0.50: impassable terrain for ordinary travel.

Categorical physical barriers such as deep water or a sheer cliff are not merely low-TMF ground; they require an appropriate crossing method.

Roads may later provide an improved effective travel surface without changing the underlying terrain, potentially allowing wagons through naturally wagon-impassable country.


QUESTION 64 — IMPASSABLE OBSTRUCTIONS
-------------------------------------

When continuous travel encounters an impassable obstruction, movement stops at the obstruction and the player is told why. The game does not silently pass through or automatically bypass the obstruction.

Examples include rivers, sheer cliffs, walls, buildings, dense physical barriers, and terrain below the current travel method's passability threshold.

The browser client should eventually support optional audio. UI notification sounds may include a one-time beep/chime when travel transitions from moving to stopped. Atmospheric world sounds may include events such as wolf howls, bear roars, weather, running water, campfires, crowds, horses, or smithing.

Sound supplements rather than replaces textual information. UI and atmospheric audio should have separate mute/volume controls. Repeated sounds require cooldowns/limits so they do not play continuously or become audio clutter. Ambient audio should be restrained, and a travel-stop notification fires once on the transition to stopped rather than repeating while the character remains stopped.


QUESTION 65 — AUTOMATIC OBSTACLE ROUTING
----------------------------------------

Continuous travel does not automatically steer around obstacles. If the selected course becomes impassable, movement stops and the player chooses how to proceed.


QUESTION 66 — ACTIONS WHILE TRAVELING
-------------------------------------

Travel is a continuing state and does not prevent ordinary compatible interaction. Characters may perform reasonable actions while moving, including LOOK, WATCH, conversation, checking inventory, maps, and compasses, eating/drinking, and manipulating reasonable carried items.

Actions inherently requiring a stationary character—such as gathering, crafting, construction, butchering, or making a fire—require the character to stop first.


QUESTION 67 — RELATIVE POSITIONING AND MOVEMENT INTENTS
-------------------------------------------------------

CCMud should support convenient relative-movement controllers operating on the same continuous coordinates used by ordinary travel. These are tactical movement intents, not teleportation or guaranteed outcomes. Exact syntax and combat integration are provisional and may be substantially revised during combat design.

CLOSE ON <target> <distance>
Approach the target directly and stop upon reaching the requested distance. CLOSE never moves the character away merely to create the specified separation. If already within the requested distance, the character does not move.

Example:
CLOSE ON JENNY 10

KITE <target> <distance> <preferred direction>
Attempt to maintain the specified separation from the target. KITE may require no movement if the desired distance is already maintained and the target is stationary. If the target closes, the character retreats, favoring the specified direction, and automatically increases movement speed as necessary and physically possible to preserve the requested gap. If the target is too far away, the character may close toward the requested distance.

Example:
KITE JENNY 10 SE

KITE is not immunity from melee. Terrain, obstacles, stamina, wounds, encumbrance, relative speeds, and the target's actions may make the desired separation impossible to maintain.

ORBIT <target> <distance> <CW/CCW>
Continuously move around the target at approximately the requested radius in the player-selected clockwise or counterclockwise direction. Unlike KITE, ORBIT deliberately keeps the character moving even when the target is stationary. The controller adjusts inward/outward and changes speed as necessary and physically possible to maintain the requested radius. ORBIT is defensive positioning rather than substantial travel away from the encounter area.

Example:
ORBIT JENNY 10 CW

RETREAT <target> <preferred direction>
Continuously attempt to increase separation from the target while favoring the specified direction. There is no desired stopping distance. The controller uses the minimum practical movement speed needed to increase/preserve increasing separation and escalates automatically when pursuit requires it, up to what the character can physically sustain.

Example:
RETREAT JENNY SE

All relative-movement commands remain subject to actual world geometry and physics. They optimize only for the explicitly named target; for example, successfully kiting Jenny may inadvertently move the character into Bob's melee reach. This is intentional. In multi-character combat, positioning, terrain, obstacles, headings, speeds, and chosen movement intents may make the battlefield tactically significant.

These ideas remain provisional for experimentation. C05's fully turn-based combat is now authoritative. No continuous controller may generate free out-of-turn movement, evade Speed budgets, or bypass ENGAGED/WITHDRAW. Continuous combat automation implied by this older proposal is OBSOLETE.


QUESTION 68 — NON-COMBAT POSTURE
--------------------------------

Non-combat posture tracks four basic states:

- STANDING: the normal default posture.
- SITTING: either on the ground or on an appropriate object such as a chair, bench, or log.
- LYING: voluntarily lying down.
- MOUNTED: aboard a horse or other rideable mount.

Kneeling is not separately tracked unless a later mechanical need emerges; it may be represented through emotes. SITTING and MOUNTED may retain a reference to the object or mount involved.

PRONE remains a formal combat condition and is distinct from voluntarily choosing the LYING posture.


QUESTION 69 — PHYSICAL SURVIVAL NEEDS (COMPLETED)
----------------------------------------------------

Food/calories and water are genuine physiological requirements, deliberately paced so they matter without becoming maintenance chores.

Hunger is communicated descriptively rather than primarily as a visible numeric meter. Intended progression includes:
- peckish
- hungry
- famished
- starving
- dying of hunger

Thirst follows an equivalent escalating progression ending in dying of thirst.

While dying of hunger or thirst:
- normal wound healing stops;
- stamina recovery becomes substantially slower;
- one flesh wound becomes due for every full game-world day spent in the dying state while the character is subject to the active survival process;
- approximately 32 continuous game-world days in that state would ultimately be fatal through accumulated flesh wounds.

Fully logged-off characters never receive starvation/dehydration wounds. Hunger/thirst state may advance offline, but wounds are not applied offline. On login the game checks whether a wound is due; if so it applies the appropriate current wound and warns the player approximately how many additional days at the present rate are likely to be fatal. A long absence must not dump a large backlog of offline wounds onto the character.

Food and water depletion is intentionally slow. After eating and drinking to full, it should be at least about one real-world week before ordinary hunger becomes noteworthy. Exact calories, hydration, activity modifiers, thresholds, and stamina-recovery reduction remain for later balancing.

Sleep is NOT a survival requirement and has no fatigue/deprivation meter. Sleep does increase natural wound-healing rate and may permit administrator-delivered dreams or visions. This agrees with the current Q54 record. CC O1 supplies the recovery rules and pauses recovery during combat; exact world-clock integration remains as noted in C05.

Shelter is NOT a physiological requirement and lack of shelter causes no damage merely because the character is unsheltered. Shelter is valuable as a physical place: safe/useful storage, hanging or keeping a deer carcass, cooking stew, warming coffee, sleeping, working, getting out of weather, and establishing a useful wilderness home. Weather may affect other systems such as movement, but there is no hidden shelter/exposure-damage meter.


QUESTION 70 — SURVIVAL PRESSURE
-------------------------------

Answered by Q69. Survival needs are real and potentially lethal, but intentionally slow and forgiving enough that eating and drinking do not become routine busywork.


QUESTION 71 — PHYSICAL MOVEMENT ABILITIES
-----------------------------------------

SWIM, CLIMB, JUMP, CRAWL, and FALL are genuine physical mechanics, not merely emotes. ATHLX is the primary CAP for these actions, with physical circumstances modifying difficulty.

An obstacle that blocks ordinary travel may still be attempted by another physical method. A steep cliff may stop WALK but permit a deliberate CLIMB attempt. Failure can cause a real fall and real wounds based on the circumstances.

Water can be deliberately entered and swum. Swimming is genuinely dangerous. Equipment and carried weight matter physically; entering deep water in chainmail or plate is allowed but may be a very poor decision. Exact checks, stamina costs, drowning rules, fall severity, and equipment effects are deferred to detailed implementation.


QUESTION 72 — COASTLINES AND DELIBERATE WATER ENTRY
----------------------------------------------------

Automatic/continuous land travel stops at the water's edge. It never casually walks a character into deep water.

A player may deliberately enter and SWIM into a river, lake, or ocean. Water is not an artificial world boundary. Swimming into open ocean is allowed; the consequences depend on actual physical circumstances, equipment, carried weight, stamina, weather, currents/waves, and swimming ability.

Design principle: prevent accidental stupidity, but permit deliberate stupidity.


QUESTION 73 — PASSIVE PERCEPTION AND THE SENSE CAP
--------------------------------------------------

Passive perception exists and is governed by SENSE.

SENSE replaces RIDNG entirely; this is not a ninth CAP. The eight CAPs are now:
WEAPN, RANGE, ATHLX, THIEV, SENSE, MAGIC, ACADX, FAITH.

Character creation remains 15 points distributed among exactly five CAPs, with the previously established starting limits. Any older RIDNG references in this document should be understood as superseded and should be migrated to SENSE where appropriate rather than implying a current RIDNG CAP.


QUESTION 74 — ACTIVE SEARCHING AND TRACKING
-------------------------------------------

SEARCH is fundamentally a SENSE action.

TRACK is also primarily based on SENSE, but relevant secondary expertise may improve tracking. A representative combined-CAP idea is SENSE + (THIEV / 10). The exact formula is not yet frozen; the locked principle is that SENSE determines perception while another relevant CAP may contribute specialized expertise in interpreting what was perceived.


QUESTION 75 — HIDING VS. PERCEPTION
-----------------------------------

Deliberate hiding is an opposed THIEV-versus-SENSE system, heavily modified by actual physical circumstances such as distance, lighting, vegetation, cover, movement, and similar factors.

HISTORICAL: the October 2 SEARCH design grants movement-based opportunities, not an automatic bonus.
Passive perception and active searching are meaningfully different. Merely being nearby gives the observer passive SENSE opportunities; deliberately SEARCHing provides a substantial advantage. Exact modifiers are deferred.


QUESTION 76 — WHAT MAY BE HIDDEN
--------------------------------

Players may attempt to HIDE anything they reasonably can: themselves, objects, caches, carcasses, camps, tracks, supplies, and other concealable things.

The character who hid something always sees/recognizes their own hidden thing and does not need to rediscover it with SEARCH. Other characters must defeat its concealment through the normal perception/search mechanics.


QUESTION 77 — RECOGNITION
-------------------------

There is no automatic recognition progression from repeated exposure. Repeatedly encountering an unknown character does not make the game automatically identify or remember that person.

Recognition is controlled through the player's private labeling system established earlier. If the player wants to remember an unknown person, the player creates a private label.


QUESTION 78 — PERSISTENCE AND LIMITS OF PRIVATE LABELS
------------------------------------------------------

Private player labels persist indefinitely until the player changes or removes them. A label remains associated with the underlying identified character even years later and despite changes of clothing.

A player cannot create a new identifying label for someone whose identity is currently concealed by a cloak, disguise, or similar identity-obscuring mechanism. The label system must not become a backdoor for defeating disguise.


QUESTION 79 — CONTINUOUS DARKNESS AND NATURAL LIGHT
---------------------------------------------------

Darkness is modeled as a continuous environmental illumination value, not a binary LIGHT/DARK flag. Available natural light directly affects visibility distance and perception/SENSE effectiveness.

Natural illumination should derive from real conditions such as time of day, sun position, twilight, moon phase and position, cloud cover, weather, and related factors. A clear full-moon prairie night may provide substantial usable visibility; a moonless overcast night may be extremely dark. Enclosed underground spaces without light may approach genuine pitch black.

Exact illumination mathematics are deferred, but the world should ultimately calculate a meaningful ambient illumination value consumed by perception.


QUESTION 80 — ARTIFICIAL LIGHT AND FALLOFF
------------------------------------------

Artificial light sources have physical illumination with distance falloff rather than a binary illuminated radius. Torches, lanterns, candles, campfires, and similar sources provide strong useful light nearby, progressively less useful illumination farther away, and eventually negligible contribution.

Seeing BY a light source is distinct from seeing THE light source. A campfire may illuminate useful detail only relatively nearby while its glow can itself be visible from vastly farther away across open terrain at night.

Natural ambient illumination and artificial illumination combine into the effective visibility/perception environment.


QUESTION 81 — LOOK, SCAN, SEARCH, AND TRACK
-------------------------------------------

These are meaningfully distinct perception actions:
- LOOK: ordinary observation of the immediate surroundings.
- SCAN: deliberate examination of the broader or more distant environment.
- SEARCH: careful examination of a localized area for concealed or subtle things.
- TRACK: identifying and following physical signs left by people, animals, or other moving things.

SENSE underlies these actions, but the action determines what the character is attempting to perceive and how. TRACK may additionally benefit from relevant secondary CAP expertise.


QUESTION 82 — SEARCHING AND TRACKING WHILE MOVING
-------------------------------------------------

HISTORICAL / SUPERSEDED by the October 2 moving SEARCH design in CCMud/DESIGN.md:
SEARCH requires the character to stop moving because it represents careful examination of a localized area.

CURRENT / RESOLVED by C03: TRACK without a target discovers nearby detectable trails, nearest first. TRACK <trail>, e.g. TRACK RABBIT TRAIL #1, physically follows recorded signs at TRUDGE. Lost sign stops movement at the last resolvable segment; reposition and TRACK to reacquire. No hidden-location shortcut or mandatory SEARCH TRAIL command.


QUESTION 83 — IDENTICAL OBJECT TARGETING
----------------------------------------

Use classic MUD ordinal targeting syntax. When multiple entities match the same keyword:
- GET ROCK selects the first matching rock.
- GET 2.ROCK selects the second matching rock.
- GET 5.ROCK selects the fifth matching rock.

The ordinal is contextual among current matches and does not expose a database ID.


QUESTION 84 — UNIVERSAL ORDINAL TARGETING
-----------------------------------------

The n.keyword convention applies universally wherever multiple valid targets match, including objects, characters, animals, containers, doors, and similar entities.

Examples include LOOK 2.DEER, ATTACK 3.BANDIT, OPEN 2.CHEST, and GET 2.ROCK. Players do not need to see internal IDs.


QUESTION 85 — GENERATED LOOK PROSE AND BRIEF MODE
-------------------------------------------------

Standard LOOK descriptions use a substantial natural-language prose generator rather than presenting a disguised list of world facts.

The architecture is:
physical simulation/world data -> perception filtering -> prose composition.

The world simulation supplies factual information about terrain, slope, vegetation, hydrology, roads, structures, distant geography, weather, illumination, entities, and other physical features. Perception filters those facts according to SENSE, distance, lighting, weather, concealment, and related conditions. The prose generator then composes the perceived facts into a coherent, substantial description of the place.

The prose should understand relationships among facts. A forest following a riverbank, a road descending toward water, mountains dominating a horizon, or prairie opening into long sightlines should be described as related scenery rather than as independent database fields converted into sentences.

Variation in prose must not create factual variation. Repeated LOOKs should not make the player wonder whether the physical scene changed merely because wording changed.

A player preference, BRIEF, provides a concise factual presentation for players who do not want substantial/flowery prose. BRIEF changes presentation only; it does not alter perception or give/remove information the character is entitled to perceive.

WORLD-GENERATION/CACHING ARCHITECTURE DISCUSSED DURING Q85:

LOOK does not generate the world itself. The deterministic world generator defines physical facts; both the graphical map viewer and the MUD's LOOK system are different consumers of that same underlying world.

The intended architecture is hybrid and chunk-based:
- seed + deterministic generation algorithms define the reproducible base world;
- geographic chunks are generated/evaluated on demand and cached rather than regenerating the same area for every LOOK or every passing player;
- active chunks may remain hot in RAM while players are nearby;
- unused cached procedural data may eventually be evicted from RAM and may also expire from longer-lived caches because it can be deterministically reconstructed;
- persistent world changes never expire merely because a procedural cache does.

Thus fifty players passing the same generated tree should consume the same cached world information rather than independently regenerating that tree fifty times. If a player cuts the tree down, that modification is persistent authoritative state. If the disposable base cache later expires, the server can reconstruct the deterministic base chunk and then apply persistent modifications and current conditions.

Persistent changes include player construction, roads, names where appropriate, stored/cached possessions, harvested or removed features where persistence is required, and other player/world history that cannot be recreated solely from the original seed. C02 now settles FLUSH animals: habitat-eligible temporary creation regulated by per-character Work Timer, without simulated population depletion, with removal of expiry when bleeding will be fatal and ordinary corpse persistence on death. Other resource depletion/regrowth and long-term simulation retain their later-design status.

Exact chunk size, cache lifetimes, storage strategy, and geographic resolution are implementation decisions to benchmark after the physical world generator is substantially complete; they are not locked here.



PHASE VII — INVENTORY, CONTAINERS, EQUIPMENT, AND LOCKS — QUESTIONS 86–148
==========================================================================

QUESTION 86 — CONTAINER-BASED PHYSICAL INVENTORY
-------------------------------------------------
Inventory is physical and container-oriented rather than an unlimited abstract list. A character may hold objects in left/right hands, wear equipment at configured wear locations, and place objects in appropriate containers such as backpacks, pouches, sheaths, and similar objects.

QUESTION 87 — FULL HANDS
------------------------
If both hands are occupied and an action requires a free hand, the action fails and the player is told their hands are full. The game does not automatically stow, sheath, drop, or rearrange possessions.

QUESTION 88 — TWO-HANDED WIELDING
---------------------------------
A weapon/object specifically wielded two-handed occupies both hands.

QUESTION 89 — HAND SLOTS, GRIP, AND SWITCH
-------------------------------------------
Use three logical hand slots: LEFT-HAND, RIGHT-HAND, and BOTH-HANDS. GRIP changes an appropriate wielded weapon between one- and two-handed use when the necessary hand is available. SWITCH moves a one-handed held/wielded item from left to right or right to left. There is no dominant hand; both hands are mechanically equivalent. Weapons may have different one-hand/two-hand wielding modifiers.

QUESTION 90 — INTELLIGENT RETRIEVAL; GET, DRAW, AND WIELD
----------------------------------------------------------
The parser intelligently locates an accessible, unambiguous requested item among possessions. GET retrieves the object and HOLDs it. DRAW retrieves it and WIELDs it, ready for combat. WIELD changes a held object to an obviously combat-ready/threatening state. UNWIELD or HOLD changes it back to ordinary held state without relocating it. Room-level character descriptions append obvious held/wielded objects.

QUESTION 91 — HOLD VERSUS WIELD
-------------------------------
HOLD/WIELD is a state or manner distinction, not inventory movement. A held knife may be non-threatening; a wielded knife is visibly ready for use. UNWIELD and HOLD are equivalent WIELDED-to-HELD transitions.

QUESTION 92 — ANY HOLDABLE OBJECT MAY BE WIELDED
-------------------------------------------------
Any physically holdable object may be WIELDed, even absurd or ineffective improvised weapons. Wielding expresses apparent intent; combat effectiveness comes from the object's actual mechanics.

QUESTION 93 — HELD/WIELDED DISPLAY AND PMOTE
---------------------------------------------
The room-level character description includes held/wielded information. Players may also set a PMOTE (permanent emote), appended to the visible LDESC.

QUESTION 94 — CLEARING PMOTE
----------------------------
Movement automatically clears PMOTE. PMOTE with no argument manually clears it.

QUESTION 95 — PMOTE FORMATTING
------------------------------
Players are responsible for PMOTE capitalization, punctuation, grammar, and wording. The engine does not correct or reformat it.

QUESTION 96 — PMOTE PREVIEW
----------------------------
PMOTE ? displays exactly the rendered room-level character line the player would present to others, except private viewer-specific labels that the character cannot know.

QUESTION 97 — PMOTE IS NOT REALITY-CHECKED
-------------------------------------------
Hand, equipment, wielding, and posture changes do not clear or rewrite PMOTE. Movement clears it, and combat also clears it. Contradictory free-form PMOTE text is the player's responsibility; the engine does not semantically validate it.

QUESTION 98 — CHARACTER DISPLAY COMPOSITION
--------------------------------------------
Conceptual display order is: LDESC -> held/wielded information -> viewer's private label -> PMOTE -> system-generated visible states.

QUESTION 99 — LABELS ARE COMPLETELY PRIVATE
--------------------------------------------
A player cannot label themself. A character must never learn what labels other players assigned to them. No command may leak label text, existence, or creator.

QUESTION 100 — PRIVATE LABELS AS PARSER IDENTIFIERS
----------------------------------------------------
A private label may be used by its creator as a parser identifier, e.g. LOOK BARTENDER, GIVE COIN TO BARTENDER, or WATCH TOAST LUNATIC. Use does not expose the label to its subject. Do not overengineer rare duplicate-description collisions unless play demonstrates a need.

QUESTION 101 — PMOTE LENGTH
---------------------------
PMOTE maximum length is 60 characters for now; this may be tuned later.

QUESTION 102 — PMOTE IS VISIBLE ON LOOK
----------------------------------------
PMOTE is persistent current visible state and appears whenever the character's room-level description is rendered, including later LOOKs.

QUESTION 103 — PMOTE CHANGES ARE SILENT
----------------------------------------
Setting, changing, or clearing PMOTE sends no automatic message to others. Players should EMOTE a visible transition if desired. EMOTE means something happens now; PMOTE means an ongoing visible presentation.

QUESTION 104 — COMBAT CLEARS PMOTE
-----------------------------------
Entering combat clears PMOTE and adds the authoritative system state (fighting). The old PMOTE does not return after combat.

QUESTION 105 — SIMPLE FIGHTING FLAG
------------------------------------
Display only (fighting), not the opponent's identity, avoiding unnecessary churn in multi-character fights.

QUESTION 106 — SYSTEM-GENERATED VISIBLE STATES
-----------------------------------------------
Characters may have multiple authoritative visible states such as (fighting), (bleeding), (injured), (unconscious), or (sitting at a blue table). At most four system-generated states are shown in the room-level description.

QUESTION 107 — UNIQUE STATE PRIORITIES
---------------------------------------
Every visible state has a unique priority. If more than four are active, display the four highest-priority states. The actual priority table is deferred.

QUESTION 108 — STATE LIMIT IS INDEPENDENT
------------------------------------------
The four-state maximum applies only to system-generated state flags. Held/wielded information, private labels, and PMOTE do not count toward it.

QUESTION 109 — STATES ARE DISPLAY-ONLY
---------------------------------------
System state flags are not parser identifiers or aliases.

QUESTION 110 — DO NOT OVERENGINEER STATE TEXT
----------------------------------------------
Do not build elaborate dynamic-reference machinery merely to eliminate rare cosmetic stale/mismatched state descriptions. Fix such edge cases later only if actual play shows they matter.

QUESTION 111 — ALL ACTIVE STATES IN SCORE
------------------------------------------
SCORE shows all active states, including low-priority states hidden from the four-state public display. No separate STATUS command is required.

QUESTION 112 — SCORE IS COMPREHENSIVE
--------------------------------------
SCORE is the comprehensive personal/mechanical character dashboard: descriptions, CAPs, wounds, conditions, active states, general statistics, and similar character information. It never reveals other players' private labels.

QUESTION 113 — SCORE, INVENTORY, LOOK ME, AND PMOTE ?
-----------------------------------------------------
Inventory is not part of SCORE. INVENTORY reports possessions/carrying. LOOK ME shows the external appearance others can see, including visually apparent worn/carried equipment. PMOTE ? previews the current room-level presentation. LOOK ME cannot reveal other players' private labels.

QUESTION 114 — LAYERED WEAR LOCATIONS
--------------------------------------
Use wear locations with ordered layers rather than pseudo-locations. A location such as TORSO may define UNDER, ON, and OVER. Higher layers can conceal lower layers. LOOK/LOOK ME show visually exposed equipment rather than every mechanically worn item.

QUESTION 115 — CONFIGURABLE LAYER CAPACITY
-------------------------------------------
Multiple items may occupy a wear location/layer. Default capacity is three, but individual layers may be configured lower, including one for armor or other mutually exclusive equipment.

QUESTION 116 — VISIBLE WORN ITEMS AND WEAR ORDER
-------------------------------------------------
Do not model partial occlusion among same-layer garments. Display all items considered visible, in the order they were worn.

QUESTION 117 — STRICT REMOVAL ORDER (FINAL REVISION)
-----------------------------------------------------
Supersedes the earlier permissive idea. Higher layers must be removed before lower layers. Within one location/layer, removal is reverse wear order (last worn, first removed). A character cannot remove underwear beneath jeans/snow pants without removing the outer garments first.

QUESTION 118 — WEAR REQUIRES HOLDING
-------------------------------------
The character must be HOLDING an item to WEAR it. WEAR does not auto-retrieve from a container.

QUESTION 119 — REMOVE DELIVERS TO A HAND
-----------------------------------------
REMOVE places the item in a free hand. If no hand is free, removal cannot complete. Do not auto-stow or drop.

QUESTION 120 — DRESSING RESPECTS OUTER LAYERS
----------------------------------------------
Lower layers may be empty, but existing higher layers obstruct adding garments beneath them. A character may wear snow pants with nothing underneath, but must remove them before later adding underwear.

QUESTION 121 — STRICT WEAR-STACK ENFORCEMENT
---------------------------------------------
Layers have defined order and capacity. New same-layer items sit atop earlier ones; same-layer removal is LIFO; higher layers must clear before lower layers for WEAR/REMOVE; WEAR requires holding; REMOVE requires a free hand.

QUESTION 122 — WEAR LOCATIONS ARE INDIVIDUALLY CONFIGURED
----------------------------------------------------------
Each wear location defines which layers exist, their physical order, and each layer's capacity. Not every location uses UNDER/ON/OVER.

QUESTION 123 — ITEMS DEFINE PERMITTED WEAR LOCATION/LAYER
---------------------------------------------------------
Every wearable object explicitly defines where/layer it may be worn. Model the meaningful equipment location, not full garment geometry; e.g. an overcoat can simply be TORSO:OVER even if it hangs over the legs.

QUESTION 124 — MULTIPLE PERMITTED WEAR LOCATIONS
-------------------------------------------------
An object may permit multiple location/layer combinations. If only one is valid, WEAR can resolve it automatically; if several are valid, the player may specify the location.

QUESTION 125 — COVERED CONTAINERS REMAIN OWNER-ACCESSIBLE
----------------------------------------------------------
Outer garments may conceal a worn container and make theft harder but do not block its wearer from accessing its contents. Strict WEAR/REMOVE order still applies.

QUESTION 126 — CONTAINER CLOSURE
---------------------------------
Containers support OPEN/CLOSED or ALWAYS OPEN behavior and configurable descriptive closure terminology such as flap, lid, top, or cover.

QUESTION 127 — OPEN/CLOSE TARGET THE CONTAINER
-----------------------------------------------
Commands target the container (OPEN BACKPACK), not the descriptive closure part (OPEN FLAP). Closure terminology exists for prose.

QUESTION 128 — CLOSED CONTAINERS CONCEAL CONTENTS
--------------------------------------------------
A closed container's contents cannot be seen. Examination may describe the container/closed state but not its contents. Open or always-open containers may expose contents as appropriate.

QUESTION 129 — GET MAY TRANSPARENTLY OPEN AND RESTORE
------------------------------------------------------
If GET's accessible target is inside a closed owned/carried container, the engine may transparently open it, retrieve the object, and close it again. If found open, leave it open.

QUESTION 130 — PUT USES THE SAME FOUND-STATE RULE
--------------------------------------------------
PUT into an accessible closed container may transparently open it, place the object, and close it again. If found open, leave it open. General rule: restore temporary container manipulation to the state in which it was found.

QUESTION 131 — LOCKS ON CONTAINERS, DOORS, AND WINDOWS
-------------------------------------------------------
STATUS NOTE FOR Q131-Q145: physical property-door keys, master keys, key retrieval and picking proposals are OBSOLETE wherever they conflict with Q154/C09's identity-based doors. Keep the older record below as history; do not implement a parallel property-door key system. Mapping the earlier container/window/picking details to keyless access was not separately resolved. General containment and physical-access rules remain valid.
Containers/objects require an appropriate key to LOCK/UNLOCK normally. Doors/windows may normally be locked/unlocked from the INSIDE without a key unless configured KEY ONLY. KEY ONLY requires a key even inside. Automatic GET/PUT never bypasses a locked container.

QUESTION 132 — LOCK IDS, MASTER KEYS, AND BARS
-----------------------------------------------
Each KEY has exactly one lock ID. A lockable door/chest/object may accept up to twelve lock IDs. This naturally supports room, floor-master, and building-master keys. BARRED is independent of LOCKED; a key or successful lockpick does not defeat a bar.

QUESTION 133 — BAR SIDE FOLLOWS DOOR SWING
-------------------------------------------
A door may be barred only on the side toward which it swings. Door swing side/direction is therefore meaningful configuration and determines where BAR/UNBAR is possible.

QUESTION 134 — DOORS ARE UNBREAKABLE FOR NOW
----------------------------------------------
Do not currently allow forcing/breaking doors. This deliberately protects persistent/offline player storage and bases. Structural damage may be revisited later.

QUESTION 135 — LOCK PICKABILITY 0–20
-------------------------------------
Every lock has pickability 0–20. 0 is trivial/child-level; 1–19 progressively harder; 20 is absolutely unpickable under any circumstances. Exact CAP/tool/roll mechanics are deferred.

QUESTION 136 — EVERY PICK ATTEMPT LEAVES EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------
Every lockpicking attempt, success or failure, leaves marks/evidence and carries a chance of breaking picks. Picking does not necessarily make the lock unusable. Exact break chance is deferred.

QUESTION 137 — PICKING EVIDENCE ACCUMULATES
--------------------------------------------
Picking damage/evidence accumulates over repeated attempts and can be repaired. Exact scale and repair details beyond the following rule are deferred.

QUESTION 138 — EXAMINING AND REPAIRING PICK DAMAGE
---------------------------------------------------
A picker can examine the lock afterward and see the physical evidence; no abstract numeric feedback is needed during the attempt. Repairing picking damage requires both a proper key accepted by the lock (including a valid master key) and a simple locksmith kit. A thief who merely picked the lock cannot erase evidence without legitimate key access.

QUESTION 139 — PICKING CAN LOCK OR UNLOCK; REPAIR DOES NOT CHANGE STATE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Repairing evidence does not change locked/unlocked state. Lockpicking can manipulate a lock in either direction, so a burglar may pick a lock open and later pick it closed again; evidence remains unless legitimately repaired.

QUESTION 140 — PICK IS A TOGGLE
--------------------------------
PICK <target> attempts to toggle LOCKED->UNLOCKED or UNLOCKED->LOCKED. Do not warn a player that a lock is already unlocked; a thief may accidentally pick an unlocked door into the locked state. Attempts still leave evidence and may break picks.

QUESTION 141 — LOOK DOES NOT ADVERTISE LOCK STATE
-------------------------------------------------
Ordinary LOOK does not reveal whether a closed door/window/chest is locked. State is learned through interaction.

QUESTION 142 — OPEN/CLOSE REVEAL ONLY ACTION-RELEVANT INFORMATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
OPEN on an open object reports already open. CLOSE on a closed object reports already closed and does not reveal lock state. OPEN on a closed locked object may report that it is locked because the lock blocks the requested action. OPEN/CLOSE are not free diagnostic lock-state probes.

QUESTION 143 — AUTOMATIC KEY SELECTION
---------------------------------------
LOCK/UNLOCK may automatically find an appropriate key on the character's person. A free hand is required. The engine temporarily retrieves/uses the key and returns it exactly where found. It never rearranges occupied hands to make room.

QUESTION 144 — TRANSPARENT KEY RETRIEVAL
-----------------------------------------
Matching keys may be transparently retrieved through accessible nested containers: open as necessary, retrieve key, operate lock, return key, and restore every container's found state. Locked containers stop traversal.

QUESTION 145 — KEY RINGS
-------------------------
Key rings are ordinary physical containers specialized for keys. They are not magical key inventories. Keys on a worn/carried key ring count as physically on the character and participate in transparent key lookup.

QUESTION 146 — RECURSIVE SEARCH AND MAXIMUM NESTING DEPTH
----------------------------------------------------------
Transparent inventory searches recursively traverse accessible nested containers, for keys and for ordinary intelligent retrieval such as GET. Container nesting is strictly limited to four container levels. Example: character -> backpack (1) -> bag (2) -> pouch (3) -> key ring (4) -> key is legal because the final key is not another container level. A fifth nested container is prohibited. Closed accessible containers may be temporarily opened/restored; locked containers stop traversal.

QUESTION 147 — NO CIRCULAR CONTAINMENT
---------------------------------------
A container may never contain itself directly or indirectly. Before moving a container into another, the engine must reject any operation that would make the object one of its own ancestors. This prevents containment loops and, in Thomas's words, keeps the universe from imploding.

QUESTION 148 — FULL RECURSIVE WEIGHT AND CAPACITY
--------------------------------------------------
Character encumbrance includes the full recursive weight of containers and everything within them. Container capacity likewise includes nested containers and all recursively contained contents. Nesting cannot be used to make mass disappear or bypass carrying limits.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES REINFORCED BY QUESTIONS 86–148
--------------------------------------------------
- Model a physical world without turning routine, unambiguous handling into pointless busywork.
- Hands and meaningful physical placement matter; the engine does not silently rearrange possessions for the player.
- Transparent convenience is acceptable for mundane operations such as temporarily opening a carried container or retrieving the correct key, provided found state is restored.
- Player-authored prose is the player's responsibility. The engine supplies authoritative mechanical state but does not attempt semantic policing of PMOTE.
- Prefer simple deterministic rules over elaborate handling of rare cosmetic edge cases.
- System-generated state is authoritative; PMOTE may be wrong because it is free-form player roleplay.
- Protect persistent player investment: for now doors cannot be smashed through, and bars remain independent of locks/picking.
- Inventory recursion is bounded and safe: maximum four nested container levels, no circular containment, and full recursive weight.



PROPERTY AFTER DEATH, SUCCESSION, AND DESCRIPTIONS — QUESTIONS 149–162
======================================================================

These entries record the finalized decisions from the Property After Death interview. Later revisions below govern over superseded proposals. The original discussion queue is preserved for reference; its historical numbering is not the active interview numbering.

QUESTION 149 — PROPERTY AND POSSESSIONS AFTER PC DEATH
------------------------------------------------------

At death, everything physically carried by the PC drops immediately at the exact death coordinates. The character's body immediately disappears from the playable world and transfers to an admin-only death area. No PC corpse remains in the playable world.

From the death area, the player may remain dead, re-enter naked with the established -10,000 consequence (the Naked 10000 return described earlier), or petition for administrative review. If review finds the character blameless in the death, an administrator may permit a return at Origin. This records the current death-area choices without inventing a new numerical penalty.

Non-carried possessions remain physically where they were. Death does not teleport or erase stored property.
- Registered property follows the inheritance, eviction, and eventual sale rules finalized in Questions 154–159. The early suggestion of a Land Office sale was refined to a sale directly at the property's front door.
- A rented apartment eventually reverts to its landlord, along with everything left inside. No exact apartment-reversion interval was settled here.
- An unregistered cabin or other structure remains in the world and may become part of the landscape. After 90 in-game days without the owner entering, another character may lay claim to it.
- The original owner may contest the claim. If it goes unanswered/uncontested, the claimant retrieves the claim paperwork from the structure, gains entry/access, and becomes its owner. No additional contest-response duration was fixed in this discussion.
- The 90-day absence rule can apply to a living absent owner as well as a dead one; it is an abandonment rule.


QUESTION 150 — IMMEDIATE LOOTING; NO PC CORPSES
-----------------------------------------------

All dropped death gear immediately becomes ordinary lootable property: finder's keepers. There is no protection timer, corpse ownership, special recovery period, or reserved right for the returning character. Anyone who finds it may take it.

PC corpses never exist in the playable world. The items remain at the death coordinates; the character does not.


QUESTION 151 — CARRIED MONEY, DOCUMENTS, AND BANK INHERITANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------

Everything physically carried drops, including money, bank notes, deeds, documents, maps, worn equipment, held equipment, and carried containers and their contents. Financial or legal importance grants no special protection.

Deposited bank funds are separate from carried cash and bank notes. After 90 in-game days, deposits transfer to the named surviving heir's account. If there is no valid surviving heir, including when both account holder and heir are dead, the funds revert to the bank.

A broader heir system for homes, barns, cabins, land, and other property was flagged for further design here. Questions 154–156 subsequently settle the registered-property door/succession mechanism; this does not replace the bank's separate 90-day rule.


QUESTION 152 — DEEDS CONFER OWNERSHIP; SECURE DOCUMENT STORAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------

Possession of the property deed confers ownership of the property it represents. A person who acquires the deed by finding, looting, stealing, buying, or otherwise obtaining it acquires the property; the former holder is not protected merely by an older ownership record.

Owners should secure deeds rather than routinely carry them into danger. A safe at home is an option. Banks offer purchasable lockboxes for storing deeds and other valuables.


QUESTION 153 — REMOTE OWNERSHIP AND DOOR CONTROL — REVISED BY Q154
------------------------------------------------------------------

Remote property control must be possible at the property itself, without requiring a trip of potentially thousands of miles to the Land Office. A magical mechanism at the door is accepted for this purpose.

The initial proposal was to present a deed/claim to the locked door and pay a fee to receive an owner's key. That physical owner's-key proposal was superseded during Question 154 by identity-based magical doors. It is retained here only as interview history, not as a parallel key system. No key-issuance fee was finalized for the replacement system.


QUESTION 154 — KEYLESS MAGICAL DOORS, ACCESS, AND SUCCESSION
------------------------------------------------------------

Each constructed property door is registered to its property and numbered within it, such as Door 1 for the primary entrance and Doors 2–5 for other doors. The property owner controls all its registered doors.

There are no physical keys in this door/access system. Magical door permissions recognize named characters and ownership. This supersedes the earlier physical door-key, master-key, key-retrieval, and owner's-key proposals wherever they conflict with this system; the earlier interview record remains preserved as history. This discussion does not separately settle how every earlier container/window lock or lockpicking detail maps to magical access.

The owner may configure each door as lockable or not and grant or revoke named users' access to individual doors. Authorized users may open, close, lock, and unlock that door. They can admit other people by opening/unlocking it; admission alone does not grant permanent named access. Door-specific ban lists may prevent named individuals from entering, with bans managed at the relevant door.

The primary entrance may hold an ordered list of potential heirs. After the owner's death, an eligible heir using that entrance is offered the option to claim ownership. Acceptance registers the property to that heir and transfers control of all its doors. Existing access lists remain intact until the new owner changes them. The new owner may reconfigure doors and grant or revoke permissions individually.


QUESTION 155 — SUCCESSION ELIGIBILITY WIDENS BY TIMESTAMP
---------------------------------------------------------

Succession is passive and based on elapsed in-game time since the owner's death. The first heir is eligible first; each additional heir joins the eligible pool after another 30 in-game days.

For four heirs:
- Heir 1 is eligible initially.
- At 30 days, heir 2 also becomes eligible.
- At 60 days, heir 3 also becomes eligible.
- At 90 days, heir 4 also becomes eligible.

Earlier eligible heirs remain eligible as later heirs join. There is no formal refusal or permanent surrender of priority: an heir may simply leave the offer unaccepted and claim later while inheritance remains available and nobody else has claimed. Priority provides an initial exclusive opportunity, not an indefinite veto over later heirs. Question 156 governs eventual eviction and sale if inheritance remains unclaimed.

Each time a listed user uses the door/lock while ownership is unresolved, the door notifies them that the owner is dead and ownership is being resolved. An eligible heir is told they may claim now; a not-yet-eligible heir is told how many in-game days remain. Access alone does not make a person an heir.

Calculate eligibility and remaining time from stored timestamps when needed; do not require daily maintenance processing or a sequential accept/refuse workflow.


QUESTION 156 — UNCLAIMED INHERITANCE, EVICTION, AND FRONT-DOOR SALE
-------------------------------------------------------------------

After the final named heir has had 30 in-game days to claim and the property remains unclaimed, begin a 30-in-game-day eviction notice for those with access. This gives them time to remove their belongings.

When the eviction notice expires:
- Remove the old access permissions.
- Move the stored login/logout location of anyone logged out inside to immediately outside the main entrance. They cannot regain entry through the removed permissions.
- Anything left inside is forfeited and remains with the property.
- Put the property up for sale directly at its front door.

A buyer who has the cash may pay the asking price there and immediately become the registered owner, with authority over the property's doors. No Land Office trip is required.

The lifecycle is: owner dies -> heir eligibility widens -> final heir receives 30 days -> 30-day eviction notice -> access removal and forfeiture -> sale at the front door. The discussion did not separately specify the timing for an empty heir list.


QUESTION 157 — REGISTERED STRUCTURE BASE VALUE AND INITIAL SALE PRICE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Each registered structure receives a base value by type when created. Illustrative values discussed were cabin 1,000, house 10,000, and barn 7,000; these examples establish the valuation approach rather than an exhaustive price table.

When abandoned registered property enters sale, its initial asking price is 200% of its base value. A house with a base value of 10,000 therefore starts at 20,000.

Store the sale-start timestamp so the current asking price can be calculated when someone examines or purchases the property. Question 158 finalizes the depreciation method and floor.


QUESTION 158 — COMPOUNDED PRICE DECLINE AND PERMANENT FLOOR
-----------------------------------------------------------

The asking price decreases by 1% of its CURRENT asking price per elapsed in-game day, compounded. It is not a fixed daily subtraction based on the original amount.

Initial asking price = 2 × base value.
Current asking price = max(initial asking price × 0.10, initial asking price × 0.99^(elapsed in-game days for sale)).

The floor is 10% of the INITIAL ASKING PRICE, not 10% of the structure's base value. A base-10,000 house begins at 20,000, is approximately 7,321 after 100 in-game days, and eventually reaches a floor of 2,000. It stays at that floor forever until purchased.

Compute the compounded price from the sale-start timestamp on demand. No daily price-update job is required. Currency rounding was not specified here.


QUESTION 159 — FORFEITED CONTENTS CONVEY AS-IS; VIRTUAL TOUR
------------------------------------------------------------

Everything physically remaining after eviction conveys with the property as-is. This includes furniture, containers, stored goods, equipment, valuables, junk, and other possessions left on the premises. Contents are not generated or guaranteed for a purchaser; the buyer receives whatever actually remains.

Prospective purchasers may take a virtual tour before buying. The initial suggestion of temporarily moving a buyer inside as a non-interacting ghost was superseded by Question 160's remote room-echo design.


QUESTION 160 — VIRTUAL TOUR AS READ-ONLY REMOTE ROOM ECHOES
-----------------------------------------------------------

The buyer remains physically outside the main entrance throughout the tour. The tour is a read-only remote LOOK/echo of rooms, not character movement and not a temporary ghost state.

Show the room itself: structural description, dimensions where available, layout, exits, doors, windows, and other permanent structural features. Do not show occupants, animals, loose objects, containers, stored goods, nonstructural furniture, or other room contents. No container inspection or contents preview is provided, even though forfeited contents will convey with the sale.

The buyer may traverse the property's room layout through those echoes without entering, touching, moving, taking, depositing, or otherwise interacting with anything inside. The property must identify its constituent rooms and their connections so the tour can traverse the correct spaces.


QUESTION 161 — OWNER-WRITTEN ROOM DESCRIPTIONS AND STAFF REVIEW
---------------------------------------------------------------

Owners may freely customize interior room names/descriptions, subject to staff review. Creative prose changes presentation only; it cannot create exits, stronger doors, materials, dimensions, or other mechanical features that do not actually exist.

A submission does not become the approved/current room description before approval. Staff review has three outcomes:
- Approval makes the submitted description current immediately.
- Soft rejection notifies the player of the problem and the need to correct it, and reverts the submission one level/to its previous state. It carries no disciplinary strike. Typos and honest errors are representative reasons.
- Hard rejection reverts to the previous description and records a hard rejection on the player's moderation history. Deliberate abuse or misrepresentation may justify this outcome.

Submissions and moderation actions are written to a permanent vboard record, preserving the submission, decision, and staff context. The initial suggestion of a ban at three hard rejections was corrected by Question 162; use the finalized rolling-year threshold below.


QUESTION 162 — CREATIVE-DESCRIPTION ELIGIBILITY: FOUR HARD REJECTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Count hard rejections during the rolling previous year. A player with zero, one, two, or three hard rejections in that window may still submit creative description changes. Reaching four blocks further creative-description submissions.

The restriction ends when enough hard rejections have aged beyond one year to bring the rolling count back to three or fewer. For a player with exactly four, the oldest relevant rejection aging out restores eligibility. This is not a fixed one-year ban starting with the fourth rejection and is not a lifetime ban.

Soft rejections never count toward the threshold. Aging out of the eligibility window does not erase the permanent moderation/vboard history. Timestamp comparisons can determine current eligibility without maintenance processing. The discussion called this a rolling year without separately defining a real-world versus in-game year.


QUESTION 163 — EXTERIOR PROPERTY DESCRIPTIONS AND STAFF REVIEW
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Property owners may customize exterior descriptions using the same moderated creative-description system as interiors in Questions 161–162. Approval, soft rejection, hard rejection, permanent moderation/vboard history, and the four-hard-rejections-within-a-rolling-year eligibility rule all apply.

At submission/review, the prose must describe the property's actual physical state, dimensions, materials, features, condition, and surroundings; it does not create nonexistent mechanical features. Questions 164–165 settle how approved prose replaces generated text and persists after later physical changes.


QUESTION 164 — APPROVED EXTERIOR PROSE REPLACES GENERATED DESCRIPTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

An approved custom exterior description completely replaces the procedurally generated exterior description when the property is viewed. It is not combined with the generated description. The player has control of the approved prose, subject to staff approval and the existing four-strike eligibility rule.

This presentation policy may be revisited later if necessary; no further generator-imposed prose constraints were adopted here.


QUESTION 165 — CUSTOM DESCRIPTION PERSISTENCE AND ABANDONMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------

A custom property description remains despite subsequent damage, deterioration, alterations, or other physical changes. Such changes do not automatically invalidate or revert the approved description.

When the property is abandoned, its custom description is removed and it reverts to a generated description. Normal abandonment and overgrowth can then continue. This persistence rule refines Question 163's review-time consistency requirement.


QUESTION 166 — NO PLAYER-CREATED CUSTOM PROPERTY NAMES
---------------------------------------------------------

Properties may not receive player-created custom property names. Approved interior/exterior prose remains available, but custom property naming is not part of the system. This decision concerns the property's name; Question 161's existing interior room customization remains recorded above.


QUESTION 167 — PHYSICAL SIGNS AND THEFT
------------------------------------------

Players may create, write on, and place physical signs on their property. A sign is an ordinary world object, not protected property-name metadata.

Signs may be read, moved, damaged, destroyed, or stolen under normal object and access rules. If someone can reach a sign and the ordinary rules allow taking it, they may steal it.


QUESTION 168 — PERMANENT SIGN WRITING AND LITERACY
-----------------------------------------------------

Writing on a sign is permanent; the owner cannot simply edit its text through a command. Changing a message requires an appropriate physical alteration or replacement rather than a magical text edit. The detailed physical rewriting mechanism was not designed here.

Writing a sign requires the character to be able to read and write the relevant language. Questions 169–174 define Academics as the source of these abilities, with separate per-language unlocks.


QUESTION 169 — ACADEMICS GOVERNS LITERACY — REFINED BY Q171–Q174
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Literacy is a character capability governed by the Academics Cap (ACADX), not something every character automatically possesses and not a separate Literacy skill.

The initial answer proposed one threshold for reading and another for writing, with levels deferred. That universal-threshold formulation is superseded by the later separate speaking, reading, and writing unlocks for each language in Question 174. Retain the Academics foundation, not a competing universal literacy threshold.


QUESTION 170 — UNREADABLE TEXT STILL APPEARS AS WRITING
----------------------------------------------------------

A character unable to read a sign, letter, book, or other written object can still perceive that writing exists, but cannot see its contents. The description may indicate writing, symbols, or carving without revealing the words.

OBSOLETE: Q176's original denial of written-language recognition. Q219-Q220 supersede it: the language may be recognizable without its contents being readable. READ remains necessary for comprehension.


QUESTION 171 — AUTOMATIC LANGUAGE ABILITIES FROM ACADEMICS
-------------------------------------------------------------

Language abilities are assigned automatically by Academics level, not selected by the player. Common is available for speech at ACADX 0. Knowing how to speak a language does not automatically grant the ability to read or write it.

The initial illustrative language sequence was Common at 0, English at 3, French at 5, Italian at 7, German at 9, and World Speak at 11. This was provisional, not a finalized progression. Question 174 subsequently refines the model to separate per-language speak/read/write unlocks and supplies newer provisional examples. The complete language list and thresholds remain deferred.


QUESTION 172 — SPOKEN-LANGUAGE RECOGNITION UP TO FIVE LEVELS ABOVE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

A character may identify a spoken language whose required Academics level is up to five levels above their own ACADX without understanding its content. A language beyond that recognition range remains unidentified.

For illustration, an ACADX 4 character could recognize a spoken language requiring level 9, but not one requiring level 10. These numbers illustrate the recognition rule, not a finalized language progression. OBSOLETE scope limitation: Q176 formerly limited recognition to speech. Q219-Q220 now reuse the same recognition rules for writing, without granting comprehension.


QUESTION 173 — SPEAK SETS THE ACTIVE LANGUAGE FOR SPEECH AND WRITING
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SPEAK <language> sets one active language used for both speech and writing. Examples include SPEAK COMMON and SPEAK ENGLISH. There is no separate writing-language state or language-selection command.

A character selects a known language; actually speaking or writing still requires the corresponding per-language ability under Question 174. To write in one language while speaking in another, switch the active language as needed and switch back before continuing to write.


QUESTION 174 — FULL PER-LANGUAGE SPEAK, READ, AND WRITE UNLOCKS
------------------------------------------------------------------

There is no partial language proficiency, simulated gibberish, or gradual comprehension mechanic. At the required Academics threshold, the character fully acquires that particular ability.

Speaking, reading, and writing each language have separate automatic Academics unlocks. Acquiring one does not confer the others. This supersedes Question 169's initial universal reading/writing thresholds and refines Question 171's initial language progression.

Provisional examples:
- ACADX 0: speak Common.
- ACADX 1: read Common.
- ACADX 3: speak French.
- ACADX 6: write Common.

The conversation also repeated "read Common" at level 4 in its rough example. That duplicate does not establish a second Common-reading unlock or a different unconfirmed language ability. The final language list and progression remain to be designed later.

The +5 spoken-language recognition rule from Question 172 remains in effect.


QUESTION 175 — UNKNOWN SPEECH HIDES WORDS BUT PRESERVES EMOTES
-----------------------------------------------------------------

When a listener recognizes a spoken language but cannot understand it, show a message such as "Jacques says something in French." When the language is not recognized, show a message such as "Jacques says something in a language you don't recognize."

Do not reveal the actual words or substitute fake foreign-language gibberish. The communication's visible emote/action still fires normally: pointing, gestures, and other observable behavior remain visible even when the spoken content is unavailable.


QUESTION 176 — UNKNOWN WRITING, VISIBLE ART, AND ART MODERATION
------------------------------------------------------------------

If a character cannot read a written language, never expose its contents. The earlier "unknown language only / no written-language recognition" rule is OBSOLETE under Q219-Q220: use established recognition rules to identify a recognizable language without translating it. No handwriting/calligraphy skill or special beautiful/fancy-writing ability was introduced.

Written objects may include drawings or other visual material enclosed in <art>...</art> tags. Art is displayed to everyone regardless of literacy or language ability.

Every use of an art tag is automatically reported to a staff news board for review. Staff may block a player's art-tag use for a specified number of days. Store an expiration timestamp and prevent new art-tag use until it passes. While restricted, tell the player when access returns and the remaining restriction information. Repeated abuse may result in a permanent art-tag restriction.

This restriction targets use of the art mechanism. Questions 177–178 settle the treatment of existing art and permanent creator attribution.


QUESTION 177 — ART RESTRICTIONS AFFECT NEW CREATION; STAFF HISTORY REVIEW
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

An art restriction prevents new art creation only. Existing artwork remains because it should already have been reviewed; blocking its creator does not automatically erase it.

Staff can retrieve and review all artwork created by a particular player. Individual offending pieces may be handled separately from the restriction on future art creation.


QUESTION 178 — PERMANENT STAFF-ONLY ART CREATOR ATTRIBUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------

Every artwork permanently records both its originating account/player and the character that created it as staff-only metadata.

That attribution follows the artwork through theft, sale, transfers, changes of ownership or location, hiding, and abandonment. Ordinary players do not automatically see this metadata. Staff must remain able to trace artwork to its originating player and character.


QUESTION 179 — ART COPYING THROUGH RESTRICTED DOCUMENT COPY COMMANDS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Artwork is copied only as part of the future COPY PARCHMENT / COPY BOOK system; there is no separate artwork-copying command.

These commands are deliberately restricted to high-Academics, sage-like characters. Reliable duplication of persistent written material is important enough to restrict mechanically even if low-level manual copying might seem technically possible. The exact Academics requirement and copying mechanics remain deferred.

Copies preserve the original author/creator metadata and add the copier and date, such as "copied by Thomas" on the recorded date. Question 180 extends this to the full provenance chain.


QUESTION 180 — FULL COPY PROVENANCE AND A VISIBLE COPIER NOTE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Copies of copies preserve the complete relevant provenance chain in metadata: the original author/creator, each copier, copying dates, and other relevant copying information. Do not reduce the record to only the original author and most recent copier. Original artwork attribution remains preserved under Question 178.

The copier may also insert a visible metadata tag/note at the beginning of the copied book or paper. This note is part of the readable document, separate from the hidden staff metadata and provenance record.

At the end of Question 180, whether this visible note would be free-form or standardized remained unanswered. Question 181 below now resolves it.


QUESTION 181 — COPIER’S VISIBLE NOTE
------------------------------------

A copy made by anyone other than the original author must contain a standardized, unavoidable visible notation at the beginning indicating that the document is a copy, not an original. That mandatory notation does not display the copier’s identity. The copier may additionally add an optional free-form introductory note. If the original author produces another copy of their own work, it may be treated as another original and requires no visible copy notation. There is no limit imposed here on how many originals an author may produce.


QUESTION 182 — WHAT COUNTS AS THE “ORIGINAL AUTHOR”?
----------------------------------------------------

The no-copy-notation privilege applies only to a single-author work reproduced by that same original author. If a book or document has multiple authors, any reproduction of it is considered a copy, even if one of the original co-authors performs the copying. It receives the mandatory visible copy notation. The metadata still preserves the complete authorship and copying provenance.


QUESTION 183 — COPY OF A COPY
-----------------------------

A copy is made from the actual source document in the copier's possession, not from some abstract pristine master. Copy #2 therefore reproduces Copy #1 exactly, including its mandatory copy notation and any free-form notes previously added by earlier copiers. The current copier may then add their own optional free-form comment. Nothing inherited from the source is silently stripped out.


QUESTION 184 — WHERE DOES EACH NEW COPIER NOTE GO?
--------------------------------------------------

New copier comments are prepended. The newest copier’s optional free-form note appears first, followed by the previously inherited copier notes. Thus, readers see the most recent annotation immediately, while the complete visible history remains intact underneath it.


QUESTION 185 — CAN A COPIER DECLINE TO ADD A PERSONAL NOTE?
-----------------------------------------------------------

The copier’s free-form comment is entirely optional. A copier may add nothing. The mandatory copy indication and all inherited content remain unchanged.


QUESTION 186 — CAN THE COPIER EDIT THE BODY WHILE COPYING?
----------------------------------------------------------

COPY BOOK and COPY PARCHMENT produce an exact copy of the source. The copier cannot alter, correct, delete, rearrange, or add anything within the copied body through the COPY command. The only permitted addition is the optional copier note already established.

If someone wants to create an altered version, they must do so separately—effectively copying/pasting the text into a newly authored document and editing it normally. That new document is not treated as a faithful COPY and does not inherit the COPY command's provenance status.


QUESTION 187 — COPYING <ART> CONTENT
------------------------------------

Authorized COPY BOOK and COPY PARCHMENT reproduce <art>...</art> content exactly, just like ordinary text. No separate artistic skill or capability is required. The copier's high-level copying ability represents sufficient competence to reproduce the entire document faithfully.


QUESTION 188 — CAN AN EXACT COPY FAIL?
--------------------------------------

Once a character has the required copying capability and possesses the necessary book/parchment, ink, and pen, the COPY action does not fail. There is no random copying roll.

A qualified character can also translate while copying. For example, if the source is English and the character is capable of the necessary French language use, they may copy it as a French-language document. The underlying player-visible stored prose may still literally be the English text—the game treats it as French through the document's language metadata. This is a translation, rather than an exact same-language transcription.


QUESTION 189 — TRANSLATION AND COPY STATUS
------------------------------------------

A translated reproduction is visibly distinguished from an ordinary copy. Its mandatory notation will indicate that it was “copied and translated from” the source language/document rather than merely saying it is a copy. The provenance metadata continues to preserve the relevant source and copying/translation history.


QUESTION 190 — TRANSLATOR IDENTIFICATION
----------------------------------------

Translated copies follow the same identity rules as ordinary copies. The mandatory visible notation indicates that the document was copied and translated from another language/source, but does not identify the translator. Full translator identity, date, source, language transition, and other relevant provenance remain in metadata. The translator may add an optional free-form note under the same rules already established.


QUESTION 191 — TRANSLATING THROUGH INTERMEDIATE LANGUAGES
---------------------------------------------------------

Translation never cleans up or collapses the document's visible history. A translation made from a previous translation preserves all inherited historical information, including mandatory copy/translation notices and every copier's optional note. The new translation information is prepended, following the newest-first rule. Full provenance also remains in metadata.

So a document could acquire a long and interesting lineage over generations of scribes and translators.


QUESTION 192 — TRANSLATION REQUIREMENTS
---------------------------------------

Translation requires the character to read the source language and write the destination language. Speaking does not require a separate translation check because the established language progression ensures speaking ability comes before writing ability in that language.

So English → French requires Read English + Write French. This respects the previously established separate per-language Academics unlocks rather than introducing a new translation skill.


QUESTION 193 — TRANSLATING YOUR OWN WORK
----------------------------------------

A sole original author may translate their own work into another language and the resulting document is still considered an original. It receives no mandatory copy or translation notation. As with same-language originals, the author may produce as many original versions as desired.

The metadata should still retain the useful facts about the document and its language, but visibly it is the author's own original work—not a copy.


QUESTION 194 — AUTHOR REVISING THEIR OWN WORK
---------------------------------------------

There is no editing or revising an existing physical document. Written ink cannot be erased, overwritten through an edit command, or retroactively corrected. If an author makes a mistake or wants a changed version, they must begin again using fresh parchment/book materials and create a new document. The existing document remains exactly as written.

That also means “editing” mentioned back in Question 186 should be understood as creating a new document manually from copied/pasted text, not modifying the existing physical object.


QUESTION 195 — ABANDONING A MISTAKE MID-DOCUMENT
------------------------------------------------

Books consist of individual parchment pages. A page may be physically torn out from the beginning, middle, or end. The book's displayed page numbers automatically renumber to remain continuous; removing page 7 from a 21-page book leaves a 20-page book numbered 1–20.

Existing parchments can also be assembled into books with ADD PAGE while the character holds both the book and parchment. ADD PAGE with no position appends the parchment to the end. ADD PAGE 3 inserts it as the new page 3 when that position is valid, shifting subsequent pages forward. The parchment becomes a page of that book rather than having its text recreated.


QUESTION 196 — REMOVING A PAGE INTACT
-------------------------------------

A removed book page remains an intact parchment object, preserving everything written or drawn on it. It can be read independently, carried, transferred, or inserted into another book. Both removing a page and adding a parchment/page to a book require a higher Academics capability. This restriction is deliberately intended to reduce casual vandalism and abuse.

Protection for institutional/library books is explicitly deferred for later design rather than decided now.


QUESTION 197 — PROVENANCE WHEN PAGES MOVE BETWEEN BOOKS
-------------------------------------------------------

DEFERRED in this interview.

Page-level provenance when parchments move between books is deliberately left unresolved for later. We won't let it drag us into designing the document data model right now.

Later Questions 243–245 establish preservation of component-parchment provenance and nested history through publication and whole-book copying. Preserve those later decisions without inventing additional page-movement metadata rules here; remaining provenance visibility/details are deferred under Question 246.


QUESTION 198 — BOOK CAPACITY
----------------------------

Every book has a hard maximum capacity of 100 parchment pages. There are no different book capacities to manage. This is intentionally generous for unusual cases such as long-running journals, collected notes, or player histories.


QUESTION 199 — BLANK PAGES
--------------------------

Books may contain blank parchment pages, so players can assemble journals, ledgers, notebooks, etc. in advance and fill them over time. WRITE BOOK 42 opens the writing editor for page 42 of the held/targeted book. The normal permanent-ink rules apply once writing is committed to that page.


QUESTION 200 — WRITING ON A PARTIALLY USED PAGE
-----------------------------------------------

Existing writing can be appended to, but never altered or erased. Each parchment has a hard limit of 75 characters per line and 25 lines total, and parchment is one-sided only—nothing may be written on the back.

For books, authorship is established by TITLE, not by who physically writes each page. The character who first titles the book becomes its original author, period. Other characters may subsequently write on pages without becoming co-authors. Only that original author may change the book's title.

This also substantially narrows our earlier multiple-author discussion: ordinary collaborative writing inside one book does not create multiple authors merely because several people contributed text.


QUESTION 201 — CHANGING A TITLE
-------------------------------

A book's original author may change its title while the work remains unpublished. An unpublished work cannot be copied. Only the original author may PUBLISH it. Publication permanently fixes the work for purposes such as copying; title changes are no longer allowed afterward.

If the original author dies before publishing the manuscript, nobody else can publish it. The unfinished/unpublished manuscript remains exactly what it was—a potentially unique and genuinely rare physical artifact.


QUESTION 202 — WRITING AFTER PUBLICATION
----------------------------------------

PUBLISH permanently freezes the physical book. Once published, its title, text, artwork, pages, page order, and composition cannot be modified. Nothing may be appended, no pages may be added, and no pages may be removed. It becomes an immutable finished work suitable for copying.


QUESTION 203 — WHAT ABOUT PUBLISHED COPIES?
-------------------------------------------

Every legitimate COPY BOOK of a published work automatically inherits published/immutable status. Once the copy is created, its text, artwork, title, pages, page order, inherited historical notes, and copy/translation notation cannot be altered; pages cannot be added or removed. The copier's optional note is added during the copying process, before the resulting copy is finalized.

This preserves the integrity of every work circulating through the formal copying system.


QUESTION 204 — PHYSICALLY DESTROYING PUBLISHED BOOKS
----------------------------------------------------

A published book—original or legitimate copy—is permanent and indestructible. Fire, water, weather, decay, burial, ordinary damage systems, and similar effects cannot damage or destroy it. It cannot be removed through an OOC JUNK-style command or equivalent disposal mechanism. Burial does not cause decomposition. Administrators below level 100 cannot destroy it either. Only the exceptional authority of a level-100 administrator remains capable of removing a published book.

A book is forever.


QUESTION 205 — PUBLISHING EMPTY OR TRIVIAL BOOKS
------------------------------------------------

There is no minimum-content requirement established for PUBLISH. Every publication generates an automatic entry on the staff news board, allowing staff to review publishing activity. If staff determine that a player is abusing PUBLISH, the player loses publishing privileges for 3 months. Each subsequent publishing-abuse offense doubles the previous ban duration: 3 months → 6 months → 12 months → 24 months, and so forth.

The books already published remain permanent; the punishment restricts the player's ability to create additional published works.

Continuation clarification: the illustrative doubling sequence above was superseded by Thomas's explicit schedule in Question 206: 3 months, 5 months, 1 year, 2 years, 4 years, 8 years, 16 years. Use that later schedule.


QUESTION 206 — WHAT HAPPENS TO THE ESCALATION OVER TIME?
--------------------------------------------------------

Publishing-abuse offenses never decay or reset. The escalating ban schedule is 3 months, 5 months, 1 year, 2 years, 4 years, 8 years, 16 years, continuing on that severe escalation principle if somehow necessary. Long periods of good behavior do not erase prior publishing-abuse offenses.


QUESTION 207 — PUBLISHING WHILE BANNED
--------------------------------------

A PUBLISH-abuse ban applies to the player/account, not merely the offending character. While banned, none of that player's characters may publish a book, including manuscripts created before the ban. Existing published books remain unaffected.


QUESTION 208 — PUBLISHING THROUGH ANOTHER PLAYER
------------------------------------------------

No special automated proxy-detection system is needed. If another player publishes abusive material, that publisher assumes the normal PUBLISH-abuse risk and enters the established escalating ban sequence if warranted. If staff discover that the publisher knowingly acted as a proxy specifically to circumvent another player's publishing ban, staff may address that deliberate rule evasion separately. Innocent collaboration isn't automatically treated as ban evasion.


QUESTION 209 — PUBLISHED PARCHMENTS
-----------------------------------

Individual parchments may also be PUBLISHED. Published parchments receive the same essential protections as published books: they become immutable and indestructible and may participate in the formal copying system. Publishing a parchment also generates staff-news oversight, and frivolous/abusive parchment publishing subjects the player to the same escalating account-level PUBLISH discipline established for books.


QUESTION 210 — AUTHORSHIP OF A STANDALONE PARCHMENT
---------------------------------------------------

A standalone parchment may be TITLED, just like a book, and follows the same authorship/provenance metadata model. The character who titles it establishes its original authorship under the same rules already established for books. Publication, copying, translation, provenance, and related metadata principles therefore apply consistently to both books and standalone parchments where applicable.


QUESTION 211 — UNTITLED PARCHMENTS
----------------------------------

A parchment does not need to be titled before it can be written on. Players may freely use untitled parchment for notes, letters, directions, drafts, and similar informal writing. TITLE remains the action that establishes formal authorship metadata when the player chooses to title the parchment.


QUESTION 212 — WHO MAY TITLE AN ALREADY-WRITTEN PARCHMENT?
----------------------------------------------------------

Any character holding an untitled parchment may TITLE it and thereby become its recognized original author. The game does not protect the first writer's claim to authorship. Metadata still records who actually wrote the existing material, so staff/history can establish what happened, but the system does not undo the consequences of handing an untitled work to someone else.


QUESTION 213 — UNTITLED BOOKS
-----------------------------

The same rule applies to untitled books. A book may contain any amount of writing while remaining untitled. Whoever possesses it and successfully uses TITLE first becomes its recognized original author, regardless of who actually wrote the existing pages. Metadata still preserves the actual writing history.


QUESTION 214 — REMOVING A TITLE BEFORE PUBLICATION
--------------------------------------------------

Once a book or parchment has been titled, it can never return to an untitled state. Before publication, the original author may change the title, but every replacement title must contain at least 3 letters. Once published, the title is permanently frozen along with the rest of the work.


QUESTION 215 — TITLE LENGTH
---------------------------

Book and parchment titles have a maximum length of 75 characters, while still requiring at least 3 letters. This permits deliberately long titles without allowing unbounded title strings.


QUESTION 216 — DUPLICATE TITLES
-------------------------------

Titles do not need to be unique. Multiple books or parchments may have exactly the same title. The game won't automatically append author names or otherwise disambiguate them. Players who care about distinction can include identifying information themselves—for example, *My Travels West, by Thomas Paige*.


QUESTION 217 — TITLE LANGUAGE
-----------------------------

A title has its own language, determined by the language the author was using when they issued TITLE. It is not a universally readable OOC label. The language system governs whether another character can understand that title.

That also means a book's title language doesn't necessarily have to match every page inside it.


QUESTION 218 — UNREADABLE TITLES IN INVENTORY
---------------------------------------------

Written foreign-language titles behave the same way as hearing a foreign language under the established language system. A character who lacks the necessary language comprehension does not get the literal title leaked through the interface; they receive the same sort of incomprehensible-language representation the game uses for speech they cannot understand.


QUESTION 219 — READING VERSUS SPEAKING FOR TITLES
-------------------------------------------------

Understanding a written title requires READ capability in that language. Merely being able to speak the language is insufficient.

However, as with the previously established spoken-language recognition rules, a character may be able to recognize the language itself without understanding it. Thus someone might determine, *“This appears to be written in French,”* while remaining unable to read what the French title actually says.


QUESTION 220 — TITLE RECOGNITION CHECK
--------------------------------------

Written-language identification uses the same established language-recognition rules as identifying an unfamiliar spoken language. We will not create a separate recognition subsystem just for writing. Reading capability determines comprehension; existing language familiarity/recognition determines whether an unreadable language can nevertheless be identified.

Continuation clarification: this later decision supersedes Question 176's earlier statement that there is no written-language recognition mechanic. Reuse the established language-recognition rules; do not introduce a separate writing subsystem.


QUESTION 221 — MIXED LANGUAGES WITHIN ONE BOOK
----------------------------------------------

A book or parchment may contain multiple languages. Different pages may use different languages, and separate passages appended to the same parchment may also be written in different languages. Language therefore cannot be treated merely as a single property of the entire book; the system must preserve enough information about each written section to apply the proper reading and language-recognition rules.


QUESTION 222 — SWITCHING LANGUAGES MID-WRITING
----------------------------------------------

The writing language is fixed for each editor session based on the character's currently selected language when the editor is opened. The writer cannot switch languages from inside the editor. To change languages on the same parchment/page, they must finish/close the current editor session, change their active language, then reopen that page and append the new passage. Each appended section retains its appropriate language metadata.


QUESTION 223 — PARTIAL EDITOR SESSIONS
--------------------------------------

The writing editor is a temporary drafting buffer. Nothing becomes physical writing, consumes ink, or changes the parchment until the player commits/saves the editor session. Cancelling or abandoning the editor discards the entire uncommitted session with no effect on the document or supplies.

Once committed, however, the resulting ink is permanent under the rules we've established.


QUESTION 224 — RUNNING OUT OF INK
---------------------------------

The ink check happens before the writing editor opens. A character must possess enough ink to write a full parchment page—up to the established 25 lines × 75 characters—even if they intend to write only a short passage. If they lack that reserve, WRITE is refused with an appropriate message such as “You need more ink.”


QUESTION 225 — HOW MUCH INK IS ACTUALLY CONSUMED?
-------------------------------------------------

Ink is consumed in discrete samples/uses, not character-by-character or line-by-line. When a writing session is successfully committed, one sampling of ink is consumed regardless of how much text was added during that session.

So the resource cost encourages filling a writing session efficiently, but we don't need to simulate ink down to individual letters.


QUESTION 226 — APPENDING AND INK
--------------------------------

Every successfully committed writing session consumes one ink sampling. Reopening an existing parchment or book page later to append additional writing consumes another sampling when that new writing is committed, regardless of how little text is added.


QUESTION 227 — INK COLORS
-------------------------

Ink may have different colors, and the color used for each committed writing section is preserved. Because writing can be appended later, a single parchment may contain passages written in different ink colors.


QUESTION 228 — CUSTOM INK COLORS
--------------------------------

Ink colors come from a fixed, game-defined palette. Players cannot create arbitrary color descriptions. This keeps crafting, display, copying, and metadata consistent.

We can decide the actual palette later when we're designing crafting/materials.


QUESTION 229 — COPYING INK COLORS
---------------------------------

COPY reproduces the written content, not the source's ink colors. The copier chooses/uses their own available ink, and the resulting copy records that ink color. Source ink color is therefore not part of the requirement for textual fidelity.

This slightly refines our earlier use of “exact copy”: exact means the words/content—including inherited copy notes and <art> content—not the physical ink characteristics.


QUESTION 230 — MULTIPLE INK COLORS DURING ONE COPY
--------------------------------------------------

To copy an entire book in one operation, the selected ink bottle must contain at least as many ink samplings as there are pages being copied. The completed book uses that single ink color throughout, regardless of the source's colors, consuming the required samplings.

A scribe who wants to preserve different colors must copy pages individually using the desired inks. COPY BOOK 23, for example, copies page 23 of the book onto the parchment the character is holding, using the pen and ink on the desk. The resulting parchment can then participate in the page-assembly system we've already established.


QUESTION 231 — BLANK PAGES DURING WHOLE-BOOK COPYING
----------------------------------------------------

Whole-book copying requires and consumes one ink sampling for every physical page in the source book, including completely blank pages. A 50-page book therefore costs 50 ink samplings, period.


QUESTION 232 — BLANK PARCHMENT REQUIREMENT FOR WHOLE-BOOK COPYING
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Whole-book COPY BOOK requires the copier to have a book containing exactly the same number of blank pages as the source book. A 50-page original requires a 50-page blank destination book. Loose parchments are not automatically assembled or bound by the copying command.

Combined with our ink rule, copying that 50-page book requires the 50-page blank book plus at least 50 samplings of a single ink, along with the required pen and copying capability.


QUESTION 233 — DESTINATION BOOK MUST BE COMPLETELY VIRGIN?
----------------------------------------------------------

A destination book used for whole-book copying must be untitled and never previously written in, in addition to containing exactly the required number of blank pages. A previously titled blank book is not eligible, because TITLE has already established its authorship/history.


QUESTION 234 — DESTINATION BOOK AFTER COPYING
---------------------------------------------

A successful whole-book COPY BOOK automatically reproduces the source book's title on the destination book. The copier does not become the author merely by creating the copy. The copied work retains its established authorship and provenance, while the copy metadata records the copying event and the mandatory visible copy notation applies.


QUESTION 235 — COPYING A SINGLE PAGE
------------------------------------

COPY BOOK 23 produces a parchment that automatically carries the source book's title and source page number. The player does not need to title it manually, and the copied page remains associated with where it came from rather than becoming an anonymous loose parchment.


QUESTION 236 — REASSEMBLING INDIVIDUALLY COPIED PAGES
-----------------------------------------------------

Only the one-step whole-book COPY BOOK operation can create an official, published/immutable copy of an existing published book. Individually copying pages and later assembling them with ADD PAGE does not recreate an official copy, even if every page is present, correctly ordered, and textually identical.

So preserving the original's varied ink colors comes with a tradeoff: the reconstructed volume is a player-assembled artifact, not a formally certified copy.


QUESTION 237 — PUBLISHING THE RECONSTRUCTED BOOK AS A NEW WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------

Yes. A player who individually copies pages and assembles them into a new book may TITLE and PUBLISH that assembled volume as a new work under the normal rules. The game does not enforce intellectual-property ownership or prevent someone from claiming authorship this way. The underlying metadata still preserves the actual history of the pages, so the provenance can reveal what happened.


QUESTION 238 — COPYING AN INDIVIDUALLY COPIED PAGE
--------------------------------------------------

A loose page produced by COPY BOOK 23 may itself be copied using COPY PARCHMENT. Each subsequent copy follows the established rules: content is reproduced faithfully, mandatory copy notation accumulates appropriately, optional copier notes are preserved and may be added, and the complete provenance chain remains in metadata.


QUESTION 239 — COPYING UNPUBLISHED LOOSE PAGES
----------------------------------------------

COPY PARCHMENT requires the specific parchment being copied to have published status. Having content derived from some other published source does not bypass that requirement. Unpublished parchments cannot be formally copied.


QUESTION 240 — PUBLISHING INDIVIDUAL PAGES FROM AN UNPUBLISHED BOOK
-------------------------------------------------------------------

A parchment removed intact from an unpublished book may become an independent work and be TITLED and PUBLISHED under the normal parchment rules. Its former membership in an unpublished book does not prevent publication. Once published, that parchment gains the same permanence, immutability, copyability, and anti-abuse oversight as any other published parchment.


QUESTION 241 — ADDING A PUBLISHED PARCHMENT TO AN UNPUBLISHED BOOK
------------------------------------------------------------------

A published parchment may be inserted into an unpublished book with ADD PAGE. The parchment retains its own published/immutable status; putting it into an unpublished volume does not make it editable. The surrounding book remains unpublished and may otherwise continue to be assembled or written under the normal rules.


QUESTION 242 — REMOVING THAT PUBLISHED PAGE AGAIN
-------------------------------------------------

A published parchment inserted into an unpublished book may later be removed intact. Adding or removing it changes the unpublished book's composition, not the published parchment itself. The parchment retains its title, content, provenance, publication status, and indestructibility throughout.


QUESTION 243 — PUBLISHING A BOOK CONTAINING ALREADY-PUBLISHED PARCHMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

An unpublished book may contain already-published parchments and may itself subsequently be PUBLISHED. Publication of the containing book is allowed because each parchment retains its own provenance metadata—where it came from, its history, and, where known, who actually wrote it. The resulting book becomes a permanent published work without erasing the independent history of its component parchments.

This also reinforces that provenance belongs deeply enough in the document/page structure to survive assembly into other works.


QUESTION 244 — REMOVING INDEPENDENTLY PUBLISHED PAGES AFTER THE BOOK IS PUBLISHED
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Once the containing book is PUBLISHED, its composition is permanently frozen. Even an independently published parchment that was previously removable becomes permanently bound into that published book and cannot thereafter be removed. Its own provenance remains intact, but its physical membership in the book is now permanent.


QUESTION 245 — COPIES OF BOOKS CONTAINING INDEPENDENTLY PUBLISHED PAGES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Whole-book copying preserves the full nested provenance of component parchments. If a page originated as an independently published work, that history remains traceable through subsequent books and generations of copies. Copying a containing book never flattens or erases the histories of the parchments within it.

THE BEST PLACE TO BEGIN
-----------------------

When we begin, I recommend we start with Question 1 and proceed as an interview, but not blindly through all 208. The first twelve questions define the playable target. Once those are settled, we can identify the shortest development path and postpone whole categories that do not belong in the initial release.

My provisional recommendation is that the first real milestone prove this loop:

Create a character → enter the starting region → travel continuously → perceive generated terrain → find and gather a resource → leave persistent evidence → craft or build one useful object → log out → return and find the world correctly preserved.

If that works, CCMud’s distinctive foundation works. Combat, economies, governments, advanced construction, and large-scale civilization can then grow upon something real rather than delaying the first playable game.
